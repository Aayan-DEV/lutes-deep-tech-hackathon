# Make the tracker prove it moved

**Bottom line:** build a **self-verifying tracker**. It does not trust its own commands. After every move and every stow, it checks two independent witnesses: the light the panel actually sees (panel current plus the 4 panel-mounted LDRs) and the effort the servo actually spent (servo rail current from a second INA219). It then labels the move OK, JAMMED, SLIPPED, NO DRIVE, SENSOR FAULT or UNVERIFIABLE, and it shows two numbers side by side: "claimed availability" (what a control system that only checks its own commands would report) and "verified availability" (what really happened). The notes back this choice more strongly than any other option. On three US sites, **more than 550 rows did not reach the 52° hail stow angle while the plant software showed them as stowed**, and only a drone flight found it ([Raptor Maps, 2026](https://raptormaps.com/blog-posts/when-the-data-lies-how-autonomous-drone-inspections-caught-a-tracker-stow-failure-that-scada-missed)). Across about 2 GWp and more than 50 utility sites, **median tracker availability was 66% to 88%, against supplier claims of up to 99%**, and wrong or missing angle data was a main problem ([3E, Jan 2026](https://www.3e.eu/resources/publications/rethinking-tracker-availability-utility-scale-solar-plants)). Hail causes about **54% of US solar insurance losses from 1.4% of claims**, and a working hail stow is the single most insurable action an owner can take ([pv magazine USA, Sep 2025](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2025/09/18/hail-damage-to-solar-projects-accounts-for-about-1-of-filed-claims-but-over-50-of-total-losses/)). The idea also fits this kit unusually well. An SG-90 has no position output, so it is a perfect stand-in for a row whose reported angle nobody checks. The verification uses large light changes, so it works under a lamp, where the earlier research showed that small tracking gains cannot be measured. None of the single pieces is new. Current-based stall detection is patented, Sandia published a residual fault classifier, and drones verify stow after the fact. The honest claim is narrower and still strong: **a tracker that issues a receipt for every move at the moment of the command, by fusing two physically independent witnesses, and reports its own true availability.** In the sources searched, no public product or paper describes that, although vendors do not publish their control logic, so it cannot be ruled out.

## 1. The recommendation in one page

### 1.1 What the team builds

- **Working name:** "Proof-of-Motion tracker". The team can rename it.
- **Core loop:** command a move, record the servo current during the move, compare the light before and after with what a simple cosine model predicts, then issue a verdict.
- **Two independent witnesses:**
  1. **Optical witness.** Panel short-circuit current (Isc, the current when the panel output is nearly shorted, which rises in direct proportion to light) from INA219 #1, plus the four LDRs on the panel-mounted block. Because the LDRs ride on the panel, a real move must change what they see.
  2. **Effort witness.** Servo rail current from INA219 #2. SG-90s draw about 10 mA idle, 100 to 250 mA while moving, and about 360 mA (measured) to 650 mA (datasheet) when stalled ([ProtoSupplies](https://protosupplies.com/product/servo-motor-micro-sg90/); [Reefwing / MicroPik](http://reefwingrobotics.blogspot.com/2017/02/raspberry-pi-and-towerpro-sg90-micro.html)).
- **Optional third witness (recommended if a third INA219 is free):** a spare 0.7 W cell fixed to the base as a "sky reference". It tells the tracker whether the light changed or the panel changed. Plants use reference cells in the same way for soiling and irradiance ([Campbell DustVue 10](https://s.campbellsci.com/documents/es/product-brochures/b_dustvue10.pdf); [US 9,800,202](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9800202)).
- **Outputs:** a verdict per move on the LED matrix, an RGB traffic light, buzzer patterns, a red "STOW" button, and a laptop dashboard with a receipt log and the two availability numbers.

### 1.2 The verdict table

| Effort witness (servo current) | Optical witness (light change vs prediction) | Verdict | Real-world failure it mirrors |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal move profile | Matches prediction | **OK (verified)** | Healthy row |
| High current that does not fall back after the expected move time | No change | **JAMMED** | Seized motor, mechanical binding, snow drift blocking rotation ([Intertek CEA via PV Tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/misaligned-from-the-start-how-quality-assurance-gaps-in-tracker-and-racking-systems-put-solar-projects-at-risk/); [Solar Power World](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2024/04/reducing-extreme-weather-damage-risk-for-solar-trackers/)) |
| Normal or slightly low current | No change | **SLIPPED / DECOUPLED** | Broken coupling or slew drive, the most cited mechanical failures ([IEA PVPS T13](https://iea-pvps.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/IEA-PVPS-T13-26-2024-SLIDES-Bifacial-Tracking-Systems.pdf)) |
| Almost zero current | No change | **NO DRIVE** | Dead network controller, lost power or comms; one cause in the 550-row case ([Raptor Maps](https://raptormaps.com/blog-posts/when-the-data-lies-how-autonomous-drone-inspections-caught-a-tracker-stow-failure-that-scada-missed)) |
| Normal | Panel and most LDRs agree, one LDR does not | **SENSOR FAULT (LDR n)**, keep working on the rest | Wrong or missing angle telemetry ([3E](https://www.3e.eu/resources/publications/rethinking-tracker-availability-utility-scale-solar-plants); [EPRI](https://pvpmc.sandia.gov/app/uploads/sites/243/dlm_uploads/2026/05/7_DanielFregosi.pdf)) |
| Normal | LDRs agree, panel current does not move or reads zero | **PANEL / WIRING FAULT** | String or combiner fault |
| Any | Expected change is smaller than the sensor can see, or the light is too dim | **UNVERIFIABLE** (never counted as OK) | Missing data, the main uncertainty in fleet availability studies ([EPRI](https://pvpmc.sandia.gov/app/uploads/sites/243/dlm_uploads/2026/05/7_DanielFregosi.pdf)) |

### 1.3 The two availability numbers

- **Claimed availability** = share of time the tracker believes it is at its commanded angle. For an open-loop servo this is always 100%, because every command was "sent". This is the same blind spot Raptor Maps describes, where control systems confirm a command was sent and acknowledged, not that the row moved ([Raptor Maps](https://raptormaps.com/blog-posts/when-the-data-lies-how-autonomous-drone-inspections-caught-a-tracker-stow-failure-that-scada-missed)).
- **Verified availability** = share of time since the last verified-OK move. A failed move makes the tracker "unavailable" until the next verified move.
- **Show it as a range.** Pessimistic: UNVERIFIABLE time counts as down. Optimistic: UNVERIFIABLE time is left out. This mirrors the 3E result, whose 66% to 88% spread came from how the data was filtered ([3E](https://www.3e.eu/resources/publications/rethinking-tracker-availability-utility-scale-solar-plants)).

### 1.4 Why this beats the other options in the notes

1. **Biggest, best-quantified problem.** Tracker issues were just under 14% of all US equipment-driven loss in 2025 and grew 25% in a year, while inverter faults fell almost 40% ([pv magazine USA, Feb 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/02/25/u-s-solar-assets-lose-average-5070-per-mw-from-power-losses/)). Hail stow is worth about "$50 million down to below $5 million" in losses for a typical utility project, per VDE's estimate ([pv magazine USA, Sep 2025](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2025/09/18/hail-damage-to-solar-projects-accounts-for-about-1-of-filed-claims-but-over-50-of-total-losses/)).
2. **Survives an indoor lamp.** The earlier research showed that the INA219's 1 mA step can be larger than the signal under room light, so tracking gains of a few percent are not measurable ([TI INA219 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ina219.pdf); [DFRobot SEN0291 wiki](https://wiki.dfrobot.com/Gravity:%20I2C%20Digital%20Wattmeter%20SKU:%20SEN0291)). Verification needs big, binary signals: a 60° stow halves direct light (cos 60° = 0.5), and stall current (360 to 650 mA) sits well above normal moving current (100 to 250 mA).
3. **Turns a kit weakness into the premise.** The SG-90 has no position output and no encoder, so current is its only "sense of touch" (proprioception, a machine's sense of its own body position) (hardware notes Q8; software notes section 3).
4. **The demo is physical and binary.** Judges press a finger on the panel, pull a pin or unplug a wire, and see the tracker catch it. They do not need to imagine crops, clouds or a year of energy.
5. **Strong feasibility story.** Row controllers already read motor current (patents show microcontrollers with motor current, motor voltage and battery temperature inputs) ([US 11,411,529](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11411529)), and many trackers can add hail stow by software alone ([pv magazine USA, Sep 2025](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2025/09/18/hail-damage-to-solar-projects-accounts-for-about-1-of-filed-claims-but-over-50-of-total-losses/)).

### 1.5 The honest novelty sentence for the pitch

> "SCADA (the plant's central monitoring and control software) verifies the message. We verify the motion. Drones check stow after the fact, Sandia classifies faults from power residuals, and patents read motor current. We did not find a tracker that checks every move with two independent physical witnesses at the moment of the command and reports its own true availability. Vendors do not publish their control logic, so someone may do it privately."

## 2. Five-minute demo script

The hackathon gives each team **5 minutes for pitch and demo plus 3 minutes of judge Q&A** (from the official TOPICS + CRITERIA sheet). Every number marked "measured" must come from the team's own bench trials. Do not invent them.

### 2.1 Set-up before the judges arrive

- Lamp at **30 cm or more** from the panel, so distance changes do not swamp angle changes. The earlier research calculated from the inverse-square law that moving from 20 to 21 cm from a lamp loses 9.3% of light, while a 20° pointing error loses only about 6%.
- Place the lamp **to one side at roughly 30° to 45° elevation, not straight overhead.** If the lamp is directly above, a pan rotation barely changes the light, and pan moves become unverifiable.
- Bright lamp, so panel Isc is above about 20 mA. At 6.8 mA (about 50 W/m²) the INA219's 1 mA step is already about 15% of the signal.
- Dashboard on the laptop. LED matrix facing the judges. Red STOW button on the base.
- A printed **fault-injection pin** in the pan coupling (see section 3.2).

### 2.2 The script

| Time | What the presenter does | What the judges see | What it stands for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:30 | Hook. "On three US sites, more than 550 rows reported 'stowed' for hail. They were not. A drone found it, not the software." | One slide: 550 rows, 66% to 88% vs 99% availability | Raptor Maps; 3E |
| 0:30 to 1:00 | Move the lamp. Tracker follows. | A tick icon after every move. Dashboard: claimed 100%, verified 100% | Healthy row |
| 1:00 to 1:40 | Invite a judge to hold the panel with a finger, then move the lamp. | Current spike, "JAM" icon, three buzzer beeps, red LED. Firmware cuts the servo after a short time limit to protect it. Verified availability drops; claimed stays at 100%. | Seized motor; the Cutlass/Old 300 block that missed hail stow because of a pre-existing motor issue ([VDE Americas, Nov 2025](https://www.vde.com/en/vde-americas/newsroom/vde-case-study-in-resilient-power-report)) |
| 1:40 to 2:20 | Pull the fault-injection pin on the pan axis. Move the lamp. | Servo moves with normal current, light does not change, "SLIP" icon. A naive controller would report success. | Broken coupling or slew drive |
| 2:20 to 2:50 | Put tape over one LDR. Move the lamp. | "SENSOR 3" icon, amber LED, tracker keeps working on the other three LDRs plus panel current | Bad angle telemetry |
| 2:50 to 3:30 | Reinsert the pin. Press the red STOW button. Then unplug the servo power lead and press STOW again. | First: "STOW OK" receipt with the measured light drop next to the predicted drop. Second: "NO DRIVE, STOW FAILED", alarm. | The 550-row case: one command acknowledged, rows not protected. Caught in about a second (measured) instead of by a drone. |
| 3:30 to 4:15 | Results slide. | A confusion matrix (a table of true fault vs detected fault) from the team's own trials, for example "20 trials per fault, X correct" (measured), plus detection time per move (measured) | Technical evidence |
| 4:15 to 5:00 | Feasibility and prior art slide. | "Real rows already have motor current and string current. This is a firmware feature. Prior art: Raptor drones, Sandia residuals, current patents. Our step: a receipt for every move, now." | Feasibility and honesty |

### 2.3 Expected judge questions and short answers

1. **"Real trackers have inclinometers. Why do they need this?"** The angle sensor is a single source. In the Raptor case a wrong PLC configuration value made SCADA report success, and some network controllers were dead after a substation power-down ([Raptor Maps](https://raptormaps.com/blog-posts/when-the-data-lies-how-autonomous-drone-inspections-caught-a-tracker-stow-failure-that-scada-missed)). Practitioner guidance already says to test each row's angle sensor against a reference inclinometer ([OxMaint](https://oxmaint.com/industries/power-plant/solar-pv-tracker-maintenance-programs-single-axis-dual-axis)). Light and motor current do not share those failure modes.
2. **"Drones already do this."** They do it after the fact and only when someone flies. VDE recommends watching tracker positions in real time during hail and starting manual stow if automation does not respond within one minute ([pv magazine USA, Jan 2025](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2025/01/22/actionable-insights-to-safeguard-solar-projects-from-hail-damage/)). A drone cannot meet that one-minute window.
3. **"Sandia already classifies tracker faults."** Yes, and we cite it. Sandia predicts fault-dependent signals and classifies the residuals (the difference between predicted and measured), with nearly zero false alarms on emulated stall and timing faults ([Sandia 2024](https://www.sandia.gov/research/publications/details/machine-learning-for-single-axis-tracker-fault-detection-and-classification-2024-01-01/)). That works on plant time series. Ours runs per move, on the tracker, and adds actuator effort as a second witness.
4. **"Current monitoring is patented."** Yes. US 12,003,208 compares required motor power with expected values to find stalls, obstructions and gearbox failures ([USPTO](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12003208)). We do not claim current monitoring. We claim the fusion with an optical witness and the truthful availability report.
5. **"What happens if someone walks past and shades it?"** The check only runs in a short window around each move. With the optional fixed reference cell, the tracker can tell "the room got darker" from "the panel moved". Without it, sudden shading during a move can cause a false verdict. Say so.

## 3. Build plan in hours

Hours are judgement estimates from the notes and this synthesis, not measured. They assume 3 to 4 people working in parallel.

### 3.1 Timeline

| Hours | Task | Who | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 1 | **Bench measurements.** INA219 zero offsets (average 128 samples). Servo current traces for 10°, 30° and 60° moves on each axis. Stall current for 200 ms (finger hold). Panel Isc at the demo lamp position. A cosine check: Isc every 15° away from the lamp. | Electronics | Thresholds and a measured cosine table |
| 0 to 1.5 | **Print** the fault-injection coupling for the pan axis and, if needed, a clip for the fixed reference cell. | Mechanical | Parts |
| 1 to 2 | **Wiring.** INA219 #1 on the panel (address 0x40), near-short load so it reads Isc. INA219 #2 in the servo 5 V line (0x41). Optional INA219 #3 on the fixed reference cell (0x44). | 2 to 4 | **Move routine firmware.** Average optics before the move. Move one axis. | 4 to 5 | **Classifier and safety.** Rules from the verdict table, thresholds from the bench data. Stall guard: detach the servo if current stays at stall level past a time limit. Retry once on JAMMED, then alarm. | Firmware | Verdicts |
| 5 to 6 | **Availability counters and stow.** Claimed vs verified (pessimistic and optimistic). STOW command with a stow receipt. | 5 to 7 | **UI.** LED matrix icons (tick, JAM, SLIP, NO DRIVE, SENSOR n, STOW OK, STOW FAIL). RGB traffic light. Active buzzer patterns. Red STOW button. Laptop dashboard over USB serial (a small Python script is fine) with a receipt log and the two availability numbers. | UI person | Demo-ready interface |
| 7 to 8 | **Validation trials.** About 20 trials per fault class plus 20 healthy moves. Build the confusion matrix. Measure detection time. | Whole team | The results slide |
| 8 to 9 | **Pitch and backup video.** Rehearse the script twice. Record the 2 to 3 minute backup video. | Whole team | Submission |

Total: about 9 hours of team time, with the mechanical and UI work overlapping the firmware.

### 3.2 Hardware details that matter

- **Fault-injection coupling.** A printed PETG adapter between the pan servo horn and the pan bracket, held by a removable pin. Pin in: normal drive. Pin out: the servo spins freely and the panel stays put, which is a clean, repeatable "slipped horn". This avoids stripping a real horn on stage. Put it on the pan axis so the panel does not fall when decoupled.
- **Balance the tilt axis if time allows.** A panel hanging off-axis makes the servo fight gravity. Moving the centre of mass onto the axis cut actuator energy by 39.5% in one tracker redesign ([Hue Univ.](https://jos.hueuni.edu.vn/index.php/hujos-ns/article/view/5072)), and a balanced panel gives cleaner current signatures for the classifier. The hardware notes rate this at about 2 hours.
- **Move one axis at a time.** Two SG-90s moving or stalling together can exceed a USB 2.0 port's 500 mA and reset the board ([Electro-Tech-Online](https://electro-tech-online.com/threads/usb-current-limits.128385)).
- **Pins.** The build sheet uses A0 to A3 for the LDRs, D9 and D10 for the servos, and A4/A5 for I2C. Buttons, buzzer and the RGB LED go on free digital pins (RGB needs three PWM-capable pins).

### 3.3 If the kit has fewer INA219s

| INA219s available | Where to put them | What you lose |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Panel, servo rail, fixed reference cell | Nothing |
| 2 | Panel, servo rail | Cannot separate "light changed" from "panel moved" between moves |
| 1 | **Servo rail.** The LDRs become the only optical witness. | No panel-current witness; LDR faults are harder to separate from real changes |

The servo rail gets priority because nothing else in the kit can sense effort, while the four LDRs already give an optical signal.

### 3.4 Risks and fixes

| Risk | Fix |
|---|---|
| Small moves near alignment barely change panel current (cosine curve is flat there) | Use the LDR difference near alignment and panel current far from it (section 4.2). If neither can see the move, label it UNVERIFIABLE. |
| Room light changes during a move | Keep the check window short (one move is roughly 0.1 to 1 s). Use the fixed reference cell if available. |
| SLIP current looks the same as a normal move | That is expected. SLIP is decided by the optical witness, not current. |
| Stall damages the servo or browns out USB | Hard time limit on stall current, then detach. |
| INA219 offset of up to ±10 mA worst case at PGA /1 | Measure and subtract the zero offset at start-up ([TI](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ina219.pdf)). |
| Classifier works on the bench but fails on stage | Small microcontroller fault classifiers can generalise poorly; one IMU-based Arduino network fell from 97.1% training accuracy to 73.19% on unseen data ([EPJ Conf 2025](https://www.epj-conferences.org/articles/epjconf/pdf/2025/28/epjconf_aimace2025_05014.pdf)). Use simple rules, not a trained model, and run the validation trials at the venue under the demo lamp. |

## 4. How the verification works

### 4.1 The prediction

- Direct light on a panel scales with **cos θ**, where θ is the angle between the panel's facing direction and the light ([PVEducation](https://www.pveducation.org/pvcdrom/properties-of-sunlight/arbitrary-orientation-and-tilt)). Isc follows light in direct proportion ([PVEducation, short-circuit current](https://www.pveducation.org/pvcdrom/solar-cell-operation/short-circuit-current)).
- **Model:** predicted Isc_after = Isc_before × cos(θ_after) / cos(θ_before), plus a small diffuse floor learned during a start-up scan. The software notes suggest the same fit, P(θ) = a·cos(θ − θ0) + b, as a "digital twin lite" that DLR's heliostat calibration work uses at full scale ([Pargmann et al. 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11324880/)).
- θ comes from the LDR balance. When the LDRs balance, θ is close to zero.

### 4.2 Why two optical signals are better than one [CALC]

Panel current and the LDR difference are sensitive in opposite places. That is the strongest technical point of the design.

| Move | Change in panel Isc (cosine) | Change in a ±45° tilted LDR pair (normalised difference = tan α × tan θ) |
|---|---|---|
| 0° to 10° | 1.5% (hard to see) | 0 to 0.176 (easy to see) |
| 30° to 40° | 11.5% drop (easy to see) | 0.58 to 0.84, but one sensor nears the edge of its view |
| 0° to 60° (stow) | 50% drop (very easy) | Saturated, one sensor in shade |

- The tan α × tan θ formula is from the hardware notes for a tilted sensor pair. A cross-vane block, which the team may have instead, behaves similarly near alignment because the vane shades one side quickly.
- **Rule:** near alignment, verify with the LDR difference. Far from alignment and for stows, verify with panel current. When both agree, the verdict is strongest.

### 4.3 What the optical witness cannot do

- Panel-mounted sensors measure the angle **relative to the light**. Between commands, they cannot tell "the panel sagged" from "the lamp moved".
- The check works because the change lines up in time with a command. A change that happens during the half-second of a commanded move is almost certainly the move.
- The fixed reference cell removes this limit. If the reference cell stays steady while the panel's light changes without a command, the panel drifted. Real drives can back-drive when de-energised, which is why passive locks are patented ([US 12,537,476](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12537476)). This "DRIFT" verdict is a stretch goal.

### 4.4 Stow verification

- Before stowing, record Isc and LDRs. After the stow, compare the measured drop with the cosine prediction for the stow angle.
- **STOW OK** if both witnesses agree. **STOW FAILED** with the reason (JAMMED, SLIPPED, NO DRIVE) if not.
- On STOW FAILED, alarm loudly and retry once. Real best practice is to stow the whole site, hold stow at least one hour after the all-clear, and test the chain monthly ([pv magazine USA, Jan 2025](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2025/01/22/actionable-insights-to-safeguard-solar-projects-from-hail-damage/)). A "self-test stow" button that runs the whole chain and prints a receipt maps directly onto that monthly test.

### 4.5 Testing the coordinator's lead against the notes

The lead holds up. These are the corrections and additions.

1. **Supported.** Every headline number in the lead is in the notes. Two caveats: the 3E paper itself returned an error, so the 66% to 88% figure comes from 3E's own summary, and the 550-row case is a vendor case study from a drone company that sells this kind of inspection.
2. **Correction: utility trackers do have angle sensors.** The pitch should not say "real trackers have no feedback". It should say "their reported angle is a single, unchecked source". The SG-90 stands in for that blind spot.
3. **Correction: SLIPPED is decided by light, not current.** A slipped horn may draw normal or slightly lower current. Only the optical witness separates it from OK.
4. **Addition: NO DRIVE.** Near-zero current plus no light change. This mirrors the dead network controllers in the Raptor case and is the easiest fault to demo (unplug the servo power lead).
5. **Addition: UNVERIFIABLE.** Small moves near alignment and dim light cannot be verified. Counting them as OK would repeat the exact error the project criticises.
6. **Addition: the fixed reference cell.** It fixes the "light moved or panel moved" ambiguity and uses a spare cell already in the kit.
7. **Not found: a better top option.** The applications notes rank dynamic agrivoltaics first as a pitch theme, and the software notes rank "scan and point at the most light" first by points per hour. Both are good runner-ups (section 9). Neither has a quantified, dollar-sized failure story like hail stow, and both depend on energy differences the kit cannot measure well under a lamp.

| Most frequent hardware faults | Slew drives and motors (IEA survey); corrosion is the most frequent and costly per a QA firm; a tracker motor has about 60 small parts and can seize | [IEA PVPS T13](https://iea-pvps.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/IEA-PVPS-T13-26-2024-SLIDES-Bifacial-Tracking-Systems.pdf); [PV Tech, Feb 2026](https://www.pv-tech.org/misaligned-from-the-start-how-quality-assurance-gaps-in-tracker-and-racking-systems-put-solar-projects-at-risk/) |

- **Do not compare these availability numbers one-to-one.** The 99%, 90% to 95%, 85% and 66% to 88% figures use different datasets and definitions. The trend across newer, larger datasets is lower availability.
- **No public source gives a clean split of failures by component** (motor vs controller vs battery vs radio vs angle sensor).

### 5.2 Stow failures

- **Hail is the costliest weather risk.** GCube: 1.4% of US solar claims, 54% of losses ([pv magazine USA, Sep 2025](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2025/09/18/hail-damage-to-solar-projects-accounts-for-about-1-of-filed-claims-but-over-50-of-total-losses/)). kWh Analytics: 6% of events, 73% of weather-related financial losses ([pv magazine, Jun 2025](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/06/11/hail-risk-remains-top-issue-faced-by-solar-projects/)).
- **Stowing is cheap and pays.** A modelled 200 MW Texas site lost about $12,000 a year of production (about 0.1% of revenue) to hail stow and saved about $2 million a year in insurance premium (search summary, not opened) ([Renewable Energy World](https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/solar/weather-might-damage-solar-panels-more-than-our-models-predict/)).
- **Stows fail in execution.** More than 550 rows missed hail stow while SCADA showed success ([Raptor Maps](https://raptormaps.com/blog-posts/when-the-data-lies-how-autonomous-drone-inspections-caught-a-tracker-stow-failure-that-scada-missed)). At Cutlass I and II and Old 300, damage was confined to a part of one plant that "did not reach hail stow because of a pre-existing tracker motor issue" ([VDE Americas](https://www.vde.com/en/vde-americas/newsroom/vde-case-study-in-resilient-power-report)). Some trackers need 15 minutes or more to reach hail stow, while operators may get 30 minutes or less of warning ([Solar Builder, Feb 2024](https://solarbuildermag.com/projects/12-takeaways-from-vde-tracker-hail-stow-strategies-report/)).
- **Wind.** 44.2% of severe weather events hitting US PV involved high winds, and more than 50% of surveyed users reported damage from inadequate tracker response to extreme weather ([IEA PVPS, May 2025](https://iea-pvps.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/06_L.Micheli_Windstorm_Intersolar.pdf)). A Queensland 2P plant collapsed at gusts of about 74 km/h while the neighbouring plant stowed and survived (search summary) ([RELX SDG report](https://prod.sdgresources.relx.com/reports/mitigating-extreme-weather-risk-solar-power-plants)).
- **IEA PVPS Task 13 warns** there is "a significant risk that a tracker will not respond appropriately" to rare extreme weather ([IEA PVPS T13](https://iea-pvps.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/IEA-PVPS-T13-26-2024-SLIDES-Bifacial-Tracking-Systems.pdf)).

### 5.3 Misalignment

- A 5 MWp Spanish plant (187 trackers) had misalignments of several degrees in most trackers, up to 4.2°. Fixing orientation alone gained only 0.034%. Height-aware backtracking gained 0.95% a year ([López, Solar Energy 2024](https://dspace.umh.es/bitstream/11000/34518/1/1-s2.0-S0038092X24001841-main.pdf)).
- **So a few degrees of error is not the story.** A 3° cosine loss is about 0.14%. The costly failures are stuck rows, failed stows and broken backtracking. On real terrain, overridden backtracking costs 1% to 3% typically and can exceed 5% ([pv magazine Australia, Apr 2026](https://www.pv-magazine-australia.com/2026/04/20/scientists-identify-new-loss-factor-in-tracker-based-pv-plants-on-slightly-undulating-terrain/)).
- **For the pitch:** "my tracker is 3° off" is weak. "My tracker knows when it is not where it thinks it is" is strong.

### 5.4 The monitoring gap

- Tracker failures "tend to be quiet" and only partly reduce output, so they go unnoticed ([Raptor Maps](https://raptormaps.com/blog-posts/when-the-data-lies-how-autonomous-drone-inspections-caught-a-tracker-stow-failure-that-scada-missed); [Delfos](https://www.delfos.energy/blog-posts/maximizing-solar-plant-efficiency-navigating-tracker-failures-with-advanced-technology)).
- Losses are shifting from central inverters to distributed equipment like trackers, which "speaks to the importance of more frequent data" ([pv magazine USA, Feb 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/02/25/u-s-solar-assets-lose-average-5070-per-mw-from-power-losses/)).
- A truck roll (sending a technician to site) costs about EUR 700 in an IEA example model of a 100 MWp plant with about 60 visits a year ([IEA PVPS, Tsanakas 2025](https://iea-pvps.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/03_Ioannis-Tsanakas_Advances-needed-in-PV-park-inspection.pdf)). A verdict like "JAMMED, check for obstruction" before the visit is the practical value.

## 7. The software and hardware frontier

### 7.1 Software: what vendors ship and what it really gains

- **Every major vendor ships the same four ideas:** terrain-aware ("learned") backtracking, a diffuse-light flat mode, weather stow, and remote monitoring (Nextracker TrueCapture and NX Navigator, Array SmarTrack, GameChange Genius, Arctech, Soltec TeamTrack, FTC SUNOPS) ([pv-tech](https://pv-tech.org/nextrackers-advanced-tracker-control-system-boost-yield-with-self-learning); [Array, PVPMC 2022](https://pvpmc.sandia.gov/download/4718); [GameChange](https://www.gamechangeenergy.com/news/gamechange-solar-validates-performance-enhancement-software-features-of-its-genius-tracker-solar-tr); [Soltec](https://soltec.com/en/press-room/https-lab-soltec-com-6-2-teamtrack-gain/); [pv magazine USA, SUNOPS](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2023/08/31/ftc-solar-unveils-sunops-software-to-enhance-solar-plant-performance/)).
- **Measured gains are small.** Diffuse modes give about 0.3% to 0.7%. Terrain-aware backtracking gives about 1.5% to 3.6%. Headlines of 6% to 8% compare against naive tracking. Examples: Nextracker sites +0.63%, +1.7%, +2.2% against standard backtracking ([pv magazine Australia, 2022](http://www.pv-magazine-australia.com/2022/09/29/study-shows-nextrackers-machine-learning-software-improves-solar-plant-output/)); Array Diffuse +0.64% measured vs 0.63% predicted ([Array, PVPMC 2022](https://pvpmc.sandia.gov/download/4718)); Soltec +1.2% to +3.5% against standard backtracking ([Soltec](https://soltec.com/en/press-room/https-lab-soltec-com-6-2-teamtrack-gain/)).
- **Short tests mislead.** Array reported +2.04% over 15 days and +4.42% over 4 days in the same deck, and uses alternating A-B tests (odd and even inverter groups swap algorithms on alternate days) to cancel group differences ([Array, PVPMC 2022](https://pvpmc.sandia.gov/download/4718)).
- **Self-learning and sky imaging.** Sandia's patented sky-imaging tracker uses a CNN (a neural network for images) to predict irradiance at each angle and claims up to 2% a year, at TRL 6 (technology readiness level 6, a prototype in a relevant environment) ([Sandia SAND2024-03129M](https://www.sandia.gov/app/uploads/sites/223/2024/03/Single-Axis-Tracking-via-Sky-Imaging-and-Machine-Learning-Algorithm-SAND2024-03129M.pdf)). Fraunhofer DeepTrack couples digital twins with forecasts and trains a cheap neural "metamodel" ([Fraunhofer ISE](https://www.ise.fraunhofer.de/en/research-projects/deeptrack.html)).
- **Fault detection.** NREL compares power with simulated plane-of-array light, daily ([NREL](https://research-hub.nrel.gov/en/publications/a-methodology-to-analyze-photovoltaic-tracker-uptime-2/)). Sandia classifies residuals for stall and timing faults ([Sandia](https://www.sandia.gov/research/publications/details/machine-learning-for-single-axis-tracker-fault-detection-and-classification-2024-01-01/)). Invenergy's TADA clusters tracker angles with DBSCAN (a method that groups similar points and flags loners) ([Invenergy](https://invenergy.com/news/shining-a-light-on-solar-o-m-with-tracker-anomaly-detection-algorithms)).
- **Actuator health is patent and vendor territory.** No peer-reviewed tracker-specific motor current paper was found. Patents cover torque from current ([US 11,588,425](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11588425)), power vs expected ([US 12,003,208](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12003208)), and stuck-tracker current signatures ([US 2024/0396497](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20240396497)). Tecnalia found field motors drawing more current than specified while giving less torque ([Tecnalia](https://www.tecnalia.com/en/news/we-prevent-and-analyse-failures-in-photovoltaic-solar-tracker-motors)).
- **Multi-objective control is mostly simulation.** Agrivoltaic light targets met 91% of apple trees' light needs at about 20% less electricity (modelled) ([pv Europe](https://www.pveurope.eu/agriculture/fraunhofer-ise-algorithm-balances-crop-growth-and-solar-efficiency)). Curtailment-aware tilting lowered modelled module temperature by up to 10 °C ([Haydous et al. 2024](https://apvi.org.au/solar-research-conference/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Haydous_Z_Energy_Yield_vs._System_Longevity_An_Innovative_Algorithm_for_Single-Axis_Solar_Trackers_in_a_Curtailment-Prone_Landscape.pdf)). A persistence rule cut excess tracker movement from 114% to 0.16% for about 0.17% of energy ([Adinolfi Borea et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics15030597)).

### 7.2 Hardware: what the kit can and cannot do

- **Kit contents check.** The shared kits have no accelerometer, IMU, humidity sensor, rain sensor or relay. KTS021 adds an LM35, 2 extra photoresistors, 2 shock sensors, a third SG90 and an 8x8 matrix ([Botland KTS021](https://botland.store/arduino-starter-kits/14317-electronic-components-set-for-arduino-iduino-kts021-5903351241700.html)). The UNO R4 WiFi has no user button, buzzer or IMU ([ABX00087 datasheet](https://docs.arduino.cc/resources/datasheets/ABX00087-datasheet.pdf)).
- **Sensor geometry.** Tilted LDR pairs give a brightness-independent signal (tan α × tan θ), and a log-ratio removes LDR part-to-part mismatch as a constant offset (hardware notes, CALC). Pyramid and vane sensors are published ([Lee et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3297124/); [CSAE 2015](https://www.aeeisp.com/nygcxb/article/doi/10.11975/j.issn.1002-6819.2015.14.025)).
- **Reference cell.** A spare cell as a fixed reference is how commercial snow and soiling sensors work ([SEVEN 3S snow sensor](https://sevensensor.com/files/d/en/3S-SS-MB_Snow_Sensor.pdf)).
- **Wind.** Only a relative index is honest: servo current under load, a printed drag flap on the potentiometer, or a printed cup anemometer. Array's passive stow reacts to wind load rather than an anemometer ([Array](https://arraytechinc.com/blog/turning-extreme-wind-into-an-operational-advantage-key-engineering-lessons-from-solar-tracker-week/)).
- **Holding without power.** Self-locking worm drives are the industry answer ([US 8,176,806](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8176806)), but a printed worm is more than a day of work. Balancing the panel plus a friction collar is the realistic upgrade.
- **Operator UI.** 12x8 LED matrix (nearly free in power), capacitive touch, buttons, buzzers and RGB LEDs from the kits ([Arduino LED matrix tutorial](https://docs.arduino.cc/tutorials/uno-r4-wifi/led-matrix)).

### 8.2 Claims the team can make (with "in the sources we searched")

1. **A receipt for every move and every stow, at the moment of the command,** from two physically independent witnesses (light seen by the module, and actuator effort).
2. **A failure label that names which witness disagreed** (JAMMED, SLIPPED, NO DRIVE, SENSOR FAULT, UNVERIFIABLE), so a non-expert knows what to check.
3. **Claimed vs verified availability on the device itself,** with unverifiable time shown honestly as a range.
4. **A sensitivity-aware check** that switches between LDR difference near alignment and panel current far from it.

The notes could not establish whether any commercial controller does continuous, independent, row-level verification, because vendors do not publish their logic ([IEA PVPS T13](https://iea-pvps.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/IEA-PVPS-T13-26-2024-SLIDES-Bifacial-Tracking-Systems.pdf)). Before the pitch, spend 15 minutes searching Hackster, Instructables, Devpost and GitHub for "servo verification solar tracker". The notes did not search those exhaustively.

## 10. Runner-up options

1. **Crop-first agrivoltaic mode (about 3 hours).**
   - What: one of the two spare KTS021 photoresistors sits under the panel as a "crop sensor". The tracker lets light through until the crop's daily light dose is met, then tracks for power. The LED matrix shows a "crop dose" bar next to a "panel energy" bar. This is a miniature of Mälardalen's DLIT idea (daily light integral tracking) ([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/?p=67099)).
   - Evidence: Sun'Agri, Ombrea, Insolight and Nextracker sell dynamic agrivoltaics ([Fraunhofer publica](https://publica.fraunhofer.de/handle/publica/489724); [TaiyangNews](https://taiyangnews.info/business/french-energy-giant-acquires-ombrea); [Nextracker](https://info.nextracker.com/nx-agripv)). Researchers call for standardised indicators and suggest a 30% shading threshold to protect fruit quality ([Agronomy 2024](https://doaj.org/article/cac1d8898dec4b8586e325c3e80a621b)).
   - Why second: the strategies are simulation-only, the crop benefit cannot be shown on a desk, and there is no dollar-sized failure story.
2. **"Point at the most light, not at the lamp" scan map (about 2 to 3 hours).**
   - What: sweep a coarse grid of angles, log panel power, fit a cosine lobe, show the map as a heatmap on the 12x8 matrix, and park at the best angle. Block the lamp with a "cloud" card next to a white reflector card, and the tracker points at the reflector. Rescan only when the residual grows.
   - Evidence: a miniature of Sandia's US 11,823,409 (up to 2% claimed, TRL 6) ([Sandia](https://www.sandia.gov/app/uploads/sites/223/2024/03/Single-Axis-Tracking-via-Sky-Imaging-and-Machine-Learning-Algorithm-SAND2024-03129M.pdf)) and Burgos radiance-based tracking ([UBU](https://riubu.ubu.es/handle/10259/9671)).
   - Why not first: power differences under a lamp sit near the INA219's resolution, and scan-and-park is patented ([EP2482092](https://data.epo.org/gpi/EP2482092A2); [US 8,178,775](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8178775)). It pairs well with the main idea, because the same fitted model feeds the verification prediction.
3. **Weather-aware safety state machine with verified cleaning tilt (about 2 to 3 hours).**
   - What: priority WIND > SNOW/SOIL > NIGHT > NORMAL, with hysteresis. Wind index from servo current under load or a printed flap on the potentiometer. Soiling detected when panel current falls well below what the LDRs predict, with the LM35 gating "snow" to near-freezing. Tilt to maximum, then check whether panel current recovered.
   - Evidence: steeper tilts shed snow faster ([EPJ PV 2024](https://www.epj-pv.org/articles/epjpv/abs/2024/01/pv230061/pv230061.html)); vertical night stow with coating cut soiling losses by up to 85% in Saudi Arabia ([Prog. Photovolt. preprint](https://flamechallenge.authorea.com/doi/full/10.22541/au.168052690.09908310/v1)).
   - Why not first: indoor snow and wind are simulations of simulations. The "verified recovery" step is really the main idea applied to cleaning, so it can be a stretch goal on top of it.

## 11. What is still uncertain

- **3E's full paper was not opened.** The 66% to 88% figure and its filtering logic come from 3E's summary.
- **The 550-row case is a vendor case study** by a company that sells drone inspections.
- **No source gives a per-component failure split,** so the team should not claim which fault class is most common.
- **SG-90 slip and stall signatures are not published** for this panel load. The thresholds must come from the team's bench trials.
- **Detection accuracy is unknown until measured.** If the confusion matrix is poor for one class (likely SLIP near alignment), say so and show the UNVERIFIABLE label doing its job.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "innovation" means for this team. The kit cannot win on energy, and the utility software frontier is about fractions of a percent that a desk lamp cannot reveal. The industry's most expensive gap, however, is not pointing accuracy. It is trust: rows report angles they are not at, availability numbers float between 66% and 99% depending on who counts, and a single unverified hail stow can decide whether a plant loses millions. A hobby servo with no position feedback is an honest model of that blind spot, and two INA219s plus four LDRs are enough to close it in a way judges can test with a finger.

The less obvious lesson is about honesty as a feature. A tracker that says "I could not verify that move" is more useful than one that says "OK" every time, which is exactly the failure the field data describes. If the team ships one idea, it should be that the device reports what happened, not what it was told to do.
