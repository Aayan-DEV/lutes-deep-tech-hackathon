# Mongolia: Quantified Solar and Energy Problems a Small Smart Sun-Tracker Could Address (2015-2026)

Source type tags: [Official] = World Bank / UN / government / IFRC; [Peer-reviewed] = journal article; [Thesis] = university thesis; [News] = media; [Vendor] = company material; [Conf] = conference paper.

Important caveat for the report writer: most herder-solar data in public sources is from the 2000-2013 programme era. I found no reliable 2015-2026 survey of herder solar ownership, kit sizes, or failure rates. Several key World Bank PDFs (ESMAP "BlueSky" paper, 2014 "Development Impacts" report) returned HTTP 403/404 when fetched, so their internal numbers are known only through search snippets.

## 1. Nomadic herders and portable solar (100,000 Solar Ger programme, REAP)

### Takeaway
Mongolia already ran one of the world's best-known off-grid solar programmes: about 100,000 portable solar home systems (SHS) reached roughly 60-70% of herders by 2012, sold at about half price through 50 private sales and service centres. The data is old (pre-2013), and there is no public, recent measure of how many of those systems still work.

### Cited Findings
- The National 100,000 Solar Ger Electrification Program started in 2000 (one source says 1999). The World Bank Renewable Energy for Rural Access Project (REAP) helped complete it [Official, 2013] — [World Bank results story](https://www.worldbank.org/en/results/2013/04/08/portable-solar-power-for-nomadic-herders); [World Bank project page P099321](https://projects.worldbank.org/en/projects-operations/project-detail/P099321)
- Closing figure: over 100,146 SHS units delivered, giving an estimated 70% of herders (about 500,000 people) electricity access [Official, ~2012-2013] — [World Bank results story](https://www.worldbank.org/en/results/2013/04/08/portable-solar-power-for-nomadic-herders); same 100,146 figure in a 2017 Murdoch University thesis [Thesis] — [Murdoch research portal](https://researchportal.murdoch.edu.au/esploro/outputs/graduate/A-critical-review-on-solar-home/991005540537907891)
- Coverage figures conflict: ESMAP paper by World Bank staff gives 60-70% herder coverage and says REAP beat its target by about 35% [Official] — [ESMAP BlueSky paper](https://www.esmap.org/sites/default/files/esmap-files/BlueSky.pdf); the project record in December 2011 showed 54.4% of herders served by SHS and small wind systems (target 50%), with 54,841 herder families having received 87,763 systems (mid-project figure) — [World Bank project page](https://projects.worldbank.org/en/projects-operations/project-detail/P099321)
- Before REAP, donor grants had given SHS to over 30,000 herder families by 2005, and the effort was stagnating [Official] — [World Bank results story](https://www.worldbank.org/en/results/2013/04/08/portable-solar-power-for-nomadic-herders)
- Delivery model: government bulk-procured SHS to get low prices; herders paid with a subsidy covering about half the cost; 50 privately owned SHS sales and service centres, partnered with soum (village) administrators [Official] — [ESMAP BlueSky paper](https://www.esmap.org/sites/default/files/esmap-files/BlueSky.pdf); [World Bank documents: "Capturing the sun in the land of the blue sky"](https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/718091468323710464)
- Remaining gap at programme end: some 200,000-300,000 of the herder population still lacked electricity [Official] — [ESMAP BlueSky paper](https://www.esmap.org/sites/default/files/esmap-files/BlueSky.pdf)
- Scale of target market: about 140,000 nomadic households (REACH case study, research done 2018-19) [Academic case study] — [REACH Alliance](https://reachalliance.org/case-study/providing-electricity-to-nomadic-herders-mongolias-renewable-energy-for-rural-access-project)
- Herders move 3-4 times a year; a quarter of Mongolia's population are nomadic herders [Thesis, 2017] — [Murdoch research portal](https://researchportal.murdoch.edu.au/esploro/outputs/graduate/A-critical-review-on-solar-home/991005540537907891)
- What herders power: food refrigeration, phone charging, TV to watch the weather forecast (described as essential to herding); before solar, herders trekked to a village to charge phones [News, 2017] — [ZME Science](https://zmescience.com/ecology/renewable-energy-ecology/70-of-mongolian-nomads-now-have-solar-power); World Bank video: families charge mobile phones and watch TV, and power is "back right away" after moving the ger [Official video, 2012] — [UN Media / World Bank video](https://media.un.org/avlibrary/en/asset/u121/u121102d)
- Panel placement: panels are often mounted on or leaned against the sides of gers [News, 2013] — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/features/2013/12/16/mongolias-nomads-warm-to-solar-power); a herder in the World Bank video says that when the panel is set straight up it can catch sun almost all day, which implies a fixed, steep tilt [Official video, 2012] — [UN Media](https://media.un.org/unifeed/en/asset/u121/u121102d)
- The ger door traditionally faces south (sun and warmth) [News] — [Yahoo/Architectural Digest-style feature](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/articles/mongolia-traditional-housing-type-meets-123218623.html)
- A 2014 World Bank report, "Mongolia: Development Impacts of Solar-Powered Electricity Services", is based on two post-project beneficiary surveys (Chapter 2) — [World Bank Open Knowledge](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/1a1c501f-9d84-5b21-99b3-3285e7fa4a3f/full) (403 on fetch; survey numbers not extracted)

### Inferences
- The herder market is "electrified but under-served": most herders own a first-generation SHS from 2000-2012, so by 2026 many panels are 14-26 years old and the original lead-acid-era batteries (chemistry not confirmed, see Gaps) are far past typical SHS battery life. This points to a replacement / upgrade market rather than a first-access market.
- Since panels are leaned on the ger wall at a fixed steep angle, a herder SHS likely loses morning and afternoon yield. Hand-turning is plausible but not documented.
- The product a herder actually needs is "more energy per small panel + knowing battery health", because TV (dzud weather forecasts), phones, and fridges are the loads.

### Gaps
- Typical kit sizes (the brief's 50-200 W range) could not be verified. I did not find a fetched source stating REAP SHS wattage or battery capacity; the ESMAP and 2014 World Bank PDFs that likely contain this were blocked (403/404).
- No 2015-2026 survey of the share of herder households with working solar. The 70% figure is a 2012 programme-coverage estimate, not a recent household survey. Mongolia National Statistics Office household data was not checked.
- No Mongolia-specific data on SHS failure or battery replacement rates. General SHS literature says the battery is usually the first component to fail [Peer-reviewed/preprint] — [Oxford arXiv 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.14098); [Narayan et al., TU Delft](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/estimating-battery-lifetimes-in-solar-home-system-design-using-a-), but there is no Mongolian field number.
- No documented evidence that herders manually turn panels during the day. Searches found only fixed, leaned placement.

## 2. Climate: solar resource, cold, low sun, snow, dust, dzud

### Takeaway
Mongolia has a strong, clear-sky solar resource (270-300 clear days, 2,250-3,300 sunshine hours, about 1,200-1,600 kWh/m² per year), but it is paired with very low winter sun, extreme cold, spring dust storms in the Gobi, and dzud winters that killed about 7.1-7.2 million animals in 2024.

### Cited Findings
- 270-300 clear days and 2,250-3,300 sunshine hours per year [Official, 2012 SREP presentation] — [CIF SREP Mongolia progress update](https://www.cif.org/sites/cif_enc/files/Mongolia%20-%20Meeting%20of%20SREP%20Pilot%20Countries,%20Progress%20Update%20(ppt)%20-%20October%202012.pdf); repeated in [GRATA law firm renewable regulation note](https://gratanet.com/laravel-filemanager/files/3/Regulation_of_renewable_energy_in_mongolia_en.pdf)
- Daily irradiation estimates conflict: 3.4-5.4 kWh/m²/day (citing IRENA; notes too few ground stations to map it properly) [Peer-reviewed, National University of Mongolia journal] — [NUM physics journal](https://journal.num.edu.mn/physics/article/download/365/327/1329); 4.3-4.7 kWh/m²/day and ~1,400 kWh/m²/yr average — [Mondaq/GRATA](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/renewables/1395290/regulation-of-renewable-energy-in-mongolia); 5.5-6.0 kWh/m²/day over two-thirds of the country (SREP investment plan figure, likely optimistic) — [Oxford Business Group 2012](https://oxfordbusinessgroup.com/reports/mongolia/2012-report/economy/renewable-results-using-open-space-to-generate-wind-and-solar-power)
- Area split: 17% of the country above 1,600 kWh/m²/yr, 25% at 1,400-1,600, 51% at 1,200-1,400, 7% below 1,200 [Official, 2012] — [CIF SREP presentation](https://www.cif.org/sites/cif_enc/files/Mongolia%20-%20Meeting%20of%20SREP%20Pilot%20Countries,%20Progress%20Update%20(ppt)%20-%20October%202012.pdf)
- The Gobi (south) is the strongest band (ADB 2009 study); Gobi potential about 5.4 kWh/m²/day (NREL-based, 2011) [Industry presentation] — [Newcom 2011 presentation, Renewable Energy Institute](https://renewable-ei.org/en/images/pdf/20110912/110912_Newcom.pdf); [pv magazine "Step by steppe"](https://pv-magazine.com/magazine-archive/step-by-steppe)
- Ulaanbaatar winter PV yield falls to about 2.43 kWh/day per kW installed (NASA POWER-based, third-party tool) [Vendor/tool] — [ProfileSolar Ulaanbaatar](https://profilesolar.com/locations/Mongolia/Ulan-Bator/)
- Dust storms: 20-37 dust-storm days/year in desert and semi-desert areas, 10-17 in the western Great Lakes area, under 5 in central/northern mountains; highest in the Gobi [Conf, ISPRS, date not stated] — [ISPRS proceedings](https://www.isprs.org/proceedings/XXXVII/congress/7_pdf/5_WG-VII-5/17.pdf)
- Satellite dust-day counts (spring): 10 (2017), 31 (2018), 42 (2019), 39 (2020), 54 (2021 peak); March is the most dust-active month [Peer-reviewed, Remote Sensing 2025] — [MDPI Remote Sensing 17:410](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-17-00410/article_deploy/remotesensing-17-00410.pdf)
- Dust intensity rising about 0.8/year at roughly two-thirds of stations, 2001-2022 [Peer-reviewed] — [FAO AGRIS record](https://agris.fao.org/search/zh/records/687a94e5464818917f338fba)
- 2023-24 dzud: about 7.2 million livestock dead by 24 April 2024 (about 11% of the herd, highest share since 2010), worst in Sukhbaatar, Dornogovi, Govisumber, Khentii [Official, FAO GIEWS] — [ReliefWeb GIEWS update, 6 May 2024](https://reliefweb.int/report/mongolia/giews-update-mongolia-6-may-2024-extreme-winter-weather-known-dzud-driving-acute-food-insecurity-pastoral-households-country); UNICEF: 7.1 million by 25 April [Official] — [UNICEF sitrep No. 3](https://reliefweb.int/report/mongolia/unicef-mongolia-humanitarian-situation-report-no-3-dzud-22-march-2024)
- The dzud affected about 75% of herder households; 2,257 families had lost over 70% of their livestock by March 2024 [Official, IFRC] — [IFRC operation document](https://go-api.ifrc.org/api/DownloadFile/87478/MDRMN020OS); 70% of the country was in "dzud or near dzud" conditions vs 17% a year earlier — [ACAPS Mongolia](https://acaps.org/en/countries/mongolia)
- 2022-23 winter killed about 4.4 million head; national herd was 64.7 million at end-2023 [News] — [bne IntelliNews](https://www.intellinews.com/mongolia-s-livestock-losses-top-half-a-million-in-devastating-dzud-312455/)
- Energy and dzud link: TV/radio give short-term weather forecasts; phones are key for coordination between valleys and for contact with children in boarding schools [News] — [ZME Science](https://zmescience.com/ecology/renewable-energy-ecology/70-of-mongolian-nomads-now-have-solar-power); JICA studied how herders receive dzud warnings and what equipment they own [Official] — [JICA report](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/12373205.pdf) (findings not extracted)

### Inferences
- Low winter sun (computed, not sourced): noon sun elevation at the December solstice is about 90° - latitude - 23.4°, so about 24.6° at 42°N (south Gobi), 18.7° at Ulaanbaatar (47.9°N) and 14.6° at 52°N (north). A panel leaned at a steep fixed angle actually suits this noon sun, but in winter the sun rises in the south-east and sets in the south-west over a short day, so an east-west turn could matter.
- Dzud is when energy matters most (forecasts, phones, light, maybe heated water for animals), and it is also when solar output and battery capacity are lowest (short days, cold, snow). This mismatch is the sharpest herder pain point.
- Gobi dust (20-37 storm days, peaking in March) implies soiling losses and a value for a "clean me" alert from a power-vs-expected-irradiance check.

### Gaps
- No verified Mongolia-specific data on battery performance at -30 to -40 °C, or on snow cover losses on herder panels.
- No PV soiling measurements for the Gobi or Mongolia were found.
- No 2024 data on how many herders lost power during the dzud or whether SHS units survived.
- Ulaanbaatar is widely described as the world's coldest capital [News] — [SBS Dateline](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/dateline/article/worlds-coldest-capital-relying-on-coal-is-also-among-its-most-polluted/jzu9r77dr), but I did not fetch a specific minimum-temperature figure from a source.

## 3. Ulaanbaatar air pollution, coal stoves, and electric-heating programmes

### Takeaway
Ger-district coal stoves made Ulaanbaatar one of the most polluted cities in winter (PM2.5 up to 436 µg/m³). The government tried a 50% then 100% free night electricity tariff (2015-2017) and a raw-coal ban (May 2019). No Ulaanbaatar-specific study of how much this smog cuts PV output was found.

### Cited Findings
- Winter PM2.5 reaches 436 µg/m³ and PM10 1,100 µg/m³; Ulaanbaatar among the top 5 cities with worst air quality [Thesis, TU Wien 2017] — [TU Wien repositum](https://repositum.tuwien.at/handle/20.500.12708/3828)
- As of 2022, 84.1% of Ulaanbaatar electricity came from thermal coal plants, about 9% from renewables [Policy paper, SAIS] — [SAIS Perspectives 2024](https://saisperspectives.com/23-24issue/2024/7/28/good-cheap-fast-is-solar-energy-a-magic-wand)
- Night tariff: 50% night discount from 2015 (MNT 3.2 billion deficit); free night electricity (roughly 21:00-06:00) for ger districts from January 2017, expected to add a MNT 25.8 billion deficit; over 146,000 ger-district residents eligible [News/Regulator] — [UB Post](https://theubposts.com/a/11774); [ERC macro overview Jan 2017](https://ecrc.mn/media/ecrc/content/macro-overview/2017/Macro_overview_2017_January_eng.pdf); [BreatheLife](https://breathelife2030.org/?p=5060)
- Raw-coal ban from 15 May 2019 in five central districts (Bayanzurkh, Songinokhairkhan, Sukhbaatar, Khan-Uul, Chingeltei), with some khoroo exemptions [News, 2019] — [NPR via KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/news/news/international/2019/07/30/mongolias-capital-banned-coal-to-fix-its-pollution-problem-will-it-work)
- About 202,000 ger families; household coal burning blamed for 80% of the city's air pollution; electric heaters able to warm a poorly insulated ger were out of reach for many, and unsubsidized electricity cost more than coal [News, 2019] — [NPR via KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/news/news/international/2019/07/30/mongolias-capital-banned-coal-to-fix-its-pollution-problem-will-it-work)
- Analogue evidence (not Mongolia): in Hong Kong, PM2.5 above 33.5 µg/m³ cut global horizontal irradiance by more than 5% in most months; PV energy losses up to 7.0% (crystalline Si) and 9.7% (thin film) [Peer-reviewed] — [PolyU research](https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/quantification-of-the-impact-of-fine-particulate-matter-on-solar-/); in Suwon, Korea, GHI fell 11.9-34.3% and DNI (direct beam) 22.5-55.4% depending on PM level [Peer-reviewed, 2025] — [SKKU](https://pure.skku.edu/en/publications/impact-of-particulate-matter-pm10-pm25-on-global-horizontal-irrad/)

### Inferences
- If DNI (direct beam light, the part a tracker chases) falls 22-55% on bad PM days as in Korea, a tracker's advantage shrinks most on Ulaanbaatar's worst smog days, because diffuse light comes from the whole sky. A light-sensing device could measure this and switch to a flat "diffuse" position.
- Ulaanbaatar ger districts are a heating-and-tariff problem more than a solar problem: night tariffs mean the useful product is a smart meter / load scheduler, not a tracker. A tracker is weak here.

### Gaps
- No measured Ulaanbaatar PM2.5-to-PV-output study found.
- No post-2019 outcome data (air quality, coal use, electric heater uptake) was retrieved. Whether the free night tariff is still in force in 2026 was not verified.

## 4. Tracking gains at Mongolian latitudes, and manual turning

### Takeaway
I found no Mongolian measurement of tracker gain. Published dual-axis gains elsewhere range from near zero (hot Libya) to about 50% (Croatia), so the cold, clear Mongolian climate is favourable in principle, but the number for Mongolia is unverified.

### Cited Findings
- Dual-axis tracking gave about 50% more energy than fixed in two measurement cases, Croatia [Peer-reviewed] — [Politehnika, Univ. of Rijeka](https://politehnika.uniri.hr/index.php/politehnika/article/view/12)
- Dual-axis gain 28.31% on a cloudy day; earlier studies about 35% over fixed (Vietnam, 2020) [Peer-reviewed] — [ASTESJ 050306](https://astesj.com/publications/ASTESJ_050306.pdf)
- Tracker output slightly below fixed in eastern Libya because of higher cell temperature (2022) [Peer-reviewed] — [EEJET, uran.ua](https://journals.uran.ua/eejet/article/download/266256/262383/614853)
- Vendor note on Inner Mongolia (China, Kubuqi desert, ~40.26°N): single-axis tracker vs fixed; states that winter gain from tilted single-axis tracking is greatly reduced at latitudes below about 35° [Vendor] — [Solis case study](https://www.solisinverters.com/fi/documentation/402880ed9363086101946ed94cdc7612.html)
- Manual turning: the only first-hand herder quote found describes a panel "set straight up" catching sun almost all day, i.e. fixed [Official video, 2012] — [UN Media](https://media.un.org/unifeed/en/asset/u121/u121102d)

### Inferences
- Cold improves PV efficiency (cell temperature stays low), so the Libya-type heat penalty does not apply; Mongolia's clear, dry, cold air favours tracking more than hot or humid sites.
- For a small herder panel, a 1-axis east-west tracker (or a "smart reminder to turn it") is likely the realistic product; dual-axis servos add cost and failure points in -40 °C, wind and dust.

### Gaps
- No Mongolian or 42-52°N clear-sky tracker field study found. A PVGIS or NASA POWER simulation for Ulaanbaatar and Dalanzadgad would be needed (not done here).
- No documented practice of herders turning panels by hand.

## 5. Existing Mongolian solar companies, programmes, and gaps

### Takeaway
The public record is dominated by the 2000-2012 programme and its 50 private sales and service centres. I could not find reliable, recent information on which Mongolian companies sell or service herder solar kits today, which is itself a signal of a thin, under-documented after-sales market.

### Cited Findings
- 50 privately owned SHS sales and service centres were set up across Mongolia under REAP [Official] — [World Bank documents](https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/718091468323710464)
- SREP (Scaling-Up Renewable Energy Program, Climate Investment Funds) Mongolia pilot existed from about 2012 [Official] — [CIF SREP presentation](https://www.cif.org/sites/cif_enc/files/Mongolia%20-%20Meeting%20of%20SREP%20Pilot%20Countries,%20Progress%20Update%20(ppt)%20-%20October%202012.pdf)
- Newcom (Mongolian company) presented on Mongolian renewables in 2011 [Industry] — [Renewable Energy Institute PDF](https://renewable-ei.org/en/images/pdf/20110912/110912_Newcom.pdf)

### Inferences
- With original SHS units aging and the REAP-era service network not visibly active in recent sources, remote diagnostics (WiFi/cellular reporting of panel and battery health) addresses a real service gap: a technician trip to a herder can be hundreds of km.
- Caution: WiFi does not exist on the steppe. A herder product needs Bluetooth to a phone or SMS/LoRa, not WiFi.

### Gaps
- Names, sizes, and products of current (2020-2026) Mongolian solar retailers for herders not found. Mongolian-language searches and Montsame / UB Post coverage were not completed.
- No data on current after-sales service quality, warranty claims, or the fate of the 50 service centres.
- IRENA Renewables Readiness Assessment for Mongolia and Energy Regulatory Commission tariff tables were not fetched directly.

## 6. Is an Arduino-class smart tracker a credible proof of concept for each problem?

### Takeaway
Credible for the herder market as a proof of concept for "smart, monitored, self-aiming small solar" (more winter energy, battery health, soiling alerts, remote diagnostics). Weak for Ulaanbaatar air pollution and heating, where the real lever is tariffs, insulation, and heating, not panel aiming.

### Cited Findings
- Herder loads (TV forecasts, phones, fridge) and 70% coverage with aging systems — [ZME Science](https://zmescience.com/ecology/renewable-energy-ecology/70-of-mongolian-nomads-now-have-solar-power); [World Bank results story](https://www.worldbank.org/en/results/2013/04/08/portable-solar-power-for-nomadic-herders)
- Battery is the first SHS component to fail; smart voltage control can extend lead-acid life by 25% (simulation) — [Oxford arXiv 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.14098)
- PM cuts direct beam far more than total light — [SKKU](https://pure.skku.edu/en/publications/impact-of-particulate-matter-pm10-pm25-on-global-horizontal-irrad/)

### Inferences
1. Herder winter energy shortfall (dzud): credible PoC. Light sensors + servo show extra Wh vs a fixed panel; the power meter proves it. Must demo cold tolerance and low self-power use. Strength: medium-high.
2. Battery health / failure in remote areas: credible PoC. The power meter + display can show state of charge and warn before a battery dies; the Oxford work supports smart charge control. Strength: high (arguably more valuable than the tracking itself).
3. Gobi dust soiling: credible PoC. Compare measured power vs light-sensor irradiance to flag "clean the panel". Strength: medium.
4. Snow on panels: partial. A tracker can tilt the panel steep to shed snow; unverified in field. Strength: low-medium.
5. Ulaanbaatar smog cutting PV: weak PoC. The device can measure smog loss and choose a diffuse-light position, but ger-district solar is not the main problem. Strength: low.
6. Ulaanbaatar coal heating / night tariff: not a tracker problem. A smart meter that shifts loads to free night hours fits better. Strength: low.
- Hardware risks to state honestly: hobby servos and lead-acid batteries struggle at -30 to -40 °C; moving parts on a ger that relocates 3-4 times a year; no WiFi on the steppe; cost must stay near a herder SHS price, which was itself half-subsidised.

### Gaps
- No Mongolian price data for current SHS kits, so cost-competitiveness of an added tracker cannot be judged.
- No evidence that herders would pay for tracking versus simply buying a second panel (panel prices have fallen sharply, which may make "add another panel" cheaper than "add a tracker"). This needs a local price check and herder interviews.
