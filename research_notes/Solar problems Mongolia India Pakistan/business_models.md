# Business models and innovative product angles for a small-solar startup built around a smart sun-tracking / light-sensing device (South Asia and Mongolia)

Notation: "Source:" links follow each claim. Items tagged [MARKETING] are company or vendor claims with no independent check. Items tagged [SIM] are simulations, not field measurements. Everything under "Inferences" is my reasoning from the cited facts, not a sourced fact.

---

## Q1. Which small-solar business models work in developing countries, and why (PAYGo, energy-as-a-service, pump/water-as-a-service, cooperatives, rental, IoT + mobile money)? What are the failure stories?

### Takeaway
PAYGo (pay-as-you-go: the customer pays small daily/weekly amounts by mobile money, and the device is remotely locked if they stop paying) works when the product is reliable, payments are matched to the customer's cash-flow, and credit is managed like a lender, not a hardware seller. Its main killers are defaults (about 40% of customers struggle in Kenya/Uganda), weather shocks to farmer-customers, aggressive selling, high fixed costs and currency/funding stress. Shared pay-per-use assets (Oorja-style irrigation-as-a-service) avoid the household credit problem by selling water, not hardware, and are the model where a tracker's extra hours are most directly monetisable.

### Cited Findings

**PAYGo market size and share**
- 39% of off-grid solar products were sold through PAYGo financing in 2023, up from 24% in 2018 (World Bank/ESMAP/GOGLA trends report, as reported). Source: [Mercom India](https://mercomindia.com/pay-as-you-go-financing-off-grid-solar)
- GOGLA-affiliated companies sold about 9.3 million solar kits in 2024: a slight rise on 2023 but below the 2022 peak. East Africa led the market. Source: [pv magazine, "Hard times for pay-as-you-go model", May 2025](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/05/27/hard-times-for-pay-as-you-go-model/); [GOGLA 2024 market report page](https://gogla.org/reports/semi-annual-solar-market-report/insights-from-goglas-2024-sales-and-impact-data/)
- GOGLA reported solar water pump sales of 291,200 units in 2024 vs 23,210 in 2023, but part of the jump is because new companies were added to the dataset, so it is not pure market growth. Source: [TaiyangNews on GOGLA data](https://taiyangnews.info/business/gogla-off-grid-solar-kit-sales-reach-record-high-2025)
- For 2025, GOGLA reports total sales at a record 10.2 million units, with PAYGo volumes matching cash volumes. Source: [TaiyangNews](https://taiyangnews.info/business/gogla-off-grid-solar-kit-sales-reach-record-high-2025)
- In India, off-grid kit sales were declining (2022 data) as the mix shifts from lanterns and plug-and-play kits to "weak-grid" products (backup for unreliable grid). Source: [pv magazine India, Aug 2023](https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/2023/08/24/global-sales-of-off-grid-solar-energy-kits-hit-9-5-million-units-in-2022)
- pv magazine argues PAYGo "may have hit a structural ceiling" because microloans become unaffordable when purchasing power stagnates, and results-based financing / subsidies are increasingly necessary. Source: [pv magazine, May 2025](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/05/27/hard-times-for-pay-as-you-go-model/)

**What makes PAYGo work (and fail)**
- GOGLA's "Keeping the Lights On" study: about 40% of PAYGo solar home system customers in Kenya and Uganda struggle to pay, and the likelihood of struggling rises the longer the customer has the system. Source: [GOGLA, Keeping the Lights On](https://gogla.org/reports/keeping-the-lights-on/)
- CGAP: if repayment rates fall, companies should re-examine product and collections before concluding that poorer customers are unbankable. Customers who do not understand payment terms are at higher risk of early default. Source: [CGAP, "Stepping Back: Product, Collections and Credit Risk in PAYGo Solar"](https://www.cgap.org/blog/stepping-back-product-collections-and-credit-risk-in-paygo-solar); [CGAP, "Two Sides, One Coin"](https://www.cgap.org/blog/two-sides-one-coin-credit-risk-management-and-consumer-protection)
- CGAP illustration of thin margins: if 5 of 100 borrowers default fully, the 95 good payers must cover the loss; some executives believed their credit operations were not sustainable at current prices. Source: [CGAP, "Growth vs. Sustainability: Credit Risk in PAYGo Solar"](https://www.cgap.org/blog/growth-vs-sustainability-credit-risk-in-paygo-solar)
- Weather shock: in Zambia's 2018/19 drought, PAYGo companies saw late repayments rise by up to 20% vs good periods. Farmers are about 40% of customers and the highest-risk segment. Source: [Shell Foundation / Pula learning report (PDF)](https://shellfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/SF-Pula-learning-report.pdf)
- SunCulture's CEO (2024) says PAYGo's reputation was "tainted" by sharp selling practices that sign up customers regardless of affordability, and recommends separating sales and credit teams. [Company exec opinion] Source: [pv magazine, May 2024](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2024/05/09/unlocking-africas-potential-to-feed-the-world/)

**M-KOPA (the success case, and what it became)**
- M-KOPA reported its first full-year profit in 2024: KES 1.2 bn (about $9.2 m), reversing a KES 3.2 bn (about $24.7 m) loss in 2023; revenue rose 66% to about $416 m, driven by smartphones and digital financial services. Filings credit cost control, stronger credit underwriting and portfolio management. Source: [TechCabal, Oct 2025](https://techcabal.com/2025/10/07/m-kopa-turns-first-ever-profit-revenue-surges-66-416/)
- Daily payment = item cost / 365; default rate around 10%, which the company says has been stable for 4 years. Underwriting uses alternative data (mobile usage, repayment patterns). Source: [TechCrunch, Nov 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/28/financial-inclusion-drives-m-kopa-to-400m-in-arr/)

**Failure stories**
- Mobisol (Germany/East Africa) filed for self-administered insolvency in April 2019 and was bought by Engie in September 2019. Sector-wide pressures from about 2017: delinquent payments, high fixed costs, liquidity crunches, droughts, civil conflict, mismanagement. Insiders say many problems were internal and profit expectations were too high. Source: [NextBillion](https://nextbillion.net/rural-solar-energy-enterprise-mobisol/); [pv magazine Deutschland](https://www.pv-magazine.de/2019/09/03/engie-kauft-den-insolventen-offgrid-spezialisten-mobisol/); [African Energy (paywalled)](https://www.africa-energy.com/news-centre/article/mobisol-and-solarkiosk-insolvencies-highlight-risk-grid)
- Simpa Networks (India's flagship PAYGo solar company, ADB-backed, targeted 60,000+ households by 2015) is listed by the Global Innovation Fund as "now dissolved", implementation period ending May 2019. I found no public explanation of why. Source: [Global Innovation Fund completion assessment (PDF)](https://www.globalinnovation.fund/assets/uploads/PDF-Documents/Completion-Assessment/Simpa-Investment-Assessment-Executive-Summary.pdf); [WASHplus blog, 2013](https://washplusblog.fhi360.org/innovation/2013/07/simpa-innovates-with-pay-as-you-go-solar-in-india/index.html)

**Pump-as-a-service / water-as-a-service**
- Oorja (India) installs and maintains shared solar pumps for groups of about 15 farmers; farmers never buy a pump and pay per use plus a membership fee. Claims up to 50% energy-cost savings vs diesel [MARKETING]. Source: [Acumen](https://acumen.org/blog/oorja-is-scaling-a-decades-old-technology-for-the-first-time-in-india/)
- As of 31 Aug 2025: 256 solar pumps in 123 villages in 5 Uttar Pradesh districts, total 1,257.2 kWp, serving 3,700+ farmers and 4,700+ acres. Source: [IISD, "Beyond Irrigation: Solar Pumps in India", Feb 2026 (PDF)](https://www.iisd.org/system/files/2026-02/beyond-irrigation-solar-pumps-india.pdf); [energy-base project listing](https://energy-base.org/projects/solar-water-pump-irrigation-as-a-service-in-india)
- Claro Energy (India) piloted pay-per-use irrigation with a portable solar pump, booked by toll-free phone call and prepaid; PoweringAg (USAID) grant of US$500,000 from 2016, target 100 systems / 500 acres in Bihar and UP. I found no evidence of its current status. Source: [USAID document (PDF)](https://pdf.usaid.gov/pdf_docs/PA00Z2Q6.pdf); [WE4F profile](https://we4f.org/innovators/claro-energy-private-limited)
- SunCulture (Kenya) sells solar irrigation on PAYGo with repayments over up to 36 months; raised an $11 m syndicated debt facility (SunFunder, Triodos, NDF, AlphaMundi, AfDB) and later a $10 m receivables securitisation with Mirova. Shell Foundation says a 2016 pilot showed farmers repaid reliably "when the product worked and support systems were in place". No public 2024 repayment data found. Source: [NDF](https://www.ndf.int/newsroom/solar-irrigation-for-smallholder-farmers-scaled-up-with-debt-financing.html); [Africa Private Equity News](https://www.africaprivateequitynews.com/p/sunculture-closes-10m-solar-irrigation); [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/solutions-and-impact/connectivity-for-good/mobile-for-development/blog/pay-as-you-go-smart-assets-increase-farmers-climate-resilience/)

**Subsidy-led / cooperative distribution (Mongolia)**
- Mongolia's National 100,000 Solar Ger Electrification Program supplied 100,146 portable solar home systems by 2012, reaching about half the rural population and 70% of herders. The World Bank REAP project (2008-2012; $3.5 m IDA + $3.5 m GEF + $6.0 m Netherlands) used cost-sharing: herders bought systems at a subsidised price, through 50 private sales-and-service centres plus village administrators. Systems were designed to be packed and re-erected when the family moves. Source: [World Bank results, 2013](https://www.worldbank.org/en/results/2013/04/08/portable-solar-power-for-nomadic-herders); [World Bank feature, 2012](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2012/09/20/solar-power-lights-up-future-for-mongolian-herders); [World Bank OKR publication](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/1a1c501f-9d84-5b21-99b3-3285e7fa4a3f/full)
- Caveat: almost all sources on this program are World Bank's own materials; start date is given as 1999 in one and 2000 in another. Source: [REACH Alliance report](https://reachalliance.org/reports/providing-electricity-to-nomadic-herders-mongolias-renewable-energy-for-rural-access-project/)

**Pakistan: cash market, no PAYGo needed**
- Pakistan imported about 17 GW of solar panels in 2024 (Ember), roughly double 2023; demand came from households, small businesses and commercial users escaping high grid tariffs and outages, not a national programme. Source: [Profit / Pakistan Today, Apr 2025](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/04/14/pakistan-joins-global-shift-toward-renewable-energy-sources/); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2597108)
- Official installed capacity was reported at only 4,124 MW by Dec 2024 (from 321 MW in 2021), far below import volumes: imports are not the same as recorded installations (most rooftop/off-grid is unrecorded). Source: [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40357512)

**IoT remote monitoring + carbon revenue**
- Bboxx received Gold Standard certification (Sept 2024) for carbon projects in five African countries covering solar home systems, PAYGo clean cooking and solar water pumps; each project is projected to yield up to 60,000 credits/year. Projections only; no realised revenue published. Source: [Bboxx press release](https://bboxx.com/press-releases/bboxx-launches-multi-million-dollar-pan-african-carbon-credit-programme-for-multiple-clean-technologies); [SolarQuarter](https://solarquarter.com/2024/09/24/bboxx-earns-gold-standard-certification-for-clean-energy-in-five-african-countries-promoting-climate-action-and-community-resilience/)

### Inferences
- PAYGo's real product is credit plus a remote-lockable asset. A tracker adds cost and moving parts to the financed asset, which raises the loan size and the failure/service risk that CGAP and GOGLA identify as default drivers. A tracker therefore fits PAYGo badly unless it measurably lowers default (for example by keeping the pump useful in poor-sun weeks).
- The model where tracking value is easiest to capture is a shared, high-utilisation, pay-per-use asset (Oorja: ~4.9 kWp per pump, ~14.5 farmers per pump from 1,257.2 kWp / 256 pumps and 3,700 / 256). There, the operator owns the asset, does maintenance, and every extra pumping hour is sold water. Per-household tracking has none of these properties.
- M-KOPA's turnaround came from becoming a fintech (smartphones and loans), not from better solar hardware. The lesson for a hardware startup: the data the device produces (usage, uptime) may be worth more than the energy gain.
- Pakistan and much of India are now cash markets with cheap panels and huge installed bases of unmonitored small systems. A product selling "service to existing systems" (diagnosis, QA) addresses a larger market there than a product selling new financed systems.
- Mongolia shows subsidy plus private dealer networks can reach nomads; the hardware requirement was portability and robustness, not maximum yield.

### Gaps
- No public portfolio-at-risk (PAR) benchmark for 2023-2025 was found; GOGLA PAYGo PERFORM data would be needed.
- No reason for Simpa Networks' dissolution was found in public sources; the full GIF assessment might contain it.
- No India or Pakistan 2024 PAYGo share found; GOGLA country tables needed.
- No published SunCulture default/repayment rates for 2023-2025.
- No public evidence on solar cooperatives or panel rental models in Pakistan or Mongolia (not searched in depth).

## Q2. Where has tracking or "smart orientation" been tried for small systems, and why did it succeed or fail (LORENTZ ETATRACK, Zomeworks passive trackers, manual turn-the-panel trackers)?

### Takeaway
Small trackers exist mainly in solar pumping (LORENTZ ETATRACK claims "up to 40%" more energy) and as passive Freon-driven trackers (Zomeworks, now no longer taking orders). Independent field evidence is thin, but the best small study (Brazil, manual 3-step tracker on a pump) shows +16% water and 40 to 160 more pumping minutes per day. Adoption is low because moving parts need maintenance in places without technicians, and because panel prices have collapsed to about $0.08 to $0.22/W, so "add more panels" usually beats "add a tracker".

### Cited Findings

**Commercial pump trackers**
- LORENTZ ETATRACK active datasheets claim "up to 40% higher yield" from PV modules. No water-volume figure given. [MARKETING] Source: [LORENTZ ETATRACK active datasheet (ES)](https://partnernet.lorentz.de/files/lorentz_etatrackactive_pi_es.pdf); [ArchiExpo catalogue](https://pdf.archiexpo.com/pdf/lorentz/tracking-systems-solar-parks/108665-242920.html)
- A pumping guide repeats "extra 40% power or more" vs fixed racks. [MARKETING/secondary] Source: [Sinovoltaics](https://sinovoltaics.com/learning-center/water-pumping/solar-tracking-water-pump-systems-optimal-power-yield/)
- A 2022 Renewable Energy paper modelled a PV-powered pumping station: single-axis tracking increased production by 33.4% and cut operating costs by 28.9% vs fixed. [SIM] Source: [Univ. Zaragoza repository](https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/119980)

**Best small-scale field evidence: manual tracker on an irrigation pump (Brazil)**
- Grah Ponciano et al., Irriga 25(3), 2020: two identical PV modules each drove a separate pump, one static, one on a manual single-axis 3-position tracker (moved by hand 3 times per day). 34 days, June-Aug 2014, near Piracicaba (22.7°S). Tracked system: +50.87 pumping hours total, +66,135 L, about +16% average water volume (about 1,945 L/day), and 40 to 160 more pumping minutes per day, with the gain larger in the morning than the afternoon. Source: [Irriga paper (PDF), DOI 10.15809/irriga.2020v25n3p508-520](https://revistas.fca.unesp.br/index.php/irriga/article/download/4017/2699/17058)
- Same paper: pump turn-on rule was battery > 26.5 V and pyranometer > 350 W/m² (a start threshold). Overall solar-to-water efficiency was slightly lower for the tracker (4.55% vs 4.77%): the gain came from running longer, not converting better. Source: [Irriga paper (PDF)](https://revistas.fca.unesp.br/index.php/irriga/article/download/4017/2699/17058)
- An Egyptian study describes "critical irradiance" (minimum irradiance for the pump to run) and finds that for the same irradiance collected, systems pump the same water, just at different times of day. Source: [Scientific Papers Series Management (Romania), Art. 27 (PDF)](https://www.managementjournal.usamv.ro/pdf/vol.XV_1/Art27.pdf)
- An Iranian study measured positive-displacement pump start-up thresholds against a modelled 125 W/m²; measured thresholds on sunny days were lower than the model. Source: [Shahrood University thesis listing](https://shahroodut.ac.ir/en/thesis/thesis.php?thid=TJ746)

**Passive (thermo-hydraulic) trackers**
- Zomeworks stopped accepting new orders as of 1 January 2019; in very cold places the trackers are slow to follow the sun in the morning until the liquid warms. Source: [Greentech Renewables Q&A](https://greentechrenewables.com/comment/2019)
- An EU project (ZSW Passive Thermohydraulic Drive with V-trough concentration) targeted a 50 to 70% energy surplus [projected, not a measured result]. Source: [CORDIS JOU20139](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/JOU20139); [Archimedes paper (PDF)](https://builditsolar.com/Projects/PV/Archimedes-MSA.pdf)
- A Nigerian Freon-12 passive single-axis tracker reported about 30% average relative performance gain vs stationary (thesis, small-scale). Source: [Ahmadu Bello University repository](https://kubanni.abu.edu.ng/handle/123456789/2304)

**Seasonal / manual tilt adjustment (the "turn-the-panel" option)**
- Rajasthan simulation: seasonal tilt (58-60° winter, flatter summer) gives 8.89 to 9.02% more irradiance than fixed latitude tilt in Kota, Barmer, Jodhpur, Jaisalmer. [SIM] Source: [pv magazine India](https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/?p=12850)
- Bikaner utility plant: seasonal tilts raised incident radiation by 2.41% but annual capacity utilisation factor by only 0.26%. Source: [MDPI Sustainability 14(3):1444](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/14/3/1444/htm)
- Karachi study: daily tilt adjustment gives 6.85% more annual energy than fixed; daily beat monthly by 0.15%, seasonal by 1.24%, biannual by 6.83% (as reported in search summary; the near-equal biannual and fixed numbers look inconsistent and need checking against the paper). Source: search summary only; original not verified, see Gaps.
- Turkish techno-economic study (1 MW): manual tilt adjustment raises NPV by 12.4 to 14.9% vs fixed. Source: [ITU research portal](https://research.itu.edu.tr/en/publications/techno-economic-analysis-of-pv-systems-with-manually-adjustable-t/)
- Installer glossary claims 3 to 5% annual gain from 2 to 4 manual adjustments per year. [MARKETING] Source: [Heaven Green Energy glossary](https://www.heavengreenenergy.com/glossary/tilt-angle)

**Why adoption is low**
- Fixed systems have fewer parts and lower O&M; tracker motors and controls raise O&M spending. Battery-powered trackers sit outdoors charged by a tiny PV module, a reliability weak point. Source: [Solar Power World, 2017](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2017/01/trackers-can-make-break-solar-project/)
- Counter-evidence: low-cost prototypes exist. A Senegal off-grid prototype cost about $215 total ($72 regular system + $143 for the tracker). A tropical-country design reported up to 37% higher current. Long-term failure data: none found. Source: [Univ. of Navarra portal](https://portalcientifico.unav.edu/documentos/6652fc836004ca237d808221?lang=en); [APS March Meeting 2019 abstract](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/mar/2019/l47/5)
- Panel prices: Chinese TOPCon modules at about $0.083/W FOB in late May 2025. Source: [pv magazine, May 2025](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/05/30/china-module-prices-steady-amid-bearish-outlook/). In Pakistan, the Pakistan Solar Association put "bonafide" import prices at $0.17 to $0.22/W. Source: [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40261707/solar-panel-imports-rs73bn-illicit-fund-transfers-detected)
- Utility "smart tracking": Nextracker TrueCapture moves rows flatter under uniform cloud ("diffuse mode") and claims up to 6% total plant gain, with diffuse plus row-to-row modes up to 4%. [MARKETING] Source: [Nextracker datasheet (PDF)](https://info.nextracker.com/hubfs/Datasheets/nxt_nx_truecapture_datasheet_2022_v2.pdf); [TaiyangNews](https://taiyangnews.info/technology/unlocking-potential-of-smart-control-software)

### Inferences
- Break-even rule (my estimate): a tracker that adds X% energy competes with simply buying X% more panels. At Pakistan's $0.17 to $0.22/W, adding 30% to a 1 kW array costs about $50 to $65 in panels (plus mounting). A motorised tracker for a small array must cost less than that, and survive years with no maintenance, to win on energy alone. Most hobby or prototype trackers ($143 in the Senegal case) do not.
- Where tracking still wins: (a) space is limited (rooftop, ger, vehicle), (b) a load has a start threshold so morning/evening shape matters (pumps), (c) the asset is shared and highly used, so extra hours are sold. Oversizing panels also lifts morning power, but only proportionally; tracking lifts low-sun-angle power far more. This is the honest technical niche.
- The Brazil result is important because it was a manual, zero-electronics tracker and still gave +16% water. That suggests the product does not need motors: a cheap sensor that tells a farmer when to flip the panel captures much of the value.
- Zomeworks' discontinuation plus its cold-morning lag makes passive thermal trackers a poor fit for Mongolia (very cold mornings).

### Gaps
- No independent field measurement of LORENTZ ETATRACK water gains found; only vendor claims.
- No field data found on failure rates of small trackers in developing-country deployments.
- No sales or adoption figures for small trackers in South Asia or Mongolia.
- The Karachi daily-vs-fixed tilt paper was not retrieved; its numbers need verification.
- Stafford et al. (tracker accuracy field study including Zomeworks UTR-020) not retrieved.

## Q3. Which innovative product concepts (beyond "a tracker that follows the light") are strong or weak? Evidence of problem, evidence solution works, prior art, customer, who pays, unit economics, and hackathon demo.

### Takeaway
Strongest: (3) an install-diagnosis device for the huge base of unmonitored, often badly installed or under-rated small systems in Pakistan/India, and (2) a pump-hours booster, ideally as a "flip reminder" for manual tracking or sold to shared pay-per-use pump operators. Weak: (1) a shared tilt sensor (sun position is predictable, so an app does it without hardware), (5) nomad/vehicle trackers (no evidence of demand), and (6) data/insurance products as a standalone hackathon story (real, but the value lives in usage data and satellite indices, not a light sensor). (4) Haze/dust-aware logic is a good feature inside (3), not a product alone.

### Cited Findings

**Concept 3: install diagnosis (tilt, orientation, dirt, shading, under-rated or counterfeit panel)**
- Problem evidence, Pakistan: a LONGi representative says traders sell B- and C-grade panels as A-grade from known brands. [Industry claim] Source: [ARY News](https://arynews.tv/counterfeit-solar-panels-put-pakistans-solar-ambitions-in-jeopardy)
- Installer guide: inflated wattage (540/550 W panels delivering much less) is "one of the most common issues", fake "German Cell" stickers on rejected panels; recommends asking for flash test / IV-curve data. [Seller with commercial interest] Source: [DSG Energy](https://dsgenergy.com.pk/5-ways-to-identify-fake-solar-panels/)
- Pakistan's FBR / customs audit found Rs 38 bn over-invoicing across 2,718 import declarations, and FBR action against companies, but this is money-laundering, not quality testing. Source: [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40261707/solar-panel-imports-rs73bn-illicit-fund-transfers-detected); [Bol News](https://www.bolnews.com/latest-news/fbr-slaps-rs111b-fines-on-fake-solar-panel-firms)
- Problem evidence, India: CEEW field visits to Delhi rooftop systems list dust, bird droppings, construction dust, self-shading, tree growth, water tanks, laundry, and uneven module orientation (the worst module limits the string) as causes of low output. Source: [CEEW report (PDF)](https://www.ceew.in/sites/default/files/CEEW-research-achieving-optimum-energy-generation-from-rooftop-solar-systems.pdf)
- A 500 kWp rooftop plant in Guwahati had a performance ratio of only 0.60 (101.57 MWh/yr). Source: [Khalifa University repository](https://khazna.ku.ac.ae/en/publications/performance-evaluation-of-on-grid-rooftop-solar-pv-system-in-gmch/)
- A Pakistan study reported dust deposition caused a 21.7% output drop in June (methods not checked). Source: [Frontiers in Energy Research 2023 (PDF)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/energy-research/articles/10.3389/fenrg.2023.1293615/pdf)
- Standard to measure against: IEC 61724 (performance ratio / monitoring). Source: [Khalifa University repository](https://khazna.ku.ac.ae/en/publications/performance-evaluation-of-on-grid-rooftop-solar-pv-system-in-gmch/)
- Market: about 17 GW of panels imported into Pakistan in 2024, mostly unrecorded small systems. Source: [Profit / Pakistan Today](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/04/14/pakistan-joins-global-shift-toward-renewable-energy-sources/)

**Concept 4: haze/smog- and dust-aware orientation (stow, clean alert, diffuse mode)**
- Delhi: soiling cuts output about 0.3 to 0.6% per day; air-pollution yield loss about 6% per year (conference paper summarised by Renewable Watch). Source: [Renewable Watch, 2018](https://renewablewatch.in/2018/12/22/soiling-analysis/)
- IIT Bombay (Mumbai): soiling about 0.4%/day; weekly cleaning limits annual loss to about 1%, monthly to about 4%. Source: [pv magazine India long read](https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/?p=4339)
- IIT Delhi (Environmental Research Letters 2022): India lost 29% of utilisable solar potential 2001-2018 from pollution (horizontal irradiance, not plant output). Source: [LiCAS News](https://www.licas.news/2022/04/13/air-pollution-dims-indias-solar-energy-potential/); [The Wire Science](https://science.thewire.in/?p=466336)
- Industry anecdote: Delhi rooftops producing up to 30% less than the same design 40 km away. [Anecdotal] Source: [Arab News](https://arabnews.pk/node/1155041)
- Prior art, diffuse mode: Nextracker flattens rows under uniform cloud; claims up to 4% (with row-to-row). [MARKETING] Source: [TaiyangNews](https://taiyangnews.info/technology/nextrackers-truecapture-solar-tracker-technology-with-intelligent-control-systems)
- Prior art, soiling sensing: Kipp & Zonen DustIQ optical soiling sensor (±1% accuracy in 90-100% range), price not published, aimed at utility plants. Source: [Kipp & Zonen](https://www.kippzonen.com/products/dustiq-soiling-monitoring-system)

**Concept 2: pump-hours tracker (non-linear value from start threshold)**
- Evidence: Brazil manual 3-step tracker, +16% water, +40 to 160 min/day pumping, gain biggest in mornings, pump start threshold 350 W/m². Source: [Irriga 2020 (PDF)](https://revistas.fca.unesp.br/index.php/irriga/article/download/4017/2699/17058)
- Counter-evidence (same water at same collected irradiance, only timing shifts). Source: [Management journal Art. 27 (PDF)](https://www.managementjournal.usamv.ro/pdf/vol.XV_1/Art27.pdf)
- Externality risk in Pakistan: Punjab water authority documents show the water table below the 60 ft "critical" level across 6.6% of Punjab in 2024, coinciding with a solar tubewell boom; farmers say solar lets them irrigate rice far more often. The power minister disputes the link; the Punjab irrigation minister says solar is affecting the water table. Source: [Profit / Reuters syndication](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=213919); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2617737/pakistan)
- Tubewell-only irrigation in Punjab doubled from 4.89 m to 9.98 m acres (2010-2024). Source: [Gallup Pakistan Digital Analytics, Jan 2026](https://galluppakistandigitalanalytics.com/2026/01/28/tube-wells-from-solution-to-new-challenge-in-pakistans-agriculture/)

**Concept 1: one shared "smart sensor" / app for best manual angle**
- Value evidence: seasonal tilt about 8 to 9% [SIM, Rajasthan]; 2.41% incident / 0.26% CUF in a real Bikaner plant; 3 to 5% installer claim. Sources: [pv magazine India](https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/?p=12850); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/14/3/1444/htm); [Heaven Green Energy](https://www.heavengreenenergy.com/glossary/tilt-angle)

**Concept 5: mobile tracker for nomads / vehicles**
- Mongolia: 100,000+ portable systems already deployed to herders, designed to move with the ger. Source: [World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/results/2013/04/08/portable-solar-power-for-nomadic-herders)

### Inferences (concept-by-concept verdicts, customers, unit economics, demos)

All prices below are my rough estimates unless tied to a cited number; treat as order-of-magnitude.

**Concept 3: "Solar doctor" install-diagnosis device. Verdict: STRONGEST.**
- Problem is large and documented (17 GW of mostly unmonitored Pakistani installs; CEEW list of field faults; widespread under-rated panel complaints).
- Customer and payer: (1) installers and dealers who want a "commissioning certificate" to prove quality and win trust, (2) PAYGo lenders / banks financing rooftop systems who want proof the asset performs, (3) homeowners via a one-off paid visit. Best wedge: installers (B2B tool, one device serves hundreds of sites).
- How it works: a clip-on reference cell + IMU (tilt/heading) + current/voltage measurement compares what the panel *should* produce at this sun position and irradiance vs what it *does* produce. Outputs: wrong tilt, wrong azimuth, soiling %, shading pattern (by sweeping/time), and "panel produces X% of nameplate" (counterfeit flag).
- Unit economics (estimate): hardware BOM for a sensor kit is tens of dollars; sold as a tool or rented per inspection. Value to customer: catching a 20 to 30% shortfall on a 5 kW system is worth far more than the inspection fee.
- Prior art and honesty: professional IV-curve tracers and soiling sensors exist (DustIQ) but target utility plants and are priced for them (DustIQ price unpublished). The novelty is low cost and a "fix list" for non-experts, not the physics.
- Hackathon demo: lamp as "sun", mini solar cell on a servo pan-tilt, INA219 measures power. (a) Set panel to a wrong angle: device reports "tilt off by N°, losing X%" and the servo corrects it, power rises live. (b) Cover part of the cell with a translucent film ("dust"): device flags soiling %. (c) Swap in a smaller "fake" cell with the same label: device flags "produces 60% of rated". The LDR array gives the reference light level. This is a clean, believable demo.

**Concept 2: pump-hours booster. Verdict: STRONG IN A NARROW NICHE; reframe it.**
- Evidence it works: Brazil field data (+16% water, +40 to 160 min/day, morning gain dominant) and the documented existence of pump start thresholds.
- Weakness: oversizing the panel array is cheap and also raises morning power; and in Punjab, more pumping hours may worsen groundwater depletion, a bad story to pitch.
- Better framing options: (a) "Flip reminder": no motor; a light sensor tells the farmer when to turn the panel (3 positions/day), capturing the Brazil manual-tracking gain for a few dollars. (b) Sell motorised tracking only to shared pay-per-use pump operators (Oorja-type), where extra hours are sold water and the operator does maintenance. (c) Pitch "same water with fewer panels" or "pump earlier in the cool morning" (less evaporation) rather than "more water".
- Unit economics (estimate): Oorja-scale pump about 4.9 kWp. A +16% energy-equivalent costs about 785 W of extra panels, roughly $130 to $175 at Pakistan's $0.17 to $0.22/W. A tracker for that array must cost less than this and last years. A no-motor flip reminder easily beats that.
- Hackathon demo: a small DC motor/pump (or an LED load) with a firmware start threshold (for example "run only if power > X mW"). Fixed cell vs tracked cell under a lamp that sweeps from "morning" to "evening": show the tracked side crosses the threshold earlier and runs longer, logging "minutes of pumping gained". This directly demonstrates the non-linear value.

**Concept 4: haze/dust-aware orientation. Verdict: GOOD FEATURE, WEAK STANDALONE.**
- Evidence of problem is strong in Delhi/Lahore-type air (soiling 0.3 to 0.6%/day, ~6% annual pollution loss).
- Diffuse mode is proven at utility scale (vendor claims up to ~4%), but on a small tracker the extra gain is a few percent on a few hundred watts: small money.
- Best use: inside Concept 3 as a "clean now" alert (cleaning is cheap, gains are large: weekly cleaning limits loss to ~1% vs ~4% monthly in Mumbai data) and inside any tracker as "go flat when the sky is uniform".
- Demo: diffuser sheet over the lamp ("haze") makes LDRs read nearly equal; firmware detects low contrast and moves the cell flat; log power in tracking vs flat mode.

**Concept 1: shared tilt sensor / app. Verdict: WEAK as hardware.**
- The sun's position is deterministic from date and location, so the best seasonal angle can be computed by a free app; no sensor needed. The measured gain (2.41% incident, 0.26% CUF in Bikaner) is small for most real systems.
- Salvage: fold into Concept 3 as an SMS/app reminder ("tilt your panel to 55° this week") and add value via local measurement of haze/diffuse fraction, which an app cannot know.

**Concept 5: mobile tracker for nomads / vehicles. Verdict: WEAK.**
- No evidence found of demand; herders can turn a portable panel by hand, robustness and portability mattered more in the Mongolian programme, cold mornings defeat passive trackers, and vehicle roofs have wind and vibration issues. Mark as not worth building.

**Concept 6: data / insurance / carbon. Verdict: REAL BUSINESS, WEAK HACKATHON STORY.**
- PAYGo lenders and carbon developers do pay for verified data, but the data they need is usage and uptime (from the battery/controller), and weather-index insurance uses satellite data. A local light sensor adds a "ground truth" irradiance layer at best.
- Possible angle: "performance-verified asset" data for lenders (pairs with Concept 3). Insurance product affordability was questioned even in the Pula pilot.

**Additional concepts found**
- (7) Shared-asset tracker for pay-per-use pumps: tracker value scales with utilisation; Oorja has ~14.5 farmers per pump. Customer: the operator. Strong if hardware is rugged.
- (8) Commissioning certificate for PAYGo / bank rooftop loans: a measurable "this install meets spec" record reduces lender risk; ties Concept 3 to the finance models in Q1.
- (9) Morning-pumping scheduler: shift pumping into hours of lower evaporation and lower water-table stress messaging; addresses the groundwater critique instead of ignoring it.
