# Pakistan: Solar and Energy Pain Points a Small Smart Sun-Tracking / Light-Sensing Device Could Address (2020-2026)

Research date: 2026-10-10. Source-type tags: [Official] = government/regulator data, [Peer] = peer-reviewed, [Think tank] = NGO/analyst report, [News] = journalism, [Vendor] = installer/seller/SEO page (weak). Every number below carries its source. Where sources disagree, both are shown.

## 1. The distributed solar boom: imports, net metering, grid defection, load shedding, tariffs, who installs

### Takeaway
Pakistan imported roughly 40-54 GW of mostly Chinese panels from FY2020 to early 2026, but only about 6 GW is registered under net metering; the rest is "hidden" off-grid or behind-the-meter solar, mostly installed by households, shops and farmers with no monitoring. The drivers are a ~110% grid tariff rise (FY2022 to FY2025) and 8-10+ hours/day of load shedding in the worst-served regions; in Feb 2026 NEPRA cut the export rate for new rooftop users by more than half, which makes self-consumption (using your own solar when it is produced) the economic priority.

### Cited Findings
**Imports**
- Pakistan imported 17 GW of solar panels in 2024, more than double 2023 (Ember, Global Electricity Review 2025). Source: [pv magazine / REN21 / Ember via search](https://www.ren21.net/gsr-2025/snapshots/pk/) [Think tank]
- Fiscal-year view: 16 GW imported in FY2024, a 227% jump from 4.9 GW in FY2023 (Renewables First). Source: [Renewables First, Pakistan Electricity Review 2025 (PDF)](https://uploads.renewablesfirst.org/Pakistan_Electricity_Review_2025_89f0b613d6.pdf) [Think tank]
- First-half 2025 imports nearly equalled all of 2024 (2024 estimate: 16.62 GW). Source: [Wikipedia, Solar power in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_power_in_Pakistan) [Aggregator, treat as secondary]
- A Gulf News op-ed claims 22 GW for 2024; not corroborated. Source: [Gulf News op-ed](https://gulfnews.com/opinion/op-eds/pakistans-great-solar-escape-a-revolution-from-below-1.500272703) [Opinion]
- Cumulative: ~36 GW (Renewables First, 2025), ~50 GW as of Aug 2025 (TransitionZero), ~54 GW by end-March 2026 (Renewables First, cited by pv magazine Oct 2026). Sources: [Renewables First PDF](https://uploads.renewablesfirst.org/Pakistan_Electricity_Review_2025_89f0b613d6.pdf); [TransitionZero](https://www.transitionzero.org/shedding-light-on-pakistans-distributed-solar-revolution) (page returned HTTP 429, figure taken from search snippet only); [pv magazine, 2026-10-09](http://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/10/09/prices-drive-pakistans-metered-solar-outages-drive-hidden-uptake/) [News citing think tank]
- Panel import price fell from $0.35/W (2017) to about $0.08/W (2025); imports were duty and sales-tax free until 2025, when a 10% GST was imposed on new imports. Source: [Payne Institute, Colorado School of Mines](https://payneinstitute.mines.edu/pakistans-solar-surge/) [Think tank]
- Pakistan described as world's third-largest panel importer (Feb 2026). Source: [The Friday Times, 2026-02-18](https://www.thefridaytimes.com/18-Feb-2026/pakistan-s-solar-boom-third-largest-importer-faces-grid-strain-fraud-waste-challenges) [News]
- Battery imports grew from 1.4 GWh (2024) to 4.6 GWh (2025). Source: [pv magazine, 2026-10-09](http://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/10/09/prices-drive-pakistans-metered-solar-outages-drive-hidden-uptake/) [News]

**Net metering (registered, "visible" solar)**
- Net-metered additions: 583 MW in FY2022-23, 1,181 MW in FY2023-24 (NEPRA data). Source: [REN21 GSR 2025 Pakistan snapshot](https://www.ren21.net/gsr-2025/snapshots/pk/) [Think tank citing Official]
- 5.3 GW net-metering capacity by end-April 2025. Source: [pv magazine, 2025-06-02](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/06/02/pakistans-net-metering-capacity-hits-5-3-gw/) [News]
- 6.1 GW across 378,339 net-metered consumers as of 30 June 2025; ~85% sits in six DISCOs (distribution companies): LESCO 1.3 GW, IESCO 0.9, K-Electric 0.9, MEPCO 0.8, FESCO 0.7, GEPCO 0.6. Source: Islam, Rosenow & Amirali, "Pakistan's solar boom: Visible and invisible grid defection in a high-cost, low-reliability power system", Energy Research & Social Science (2026), DOI 10.1016/j.erss.2026.105016, [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214629626004871), summarised by [pv magazine, 2026-10-09](http://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/10/09/prices-drive-pakistans-metered-solar-outages-drive-hidden-uptake/) [Peer]

**Grid defection (customers leaving or bypassing the grid)**
- About 40 GW of modules imported since FY2020 versus ~6 GW registered; the authors treat the gap as a lower bound on "hidden" off-grid/behind-the-meter capacity, and state it is an inference, not a measurement. Source: [Islam et al. 2026, ERSS](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214629626004871) [Peer]
- Agricultural grid consumption fell 31% year-on-year in FY2025 as farmers adopted solar tubewells. Source: same [Peer]
- Renewables First: ~51 GW operational by March 2026; distributed solar generated ~51 TWh in FY2025. Source: [pv magazine, 2026-10-09](http://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/10/09/prices-drive-pakistans-metered-solar-outages-drive-hidden-uptake/) [News citing think tank]
- LESCO (Lahore) midday grid demand fell from 2.2 GW (2024) to 765 MW (2026), per Pakistan Solar Association. Source: same [News]
- Ember: solar generation rose from 2.6 TWh (2019) to 36.6 TWh (2025), 22.5% of generation; in Q1 2026 solar supplied 12.1 of 37.5 TWh (32.3%), ahead of coal, gas, nuclear and hydro for the first time. Source: same, and [Islam et al. 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214629626004871) [Peer citing Ember]
- Capacity payments rose 46% to PKR 1.9 trillion in FY2024, recovered through per-unit tariffs, creating a "utility death spiral" risk (fewer units to spread fixed costs over). Source: [pv magazine summary of Islam et al.](http://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/10/09/prices-drive-pakistans-metered-solar-outages-drive-hidden-uptake/) [Peer]
- Utilities report reverse power flows, transformer burnouts and overloaded feeders from distributed solar. Source: [Payne Institute](https://payneinstitute.mines.edu/pakistans-solar-surge/) [Think tank]

**Tariffs**
- National average base tariff rose ~110%, from PKR 16.91/kWh (FY2022) to PKR 35.50/kWh (FY2025); top residential slab PKR 48.84/kWh. Rooftop payback 2-4 years when offsetting the most expensive units. Source: [Islam et al. 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214629626004871) [Peer]

**Net-metering policy change (critical for product design)**
- Dec 2025: NEPRA proposed gross metering for new rooftop users at Rs11.30/unit buyback, 5-year contracts. Source: [Profit by Pakistan Today, 2025-12-22](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/12/22/nepra-proposes-shift-from-net-to-gross-metering-for-new-rooftop-solar-consumers) [News]
- Feb 2026: NEPRA notified Prosumer Regulations moving new connections to net billing; buyback cut by Rs17.19 to Rs8.13/unit (Profit), other reports say ~Rs11/unit (pv magazine/Islam et al.). Contract term cut from 7 to 5 years. Existing users keep old rate (~Rs25-26/unit) until expiry. Sources: [Profit, 2026-02-10](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2026/02/10/pakistans-power-regulator-ends-net-metering-for-solar-consumers-shifts-to-net-billing-model/); [Express Tribune, draft amendment](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2592910/nepra-floats-draft-amendment-rolling-back-net-metering-policy-changes-for-existing-users) [News]. Conflict: Rs8.13 vs ~Rs11 per unit; not resolved.

**Load shedding (scheduled power cuts)**
- Self-reported FY2024-25 averages: QESCO (Balochistan) 10.25 h/day, HESCO (Hyderabad) 8 h, PESCO (KP) 5.5 h, K-Electric 5.2 h. NEPRA monitoring says actual load shedding exceeds 8-10 h/day in PESCO, QESCO, SEPCO, HESCO. T&D losses: SEPCO 39.2%, QESCO 38.4%, PESCO 37.2%; QESCO bill recovery 38.7%. Source: [Islam et al. 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214629626004871) [Peer citing Official NEPRA]
- Government announced ~2.25 h/day peak-time cuts (5 pm to 1 am) to limit fuel costs. Source: [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1991664); [The News](https://www.thenews.pk/story/1410072-govt-announces-over-two-hours-peak-time-loadshedding-power-prices-may-rise) [News]
- Oct 2026: shortfall above 4,000 MW, 3-4 h load shedding in Lahore and other cities (unnamed LESCO sources). Source: [Daily Pakistan, 2026-10-05](https://en.dailypakistan.com.pk/05-Oct-2026/load-shedding-grips-lahore-other-cities-as-electricity-shortfall-tops-4000mw) [News, weak sourcing]
- Officials: daytime demand drops due to solar, but load shedding intensifies in the evening. Source: [Express Tribune, deficit 6,500 MW](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2603049/massive-outages-as-deficit-hits-6500mw) [News]
- Islam et al. find metered adoption is driven mainly by prices, while hidden uptake is driven by outages. Source: [pv magazine, 2026-10-09](http://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/10/09/prices-drive-pakistans-metered-solar-outages-drive-hidden-uptake/) [Peer]

### Inferences
- The installed base is huge (tens of GW, millions of small systems) and almost entirely unmonitored and fixed-tilt. A cheap per-site sensor/monitor has a large addressable base; a tracker has a much smaller one (see section 6).
- After the Feb 2026 net-billing change, every kWh self-consumed is worth ~Rs35-48 while every kWh exported is worth ~Rs8-11. Shifting generation toward morning/evening (which tracking does) and timing loads to solar output are both worth more than before.
- Evening load shedding plus falling midday grid demand means the evening "shoulder" hours are where value is; trackers raise early-morning and late-afternoon output most, so the timing aligns with the pain.

- Could not fetch TransitionZero page (HTTP 429); its 50 GW figure is from a search snippet.
- Exact current buyback rate for new users (Rs8.13 vs Rs11) and whether the 2026 draft rollback amendments were finalised are unverified.
- No household/commercial/agricultural split of hidden capacity found; no official count of off-grid systems exists.
- No data found on how rooftop systems are maintained (cleaning frequency, O&M contracts).

## 2. Agriculture: solar tubewells, provincial schemes, groundwater, pump start and pumping hours

### Takeaway
Roughly 650,000 tubewells are now solar-powered (Reuters estimate, Oct 2025), driven by farmers ditching diesel and grid power; Balochistan is converting ~27,000-28,000 subsidised electric tubewells for Rs55 bn, and Punjab runs a subsidised scheme for 7,000-8,000 more. Because sunlight is free, pumping is effectively unmetered and groundwater is falling, so a device that measures and controls pump energy and water could matter more than one that maximises it.

### Cited Findings
- About 400,000 tubewells switched from grid to solar, plus ~250,000 more added since 2023, so ~650,000 total (energy economist Ammar Habib, adviser to the power minister; Reuters calculation). No official count exists because tubewells need no registration. Agricultural grid consumption projected to fall 45% over three years to 2025. Source: [Reuters via Dawn, 2025-10-02](https://www.dawn.com/news/1946104) [News]
- Punjab water authority documents: area with water table below 60 ft (the provincial critical level) was 6.6% of Punjab in 2024, up ~25% from 2020; area beyond 80 ft more than doubled 2020-2024. Documents did not identify a cause. Rice area rose 30% 2023-2025 (USDA). Farmers report "pulse irrigation" several times a day. Power minister Awais Leghari called groundwater blame a "misconception". Source: [Reuters via Dawn, 2025-10-02](https://www.dawn.com/news/1946104) [News citing Official]
- A PCRWR (Pakistan Council of Research in Water Resources) report warned against converting tubewells in water-scarce areas, producing a "negative list" (including 8 Punjab districts) ineligible for solar tubewells. Source: [Dawn, "Jury still out on mega-solarisation of tube wells"](https://www.dawn.com/news/1784354) [News]
- Balochistan: ECC approved solarising 27,000 agricultural tubewells at ~Rs55 bn (federal 70% / provincial 30%), after a PM directive of 2 July 2024; farmers get Rs2 million per tubewell for disconnecting from grid; aim is to cut a ~PKR 23 bn/yr subsidy burden; PM claimed ~Rs90 bn/yr savings (unaudited projection). Sources: [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2544220/ecc-approves-solarisation-of-27000-tube-wells); [Pakistan Today, 2025-05-07](https://www.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/05/07/ecc-approves-solarisation-of-27000-tube-wells-in-balochistan); [Dawn, accord for 28,000 tubewells](https://www.dawn.com/news/1844774) [News citing Official]
- Rs14 bn approved in March 2025; Balochistan had disbursed Rs12.4 bn by 1 March. Source: [Profit, 2025-03-14](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/03/14/govt-approves-rs14bn-for-solar-powered-tubewells-in-balochistan) [News]
- Dunya News reports Balochistan shifted more than 27,000 tubewells to solar in 2025. Source: [Dunya News](https://dunyanews.tv/en/Pakistan/925429-balochistan-shifts-more-than-27,000-agricultural-tubewells-to-solar-system-in-2025) [News]
- Federal Water Resources Ministry opposed Balochistan solarisation because of continuous water-table depletion; its secretary said there is "no engineering solution" and installation should halt. Source: [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40324893/ministry-opposes-solarisation-of-balochistan-tubewells) [News]
- Balochistan water tables reported falling 30-40 ft/year (academic study) and >5 m/year in some basins (World Bank, cited by FairPlanet). Sources: [Academia.edu study on Balochistan tubewell solarisation](https://www.academia.edu/86703986/Solarization_of_electric_tube_wells_for_agriculture_in_Balochistan_Economic_and_environmental_viability) [Peer, uncertain venue]; [FairPlanet](https://www.fairplanet.org/story/pakistan-food-drought-flood-solar-powered-tube-wells/) [News citing World Bank]
- In Naseerabad/Mastung, a farmer estimates 70-80 tubewells went dry in seven months as farmers drilled deeper; depletion predates solar. Source: [Express Tribune, "The High Cost of Balochistan's Solar Transition"](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2631555/the-high-cost-of-balochistans-solar-transition) [News]
- PIDE proposes structuring tubewell solarisation as a social/economic/environmental "win-win" in Balochistan. Source: [PIDE](https://pide.org.pk/research/turning-solarization-of-agricultural-tubewells-into-a-social-economic-and-environmental-win-win-in-balochistan/) [Think tank]
- Punjab scheme: unofficial sites cite 8,000 tubewells at up to 80% subsidy (PKR 0.5-1.0 m per 10-20 HP system), or 7,000 tubewells with Rs9,887.42 m total allocation (Rs2,500 m in 2024-25). Sources: [mmdcgov.com](https://mmdcgov.com/punjab-solar-tubewell-scheme-2026-80-subsidy/); [Agro Master](https://agromaster.pk/punjab-govt-solar-tube-well-scheme/) [Vendor, unverified]
- Pump start physics: a VFD (variable frequency drive, the box that turns panel DC into variable-speed AC for the motor) lets the pump run slowly at low morning sun and ramp up; without it an AC pump starts at full speed or not at all. The start threshold is a configurable minimum DC voltage, suggested at 70-75% of array open-circuit voltage. Source: [Eneronix VFD guide](https://eneronix.com/vfd-for-solar-pumps-and-how-to-select-the-right-one/) [Vendor]
- Pakistani vendors describe soft running early morning/late afternoon, full power at midday; one installer targets a minimum 6 h/day irrigation. Sources: [Pakistan Solar Traders, pump VFD](https://pakistansolartraders.com/solar-pump-inverter-vfd/); [Solar Citizen, tubewell price](https://www.solarcitizen.com.pk/solar-tubewell-price-pakistan/) [Vendor]
- Example patent start/stop voltages ~115 V / 90 V (illustrative only). Source: [USPTO patent 6922348](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6922348) [Patent]

### Inferences
- Solar pumps are the single strongest fit for tracking: pump output is roughly proportional to array power, the pump only runs above a threshold, and a tracker widens the "above threshold" window at both ends of the day. Commercial practice elsewhere uses trackers on solar pumps for this reason (general knowledge, not Pakistan-sourced).
- But a tracker on a tubewell also increases pumping in a country where groundwater depletion is the main policy objection. A pitch that pairs tracking with metering (energy and estimated water pumped, pump-hours logs, scheduled shut-off) answers the PCRWR/Water Ministry concern instead of worsening it.
- The ~650,000 solar tubewells and Rs55 bn Balochistan programme are a large, mostly unmonitored installed base with government buyers who need verification data (did the grid disconnection happen, how many hours does the pump run).

### Gaps
- No source found on how many diesel pumps specifically were converted (only "ditching diesel" narrative).
- No Pakistan-specific measurement of minimum irradiance (W/m2) for pump start or of pumping hours lost in morning/evening.
- No official Punjab scheme document retrieved; figures are from unofficial sites.
- No IWMI report on Pakistan solar irrigation was retrieved in this pass.

## 3. Environment: heat, dust/soiling, smog, fog, floods

### Takeaway
Lahore has one of the highest measured soiling rates in South Asia (~0.8%/day loss for a 30° panel in Oct-Jan), and a 100-day uncleaned horizontal panel lost 26.2% of energy; a regional study estimates air pollution plus deposited dust cut South Asian PV output by 17-25%. Heat adds roughly 0.4-0.5%/°C loss. Pakistan-specific numbers for smog-only dimming and fog are missing.

### Cited Findings
**Dust and soiling**
- Ullah et al. (2020), Renewable Energy vol 150, pp 456-468: 120-day rooftop study in Lahore (Oct-Jan), several tilts, mono vs bifacial; soiling rate ~0.8%/day for 30° tilt, "among one of the highest" reported for urban sites in South Asia and the Gulf; proposes tilt-dependent cleaning schedules. Sources: [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0960148119319627); [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/renene/v150y2020icp456-468.html) [Peer]
- "Investigation of optimal tilt angles and effects of soiling on PV energy production in Pakistan" (Renewable Energy, 2019): over 100 days, dust caused 26.2% energy loss on a horizontal panel vs 13.5% at vertical tilt (another summary says 0° vs 30°, during rainy season); soiling loss modelled up to 10% (light) to 40% (heavy); four tilt adjustments per year give ~6.6% more yearly energy than fixed tilt. Source: [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0960148119302794) (full text returned HTTP 403; figures from search summary) [Peer]
- Taxila study: mono- and poly-crystalline module output fell 20% and 16% after 11 weeks outdoors. Source: [Power loss due to soiling on solar panel: A review, RSER](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1364032116000745) [Peer]
- Review: monthly PV efficiency deterioration from dust in Pakistan reported from 16% up to 57%. Source: [Dust impact on solar PV performance review, Energy Reports 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352484724003792) [Peer]
- Global context: annual soiling losses typically 1.5-6.2% depending on site. Source: [Soiling loss review, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772783123000444) [Peer]
- Vendor claims: 15-30% loss from dust/smog/bird droppings; 30-50% after a month uncleaned in Sindh, South Punjab, Balochistan. Source: [paksolarinsights.pk](https://paksolarinsights.pk/blog/clean-solar-panels-safely) [Vendor, unverified]

**Smog / air pollution (atmospheric dimming)**
- Bergin et al. (2017), Environ. Sci. Technol. Letters: solar production reduced ~17-25% across studied regions (incl. South Asia/Northern India), with roughly equal contributions from airborne PM and PM deposited on panels. Source: [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.estlett.7b00197) [Peer]
- China comparison: aerosols as important as clouds in winter; ~20-25% annual reduction in northern/eastern China. Source: [PNAS, Sweerts et al.](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1711462114) [Peer]
- Lahore haze episodes dominated by biomass burning and urban/industrial aerosols. Source: [ScienceDirect 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2590162124000431) [Peer]

**Heat**
- Crystalline silicon loses ~0.4-0.5% efficiency per °C rise in module temperature. Source: [RSER 2024 review, environmental factors](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1364032124007998) [Peer]
- Karachi data 2014-2018: generation peaks March-April and declines July-August due to heat; wind changes efficiency/PR by 1-3%. Source: [Academia.edu PDF](https://www.academia.edu/43686677/Effect_of_temperature_and_wind_on_PV_Modules_efficiency) [Peer, uncertain venue]
- North India rooftop (nearby climate): peak cell temperature ~64 °C with max daily power loss 15.6%. Source: [Scientific Reports 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-25846-z) [Peer]

**Floods, hail, waste**
- 2022 floods: 1,739 dead, over 2.1 m homeless; in rural Balochistan only those who kept panels out of floodwater had power. Source: [Wikipedia, 2022 Pakistan floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Pakistan_floods) [Aggregator]; [Loksujag](https://loksujag.com/story/solar-panels-batteries-recycling-pakistan-opportunity-challenge-eng) [News]
- April hailstorm in Punjab/Islamabad destroyed many rooftop panels, which became e-waste; recycling is informal (scrap dealers separate glass and aluminium). Source: [Loksujag](https://loksujag.com/story/solar-panels-batteries-recycling-pakistan-opportunity-challenge-eng) [News]

### Inferences
- Soiling is the most quantified, most fixable loss. A light sensor comparing a reference cell (or the panel's own output) with expected clear-sky output can flag "clean now", which is directly useful given Lahore's ~0.8%/day rate and scarce water (clean only when the loss justifies it).
- Under heavy smog, light becomes mostly diffuse; trackers gain little in diffuse light (pointing at the sun matters less when light comes from the whole sky). A light-sensing tracker that detects diffuse conditions and parks flat (horizontal sees the most sky) is a technically honest feature; a tracker that blindly follows the sun in Lahore's winter adds little.
- A tracker can also stow panels (tilt to near-vertical) during hail or dust storms, reducing damage and soiling. The 2019 study's 26.2% (flat) vs 13.5% (steep) soiling loss supports steeper night-time stow.
- Heat: trackers do not reduce module temperature much; this is not a strong tracker use case. Temperature sensing for monitoring is cheap though.

### Gaps
- No Lahore- or Pakistan-specific measurement isolating smog dimming from panel soiling, and no % diffuse light figure for Lahore winter.
- No data on winter fog impact on PV in Punjab.
- No Thar- or Balochistan-specific soiling rate found.
- No Sindh field study at 45-50 °C ambient with measured derating.
- No data on volume of water used for panel cleaning.

## 4. Poor-quality / counterfeit panels, bad installation angle, technician shortage

### Takeaway
Counterfeit and mislabelled panels are documented (customs fined fake importers Rs111 bn; LONGi warned of B/C-grade panels sold as A-grade), and buyers have no cheap way to check real output. Data on tilt/orientation errors and technician shortages in small installs is weak; the best peer-reviewed number is that seasonal tilt changes add ~6.6% over fixed tilt.

### Cited Findings
- Pakistan Customs imposed Rs111 bn penalty on companies (existing only on paper) for fake solar panel shipments. Source: [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2559005/fake-solar-panel-importers-fined-rs111b) [News]. Note: this case was widely reported as over-invoicing/money laundering via panel imports; "fake" here may mean fraudulent shipments rather than counterfeit panels (not verified).
- LONGi official: traders flood the market with B- and C-grade panels passed off as A-grade (article ~2024). Source: [ARY News](https://arynews.tv/counterfeit-solar-panels-put-pakistans-solar-ambitions-in-jeopardy) [News]
- Rejected/substandard panels reportedly enter via Chaman, Torkham and Afghan border and are relabelled. Source: [Feroze Power](https://www.ferozepower.com/fake-solar-panels-and-dummies/) [Vendor]
- Inflated wattage (e.g. "540-550 W" panels delivering far less) described as one of the most common issues; flash-test reports recommended. Sources: [DSG Energy](https://dsgenergy.com.pk/5-ways-to-identify-fake-solar-panels/); [hocmaterial](https://hocmaterial.com/post/10-most-common-solar-scams-in-pakistan) [Vendor]
- No fully enforced regulator verifies panel quality. Source: [Feroze Power](https://www.ferozepower.com/fake-solar-panels-and-dummies/) [Vendor]
- The Friday Times (Feb 2026) frames the boom's problems as grid strain, fraud and waste. Source: [The Friday Times](https://www.thefridaytimes.com/18-Feb-2026/pakistan-s-solar-boom-third-largest-importer-faces-grid-strain-fraud-waste-challenges) [News]
- Tilt: four tilt adjustments per year add ~6.6% yearly energy vs fixed tilt (Pakistan study). Source: [Renewable Energy 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0960148119302794) [Peer]
- Unsourced vendor claims: certified-installer failure rate <1.2% over 5 years vs 6.7% uncertified; 6-8 week lead times and +18% labour premium in Quetta/Peshawar. Source: [Alibaba-hosted guide](https://electronics.alibaba.com/question/solar-panels-in-pakistan-prices,-brands-realistic-buyer-guide) [Vendor, do not rely]

### Inferences
- A cheap "panel truth meter" (measures actual watts vs irradiance and panel temperature, compares with nameplate) addresses the counterfeit/inflated-wattage problem directly. This uses the same hardware as a tracker PoC (light sensor + power meter), but not the servos.
- Wrong tilt is likely common in DIY/informal installs, but no Pakistani dataset quantifies it; an orientation/tilt checker app or device is plausible but unproven as a pain point.

### Gaps
- No national data on share of installs with wrong tilt/orientation, shading losses, or energy lost to poor installation.
- No reliable data on technician numbers or training (NEPRA/AEDB/PSA not found in this pass).
- No measured data on the share of counterfeit panels in the market.

## 5. Existing Pakistani solar companies and gaps

## 6. Problem-by-problem: is an Arduino-class smart tracker (servos, light sensors, power meter, WiFi, LED display) a credible proof of concept?

### Takeaway
Credible as a PoC for solar irrigation pumps (more pumping hours from tracking, plus metering to answer the groundwater concern) and for soiling/underperformance detection; weak for rooftop tracking at household scale, where low panel prices ($0.08/W) make "add one more panel" cheaper than moving parts. The sensing/metering half of the device is more transferable to Pakistan than the servo half.

### Cited Findings
- Single-axis tracking gives 10-17% more irradiance than fixed tilt across 39 northern-hemisphere sites (6-60° latitude); industry guides cite 12-25%. Source: [MDPI Applied Sciences 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/8/4571) [Peer]; [PVcase](https://pvcase.com/blog/fixed-tilt-vs-tracker-system-comparison-for-ground-mounted-pv-systems) [Vendor]
- In high-diffuse locations, fixed tilt can match trackers. Source: [arXiv 2011.01276](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.01276) [Preprint]
- Seasonal manual tilt (4 changes/year) already gives ~6.6% in Pakistan. Source: [Renewable Energy 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0960148119302794) [Peer]
- Panel price ~$0.08/W in 2025. Source: [Payne Institute](https://payneinstitute.mines.edu/pakistans-solar-surge/) [Think tank]

### Inferences (assessment per problem)
1. **Solar tubewell / pump hours (STRONG fit).** Tracking widens the window above the pump's start threshold in morning and evening. Demo: servo-tracked vs fixed mini-panel each driving a small DC pump; LED display shows minutes of pumping and litres. Add an hours/energy log to address groundwater regulators. Caveat: real 10-20 HP arrays need robust, dust-proof, wind-rated actuators; an Arduino demo proves the control logic, not the mechanics.
2. **Soiling detection and cleaning timing (STRONG fit for the sensing part).** Light sensor (reference irradiance) + power meter gives a "performance ratio"; a drop on clear days means dirt. Directly addresses Lahore's ~0.8%/day soiling and water scarcity.
3. **Counterfeit / underrated panels (GOOD fit, sensing only).** Same sensors check actual W vs irradiance vs nameplate. No servos needed.
4. **Smog/diffuse-light mode (MODERATE, differentiating feature).** Light sensors can detect diffuse conditions and park flat. Unquantified for Lahore, so present as a hypothesis to test.
5. **Hail/dust-storm stow (MODERATE).** Servo stow is plausible for small ground arrays; hail damage is documented but frequency is not quantified.
6. **Rooftop household tracking (WEAK).** With panels at ~$0.08/W, adding 15% more panels is cheaper and has no moving parts; rooftops also carry wind, structural and maintenance risk; and post-Feb-2026 export rates are low. Tracking's extra midday energy is worth little; its extra evening energy is worth more, but this is a niche.
7. **Grid stress/evening peak (INDIRECT).** A WiFi device could shift loads to solar hours or signal evening shortfalls; useful but not a tracker feature.
8. **Heat derating (WEAK).** Tracking does not cool panels; temperature sensing for monitoring is cheap but not a product by itself.

### Gaps
- No Pakistan-specific measured tracker gain (single- or dual-axis) found.
- No cost data for small trackers or actuators in Pakistan; no survey of farmer willingness to pay for pump monitoring.
- No published data on pumping hours gained by tracking specifically for Pakistani tubewells.
