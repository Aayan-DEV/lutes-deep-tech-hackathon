# Energy-aware / net-energy solar tracking: literature notes (focus on small trackers)

Notes on sourcing:
- "Verified" below means I read the abstract or full text (via the publisher, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, or the PDF itself). Where a number came only from a search-engine summary or a secondary citation, it is marked "(secondary / not verified against full text)".
- Blog, vendor, and patent sources are marked as such.
- Percentages are reported exactly as the source defines them. Watch the denominator: some papers report "% of generated energy", Mousazadeh reports "% of the *extra* energy gained".

## Q1. Which papers quantify tracker self-consumption (motors + electronics)? Numbers for small trackers

### Takeaway
Measured self-consumption ranges from about 1% to over 40% of the tracking *gain*, depending on scale and design. On small trackers the controller's standby draw, not the motor, is often the dominant cost (Ahmad et al. 2013: motors 0.05%, controller 5.84% of daily generation). The one recent small-panel study (50 Wp) found actuators ate about 43% of the extra energy that tracking produced.

### Cited Findings
- **Mousazadeh, H., Keyhani, A., Javadi, A., Mobli, H., Abrinia, K., Sharifi, A. (2009). "A review of principle and sun-tracking methods for maximizing solar systems output." *Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews* 13(8):1800-1818. DOI 10.1016/j.rser.2009.01.022.** The abstract says tracking raises collected energy by 10-100% depending on period and location. The tracker's power use is about 2-3% of the *increased* energy. The authors explicitly advise against tracking small solar panels because driving losses are high. This is the most-cited statement of the small-panel problem (about 950 citations per Semantic Scholar). [IDEAS/RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/rensus/v13y2009i8p1800-1818.html)
- **Ahmad, S., Shafie, S., Ab Kadir, M.Z.A. (2013). "Power feasibility of a low power consumption solar tracker." *Procedia Environmental Sciences* 17:494-502. DOI 10.1016/j.proenv.2013.02.064.** This is an open-loop, time-and-date, two-axis tracker driven by a PLC in Malaysia (10° altitude steps, 1° azimuth steps), tested on sunny, cloudy, and overcast/rainy days. On a clear day, the motors used 0.05% of daily generated power and the controller used 5.84%, for a total of 5.89%. The key lesson is that **the controller dominated by about 100x**. [Abstract via Semantic Scholar / Crossref DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.proenv.2013.02.064). The companion conference paper is Ahmad, Shafie, Ab Kadir (2012), "A high power generation, low power consumption solar tracker," IEEE PECon 2012, pp. 366-371, DOI 10.1109/PECON.2012.6450239.
- **Osman, I.S., Almadani, I., Hariri, N., Maatallah, T.S. (2022). "Experimental Investigation and Comparison of the Net Energy Yield Using Control-Based Solar Tracking Systems." *International Journal of Photoenergy* 2022:7715214. DOI 10.1155/2022/7715214.** The authors ran fixed, single-axis, and dual-axis flat-plate PV side by side with PID control on an embedded system, under clear skies in Eastern Saudi Arabia. Controller consumption was 3.4% (single-axis) and 3.9% (dual-axis). Actuator losses were 7.8% (single-axis) and 13.0% (dual-axis), and the actuators were the main loss. Net gains over fixed after all losses were +18.73% (single-axis) and +28.98% (dual-axis). Dual-axis gave about 8.64% more net energy than single-axis despite its higher actuation loss. [Abstract (Semantic Scholar record of DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/7715214)
- **Syarif, Baride, Haryani, Atina (2026). "Evaluation of Net Energy Gain Solar Tracker Single-Axis based on Relay of 50 Wp Solar Panel." *PISTON: Jurnal Teknologi* 11(1):70-81. DOI 10.55679/pistonjt.v11i1.140** (Indonesian, small journal). The setup was a 50 Wp panel with a relay-driven single-axis tracker, tested in Baubau, Indonesia for 5 days (6-10 Aug 2023, 09:00-16:00 only). Mean power rose from 17.71 W to 22.45 W (+26.92%), and daily energy rose from 134.68 Wh to 170.64 Wh (+35.96 Wh). **Actuator consumption was 15.30 Wh/day**, so the net gain was 20.66 Wh/day (+15.34%). The actuator therefore consumed about 43% of the gross tracking gain (my arithmetic: 15.30 / 35.96). The abstract explicitly says most prior work ignores drive consumption. [Crossref record](https://doi.org/10.55679/pistonjt.v11i1.140)
- **Lazaroiu, G.C., Longo, M., Roscia, M., Pagano, M. (2015). "Comparative analysis of fixed and sun tracking low power PV systems considering energy consumption." *Energy Conversion and Management* 92:143-148. DOI 10.1016/j.enconman.2014.12.046.** The authors built two low-power PV systems (PV + MPPT + 12 V 40 Ah battery), one fixed and one tracking. They accounted for the tracker's own consumption with an analytical model and validated it experimentally. The abstract only reports a large gain in the morning and evening. **I could not get the net-gain figure** because the full text was blocked (the PoliMi repository returned 403). [PoliMi repository PDF](https://re.public.polimi.it/bitstream/11311/983615/4/Comparative%20analysis%20of%20fixed%20and%20sun%20tracking%20low%20power%20PV%20systems_11311-983615_Longo.pdf), [unibg record](https://aisberg.unibg.it/handle/10446/62790)
- **Seme, S., Štumberger, G. (2012). "Determination of the tracking system's trajectory considering the electric drive losses." *Renewable Energy and Power Quality Journal* 1(10):509-512 (ICREPQ'12). DOI 10.24084/repqj10.364.** The authors measured the energy of the PMDC drives of a two-axis tracker with E_c(t) = ∫u·i dt and built per-move energy curves E_c(Δβ) and E_c(Δa_w). These curves are exactly the "cost of a move" model a net-energy controller needs. Read from the figure axes in the extracted text, a full traverse took roughly 150-200 s and cost on the order of 10 Wh (approximate). [RE&PQJ PDF](https://repqj.com/index.php/repqj/article/download/2290/364-seme.pdf/4112)
- Review-level figures (secondary): Racharla/Hafez-type reviews and a 2024 *Energy Reports* review quote 2-3% as a typical "optimal" tracker consumption. They also quote 14-28% of average daily generation for some active single-axis actuators. I saw these only in search snippets, not in the full text. [Energy Reports review 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352484724006164) (secondary / not verified against full text)
- Vendor (not peer-reviewed): the SunTracer OG+ single-module tracker lists **35 mA at 12 V standby** (about 0.42 W, or about 10 Wh/day if always on, by my arithmetic) and up to 1000 mA while moving. A vendor test document reports about 20 Wh/day of motor energy, or 1.45% of collected energy. [thesolarstore product page](https://thesolarstore.com/products/solar-tracker-suntracer-og-for-one-60-cell-module), [vendor PDF via hellopro](https://www.hellopro.fr//documentation/pdf_prod/3/1/0/822839_daecf755df5b1d637033bb29b319c39a_7819013.pdf) (vendor; secondary)
- A UniMAP repository record of a dual-axis stepper tracker reports that the tracker used 1.38% of generated energy, with light sensors read only every 5 min to avoid micro-movements. [UniMAP record](https://ris.unimap.edu.my/entities/publication/c1c9e356-f3c9-446a-9dd7-3ca826fc9f74) (secondary / not verified against full text)

### Inferences
- The denominator matters a lot. "2-3% of extra energy" (Mousazadeh) is very different from "5.89% of total energy" (Ahmad) or "43% of the gain" (Syarif). A hackathon pitch should report **consumption as % of gross tracking gain**, because that is what decides net-positive vs net-negative.
- On a micro tracker, the always-on electronics (MCU, regulator, servo idle current) are likely to dominate, as in Ahmad 2013. Illustrative arithmetic (assumption, not sourced): a 1 W panel at 5 peak-sun-hours gives about 5 Wh/day, so a 30% tracking gain is about 1.5 Wh/day. A dev board idling at about 0.2-0.25 W for 24 h costs about 5-6 Wh/day, which already exceeds the gain. Deep sleep and power-gating the servos are therefore required, not optional, for a net-positive micro tracker.
- Actuation share grows with axis count (Osman: 7.8% to 13.0%) and with mass and wind load. Small panels lose the "economy of scale" because electronics overhead does not shrink with panel area.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed paper with a clean breakdown for a **sub-10 W hobby-servo tracker** (e.g., SG90/MG996R + Arduino) that reports measured servo energy, idle current, and MCU energy separately.
- I could not read the Lazaroiu 2015 full text for its net-gain number, or the full text of the Osman 2022 paper for panel ratings and tracking interval (publisher 403).

## Q2. Literature on "net energy gain" / "energy-optimal" tracking (controller decides whether to move using a cost/benefit model)

### Takeaway
Yes, this exists, but mostly as offline trajectory optimization or heuristic energy-saving modes on research rigs. The clearest formulation is Seme, Štumberger & Voršič (2011, IEEE TPEL): maximize (PV energy − tracking energy) using measured per-move drive energy. Utility-scale vendors use a related rule for diffuse stow: compare modeled irradiance in stow vs tracking. I found no small or micro tracker that runs a live "move only if expected gain > measured move cost" rule and verifies it with its own wattmeters.

### Cited Findings
- **Seme, S., Štumberger, G., Voršič, J. (2011). "Maximum efficiency trajectories of a two-axis sun tracking system determined considering tracking system consumption." *IEEE Transactions on Power Electronics* 26(4):1280-1290. DOI 10.1109/TPEL.2011.2105506.** The goal is to maximize PV electric energy *minus* tracking consumption. The tilt and azimuth trajectories are solved as a bounded nonlinear problem with Differential Evolution. The objective is evaluated using models of available radiation, tracking-system consumption, and cell plus DC/DC efficiency, with time and angle quantization as bounds. Result: the optimal trajectories depend on radiation, cell efficiency, tracking consumption, and the bounds. [OpenAlex abstract / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPEL.2011.2105506). A catalogue summary says the test system was 7 m² and that the gain over a simple step-by-step tracker was "not substantial", but that the ratio would change with module count or drive consumption. [COBISS record](https://plus-legacy.cobiss.net/cobiss/si/en/bib/14971926) (secondary / not verified against full text)
- **Seme & Štumberger (2012), RE&PQJ, DOI 10.24084/repqj10.364** (the conference companion). The objective function is q = E_ideal / (E_PV − E_c). E_c is the sum of measured per-step energies for tilt and azimuth plus the end-of-day return to start. Step heights and the times between steps are the decision variables, for a 15 m² PV system at 46°33′N with η = 9.8%. The paper states that the motors can only be switched on and off at constant speed, so trajectories are step functions. The authors propose future **sky-monitoring with cloud-movement prediction plus drive-energy models**, so that the tracker reaches maximum gain on cloudy days. That is essentially predictive cost-aware tracking. The paper also cites earlier "intuitive algorithms based on maximal change of angle and operating time" (Huang & Sun 2007; Visa et al. 2008). [RE&PQJ PDF](https://repqj.com/index.php/repqj/article/download/2290/364-seme.pdf/4112)
- **Flores-Hernández, D.A., Palomino-Resendiz, S.I., Luviano-Juárez, A., Lozada-Castillo, N., Gutiérrez-Frías, O. (2019). "A Heuristic Approach for Tracking Error and Energy Consumption Minimization in Solar Tracking Systems." *IEEE Access* 7:52755-52768. DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2019.2912317** (open access). The tracker is a two-DOF robotic tracker with a solar measuring device. The authors test four energy-saving strategies and three tracking strategies. The best combination saved 31.55% energy in simulation (0.06° error) and 26.98% experimentally (0.062° azimuth and 0.071° elevation error) vs. the reference. [IEEE Access OA PDF](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/ielx7/6287639/8600701/08694994.pdf)
- **Palomino-Resendiz, S.I., Flores-Hernández, D.A., Cantera-Cantera, L.A., Lozada-Castillo, N., Luviano-Juárez, A. (2023). "Design and implementation of Model-Based Predictive Control for two-axis Solar Tracker." *Solar Energy* 265:112080. DOI 10.1016/j.solener.2023.112080.** This is the same group's MPC follow-up. The abstract was not available to me, so I could not confirm energy figures. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.solener.2023.112080)
- **Array Technologies, SmarTrack Diffuse (vendor).** The controller takes on-site GHI, runs quality checks, and then compares modeled irradiance in stow with modeled irradiance under normal tracking. It decides to stow or unstow using thresholds plus a lookback period to avoid false positives. This is a deployed "model both options, pick the better one" decision, but it compares irradiance, not irradiance minus motor energy. [Array SmarTrack Diffuse page](https://arraytechinc.com/products/smartrack/smartrack-diffuse/) (vendor)
- Patent: US 8,203,070 "Automated solar tracking system" describes waiting until stored energy reaches a threshold before moving, to avoid power-cycling and many small, inefficient moves. [USPTO PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8203070) (patent; secondary, seen via search summary)
- Blog-level, **unverified**: a "Lacuna" article argues that greedy constant repositioning can cost more than it gains in wind or passing cloud, and that each move should beat its actuation cost. It claims cost-aware control saves "up to 90%" of motor energy and cites Panagopoulos et al. 2015 and Abel et al. 2018. I could not verify those citations or numbers. [Lacuna page](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/direction/maximizing-net-efficiency-in-active-control-systems/txn_3f74d2490ca1400e9848c9f304e1cf0e) (blog; treat as unreliable)

### Inferences
- The idea of "maximize generation minus actuation" is **not new academically** (Seme et al. 2011 is the anchor citation). What appears uncommon is (a) doing it **online** on a cheap micro tracker, (b) using **measured** per-move energy from an on-board current sensor rather than a lab-characterized model, and (c) counting standby and electronics energy in the decision.
- A practical decision rule in the Seme framing: move if (expected ΔP from re-aiming × time until next decision) > E_move(Δθ) + ΔE_electronics. Here E_move comes from a measured E_c(Δθ) curve like Seme's Fig. 4.

### Gaps
- I did not find the Seme 2011 TPEL full-text numbers (exact % gain vs continuous and vs step tracking).
- I did not find peer-reviewed numbers for "online", sensor-measured cost/benefit tracking on small hardware.

## Q3. Optimal tracking step interval / angular step results

### Takeaway
Coarse stepping loses very little. Franchi et al. (2022) found at most a 1.68% annual difference between 2°, 5°, 10°, and 30° steps on a one-axis PV tracker. Huang's one-axis three-position tracker (only 3 positions per day) still got +24.2% over fixed across two years. A 2025 solar-thermal study found 10-15° steps keep 90-95% of the continuous-tracking benefit with 13-19 moves/day instead of 181. Cosine loss is tiny for small errors (about 1.5% at 10°).

### Cited Findings
- **Franchi, D., Gonzatti, F., Miotto, M., da Silva, É.I. (2022). "An Experimental Study on the Angular Step of One-Axis PV Trackers and Its Impact on Energy Harvesting." *Journal of Control, Automation and Electrical Systems*. DOI 10.1007/s40313-022-00936-9.** The rig was an east-west axis with fixed tilt, tested for 1 year with 2°, 5°, 10°, and 30° steps vs a fixed module. The maximum difference between tracking strategies was only 1.68%. Clear or mostly clear days (60.9% of the period) gave 78.8% of the energy. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40313-022-00936-9) (abstract verified via Semantic Scholar)
- **Huang, B.J., Sun, F.S. (2007). "Feasibility study of one axis three positions tracking solar PV with low concentration ratio reflector." *Energy Conversion and Management* 48(4):1273-1280. DOI 10.1016/j.enconman.2006.09.020.** This paper introduces "1A-3P" tracking: three fixed positions per day (morning, noon, afternoon). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enconman.2006.09.020) (abstract not retrieved)
- **Huang, B.J., Huang, Y.C., Chen, G.Y., Hsu, P.C., Li, K. (2013). "Improving Solar PV System Efficiency Using One-Axis 3-Position Sun Tracking." *Energy Procedia* 33:280-287. DOI 10.1016/j.egypro.2013.05.069.** A 1A-3P tracker gave +39% on clear days and +24.2% total over fixed (Taipei, Mar 2010 to May 2012). The expected gain in high-radiation areas was +37.5%. Installation cost was about the same as a regular rooftop mount, and a field test ran over 30 months. [ScienceDirect OA PDF](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1876610213000799/pdf)
- **Kowalik, R., Nešović, A. (2025). "Stepwise Single-Axis Tracking of Flat-Plate Solar Collectors: Optimal Rotation Step Size in a Continental Climate." *Energies* 18(21):5776. DOI 10.3390/en18215776** (solar thermal, simulation in EnergyPlus). Continuous tracking (1° steps) needed 181 moves/day. Steps of 10-15° kept more than 90-95% of the benefit with 13-19 moves/day. At 45-90° steps, the advantage collapsed to only 5-10% over fixed. [MDPI DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/en18215776). A companion review in *Energies* 18(23):6106 (DOI 10.3390/en18236106) attributes the ">90% of continuous gain at 10-15° steps" finding to Khalifa and later Mehleri et al. (secondary / not verified against full text).
- A trade-press-level result: a "five-position" tracking strategy was reported as more energy-efficient than continuous tracking (KMUTT, "Performance Analysis of Solar Tracking Systems by Five-Position Angles with a Single Axis and Dual Axis"). [KMUTT record](https://kirim.kmutt.ac.th/converis/portal/detail/Publication/1447092228?lang=en_GB) (secondary / numbers not verified)
- Cosine loss (direct-beam power lost = 1 − cos(error)): about 0.015% at 1°, about 1% at 8°, and about 3.4% at 15°. A tracker accurate to ±5° captures more than 99.6% of direct beam plus all diffuse light. [Wikipedia: Solar tracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_tracker) (encyclopedic secondary; the maths is standard)
- Practice: a ScienceDirect topic overview describes a system that re-aims every 3-4 min horizontally and every 4-5 min vertically, with motor plus control under 3% of collected energy. [ScienceDirect topic: one-axis tracker](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/one-axis-tracker) (secondary). A tracker patent describes readings every 4 or 10 min, with longer intervals in low light. [USPTO 10690381](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10690381) (patent; secondary)

### Inferences
- My own arithmetic: the sun's hour angle moves 15°/h, i.e. 1° per 4 min. If a tracker re-aims to the sun's position every T minutes, the worst-case error is about T/4 degrees (less for the elevation axis). At T = 20 min (5° error), loss is 1 − cos 5° ≈ 0.38% at worst. At T = 40 min (10°), it is about 1.5%. On a micro tracker, the controller should therefore choose a **large deadband (5-10°)** and spend the saved energy budget on sleeping.
- The biggest win is **between none and a few moves**, not between a few and many: 3 positions/day already gets most of the gain (Huang). That strongly favors "few, well-timed moves" on small panels.

### Gaps
- Franchi 2022 full text (per-step daily data and motor energy per strategy) was not accessible.
- I found no PV paper that jointly optimizes step size **and** measured actuator energy on a small panel.

## Q4. Strategies under cloudy / diffuse conditions (go horizontal, "diffuse tracking", "smart stow")

### Takeaway
Kelly & Gibson (GM R&D) showed that under heavy overcast a horizontal panel collects about 1.3-1.8x (mean 1.48x) more than one pointed at the hidden sun. Annual gains from a horizontal-on-overcast rule are small (about 1-2% in the literature; 0.38% in a 2025 one-year utility field test), but individual cloudy days gain 5-40%. The rule is already commercial at utility scale (Nextracker DiffuseBoost, Array SmarTrack Diffuse). For a micro tracker it has a second benefit: on overcast days it also **saves the actuation energy**.

### Cited Findings
- **Kelly, N.A., Gibson, T.L. (2009). "Improved photovoltaic energy output for cloudy conditions with a solar tracking system." *Solar Energy* 83(11):2092-2102. DOI 10.1016/j.solener.2009.08.009** (Crossref verified; about 216 citations). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.solener.2009.08.009)
- **Kelly, N.A., Gibson, T.L. (2011). "Increasing the solar photovoltaic energy capture on sunny and cloudy days." *Solar Energy* 85(1):111-125. DOI 10.1016/j.solener.2010.10.015** (online 2010; about 127 citations; volume and pages from memory, DOI and title verified via OpenAlex). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.solener.2010.10.015)
- **Kelly & Gibson patent US 8,101,848 (GM)**, "Solar photovoltaic output for cloudy conditions with a solar tracking system." The method uses two-axis tracking when measured irradiance indicates sun, and goes horizontal when irradiance indicates heavy cloud. Across 20 cloudy-day measurements in Detroit (fall 2004 and spring 2005), horizontal sensors produced 1.31-1.82x (mean 1.48 ± 0.16) the output of sun-pointed sensors. Over a year in Detroit, two-axis tracking gave about 30% more than latitude-tilt fixed. [Google Patents US8101848](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8101848), [USPTO PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8101848)
- **Foes, J.G.F., Campos, R.A., Nascimento, L.R., Wetzel, J., de Souza, R.R., Rüther, R. (2025). "Single-Pyranometer Approach for Diffuse Optimization in Solar Tracking Systems." ISES Solar World Congress 2025 Proceedings** (UFSC + Brametal, Brazil). This is a one-year field test on a utility single-axis plant. The rule is to go horizontal when the clearness index K_c = GHI/GHI_clear stays below 0.3 for more than 8 min. The 8-minute persistence was set because the worst-case move to horizontal takes 8 min 20 s. After each activation, trackers stayed horizontal for 52 min on average. Results: +0.38% cumulative energy and up to +5.49% on single cloudy days, with gain negatively correlated with K_c. The authors note persistence was applied only to entering stow, not to leaving it (a known weakness). The paper cites Kelly & Gibson (2011) and Koussa et al. (2011) for daily gains of up to 40% but annual gains of only about 1-2%. [ISES PDF](https://proceedings.ises.org/conference/swc2025/papers/swc2025-0275-Foes.pdf)
- **Quinn, S.W. (2017). "Energy gleaning for extracting additional energy and improving the efficiency of 2-axis time-position tracking photovoltaic arrays under variably cloudy skies." *Solar Energy* 148:25-35. DOI 10.1016/j.solener.2017.02.005.** This paper is directly on cloudy-sky strategies for 2-axis time-position trackers. The abstract was not retrievable, so no numbers. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.solener.2017.02.005)
- A Univ. of Burgos / Valladolid study, "Optimización de la estrategia de seguimiento de seguidores solares a dos ejes en días cubiertos", compares three two-axis strategies on overcast days using a year of irradiance data: sun-normal, horizontal, and an orientation that maximizes overcast-sky irradiance. Numbers were not retrieved. [UVa portal](https://portaldelaciencia.uva.es/documentos/670cef62dae88459a5e39eb1)
- A 2013 review (Chaire ET3E, ÉTS Montréal preprint) states that tracking during cloudy periods is counterproductive because most radiation is diffuse. [ETS preprint](https://chairet3e.etsmtl.ca/pdf/Publications/Preprint/2013-JRSE-solar%20tracking_PREPRINT.pdf) (secondary, seen via search summary)
- Industry: Nextracker TrueCapture has a "DiffuseBoost" mode that turns panels skyward under diffuse light. A newer "Zonal Diffuse" feature switches only cloud-shaded zones and checks status every 2 min. TrueCapture's overall claimed gain is 2-6% (a 2018 figure covering all features, not diffuse alone). [TaiyangNews](https://taiyangnews.info/technology/nextrackers-truecapture-solar-tracker-technology-with-intelligent-control-systems), [Solar Power World 2018](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2018/11/smart-tracking-technology-updates-usher-in-new-era-of-increased-energy-harvest-on-large-scale-sites/) (trade press / vendor)

### Inferences
- For a hackathon tracker, "go flat and *stay asleep* when K_c is low" stacks two gains: higher diffuse capture, and zero motor energy. In the utility case the second gain is negligible, but on a micro tracker it may be the larger of the two.
- Foes et al. give a ready-made, citable threshold design (K_c < 0.3, persistence of about the move time, and hysteresis on exit) that can be scaled down. Persistence should be scaled to the micro tracker's move time (seconds, not minutes).

### Gaps
- I could not read the Kelly & Gibson 2009/2011 abstracts directly (publisher elides them). The 1.48x figure comes from their patent, and the "up to 40% daily, 1-2% annual" figure comes from Foes et al.'s citation of Kelly & Gibson 2011 plus Koussa 2011.
- There is no published diffuse-mode gain figure from Array; Nextracker's 2-6% is not diffuse-specific.

## Q5. Hackathon-scale demos: has anyone built a micro tracker that measures its own net energy (wattmeter on panel AND servos)?

### Takeaway
I found no hobby or hackathon-scale project that logs both panel energy and servo/controller energy on the same clock and reports a net figure, let alone one that uses that measurement to decide when to move. Student and Arduino projects typically report gross panel gain only. The closest are small research rigs (Syarif 2026, 50 Wp; Ahmad 2013, PLC; Lazaroiu 2015, low-power battery systems) that measure actuator energy but do not close the control loop on it.

### Cited Findings
- **Sarla, A., Dandu, S.C.R. (2022). "Sun Tracking System." Bachelor thesis, Blekinge Institute of Technology (DiVA).** The build is an Arduino Uno with 4 LDRs, 2 SG90 micro servos, and an LCD showing panel power. A text search of the thesis found no measurement of servo or controller energy. This is typical of the genre. [DiVA PDF](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1674078/FULLTEXT02.pdf)
- Arduino and INA219 tracker papers in small journals report panel-side gains only, e.g. +26.42% power from a calibrated INA219 comparison, and a 14-day servo-and-LDR boat tracker averaging 7.81 W vs 7.26 W fixed. None subtract servo energy. [Polines Eksergi](https://jurnal.polines.ac.id/index.php/eksergi/article/view/7559), [ResearchGate: servo/LDR tracker for electric boats](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/349499008_Development_of_a_Solar_Tracker_Using_Servo_Motor_and_Light_Dependent_Resistor_for_Electrical_Boats) (secondary, seen via search summary)
- An INA219 "servo tester" exists on Hackaday for measuring servo draw under load, but it is not tied to a solar tracker. [Hackaday.io](https://hackaday.io/project/205519-arduino-servo-tester-with-ina219) (hobby)
- Student projects that address standby energy: Cornell ECE4760 (2005) used relays to cut stepper current when not moving. [Cornell PDF](https://people.ece.cornell.edu/land/courses/ece4760/FinalProjects/s2005/tp62/website/solartracker.pdf). Harvey Mudd E155 (Hoe & Trikha, 2019) put the MCU to sleep between correction cycles. [HMC PDF](https://pages.hmc.edu/harris/class/e155/projects19/Hoe_Trikha.pdf). Neither reports a measured net-energy balance (seen via search summary). (student projects)
- Arduino forum guidance: steppers draw near full current while holding, and many drivers reduce it at rest. A worm-gear DC motor can be fully de-energized because it self-locks. [Arduino forum](https://forum.arduino.cc/t/driver-for-nema-23/609671) (forum; low authority)
- Syarif et al. 2026 (50 Wp, actuator 15.30 Wh/day vs +35.96 Wh/day gain) is the closest small-scale "net energy gain" measurement found (see Q1). [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.55679/pistonjt.v11i1.140)

### Inferences
- A micro tracker with two current/power monitors (panel side and actuator+MCU side), a fixed reference panel, and a live "net energy vs fixed" readout would fill a visible gap at hackathon scale. Adding a controller that uses the *measured* cost of each move, plus a diffuse-stow mode, would put the academic ideas (Seme 2011; Kelly & Gibson 2009; Foes 2025) into a cheap, self-auditing device.
- To make the demo convincing, include a "naive tracker" mode (moves every few seconds, servos always powered) to show the net-negative case. Mousazadeh 2009's warning about small panels gives a citable motivation for it.

### Gaps
- Absence of evidence is not proof. Search engines index hobby projects poorly, so a similar Hackster/Instructables build may exist. I did not search Hackster, Instructables, or Devpost exhaustively.
- I found no measured SG90/MG90-class servo idle and moving current data in a peer-reviewed tracker context.

## Q6. How widely are these ideas used in practice? (novelty check)

### Takeaway
Each building block is known: deadband/step tracking is standard, few-position tracking was published in 2007, cost-aware trajectory optimization in 2011, and diffuse stow is commercial at utility scale since about 2018. The combination of **measured self-consumption + online move/no-move decision + diffuse stow on a micro tracker with a live net-energy audit** was not found in the literature searched.

### Cited Findings
- Utility scale: single-axis trackers dominate new utility PV (e.g., about 94% of Brazilian utility plants, per Foes et al. citing López 2025). Diffuse stow is shipped by Nextracker (DiffuseBoost / Zonal Diffuse) and Array (SmarTrack Diffuse). [ISES 2025 PDF](https://proceedings.ises.org/conference/swc2025/papers/swc2025-0275-Foes.pdf), [Array](https://arraytechinc.com/products/smartrack/smartrack-diffuse/), [TaiyangNews](https://taiyangnews.info/technology/nextrackers-truecapture-solar-tracker-technology-with-intelligent-control-systems)
- Academic cost-aware control: Seme et al. 2011 (offline DE optimization with a measured drive model), Flores-Hernández et al. 2019 (heuristic energy-saving modes, about 27% actuator energy saved experimentally), and Palomino-Resendiz et al. 2023 (MPC). [TPEL DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPEL.2011.2105506), [IEEE Access](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/ielx7/6287639/8600701/08694994.pdf), [Solar Energy DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.solener.2023.112080)
- Low-move strategies at small scale: 1A-3P (Huang 2007/2013), five-position strategies, and 10-15° steps. [Energy Procedia](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1876610213000799/pdf), [Energies 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/en18215776)
- Patent prior art on "wait until stored energy threshold before moving" (US 8,203,070) and on predictive orientation control (e.g., USPTO 10903783 / 11387774 "Method for predictive control of the orientation of a solar tracker"). [USPTO 8203070](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8203070), [USPTO 11387774](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11387774) (patents; titles seen in search results, contents not reviewed)

### Inferences
- A hackathon project should **not claim** novelty for "diffuse stow", "step tracking", or "considering motor energy" in general. These have clear prior art, and judges or experts may know Kelly & Gibson or Seme.
- Defensible novelty claims are: (1) a self-measuring micro tracker that reports **net** energy vs a fixed twin in real time; (2) an online, measurement-calibrated move decision (the per-move energy cost is learned from its own current sensor rather than a lab model); (3) explicit handling of electronics standby energy, which the literature shows dominates at small scale but rarely optimizes.

### Gaps
- I did not search patents systematically (Google Patents / Espacenet) for "move only if expected energy gain exceeds actuation energy" claims. A quick patent search is recommended before making any novelty claim.
- I did not review the Hafez et al. (2017/2018, RSER), Nsengiyumva et al. (2018, RSER), or Racharla & Rajan (2017, RSER) reviews directly (paywalled). They may contain additional consumption tables.
