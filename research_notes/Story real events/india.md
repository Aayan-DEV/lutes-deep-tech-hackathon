# Real events in India: fixed solar panels hurt by dust and weather

All sources accessed on 11 October 2026.

Confidence key:
- **High:** two independent reputable sources agree.
- **Medium:** one reputable source.
- **Low:** blog, forum, or vendor press release only.

Note on panel type: most news reports do not say how the panels were mounted. Where the source does not say, this file says "not stated" and does not guess.

---

## 1. Dirty panels in Gujarat jumped about 50% in power after each wash

- **Date:** Study published online 23 June 2017 (Environmental Science and Technology Letters, vol. 4, no. 8). Field measurements were taken before that.
- **Place:** IIT Gandhinagar campus, Gandhinagar, Gujarat (next door to Rajasthan).
- **What happened:**
  - Duke University and IIT Gandhinagar researchers (lead author Michael Bergin) left test panels uncleaned for several weeks and measured them.
  - Each time the panels were cleaned, output rose by about 50%.
  - The grime was 92% dust. The rest was carbon and ion pollution from human activity, which is small, sticky, and blocks light more strongly than dust.
  - Their model estimated losses of 17 to 25% or more for northern India with monthly cleaning, and 25 to 35% with cleaning every two months.
- **Panel type:** Stationary test panels on a campus. Exact mounting (rooftop or ground, tilt angle) is not stated in the articles.
- **Cause:** Dust and air pollution settling on panel glass.
- **Sources:**
  1. Down To Earth, "Dust, particulate matter in air reducing solar energy output by 25 per cent: study", 27 June 2017. https://www.downtoearth.org.in/news/energy/dust-particulate-matter-reducing-solar-energy-output-by-25-per-cent-study-58159 (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  2. Solar Daily, "Air pollution casts shadow over solar energy production", 28 June 2017. https://www.solardaily.com/reports/Air_pollution_casts_shadow_over_solar_energy_production_999.html (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  3. Journal paper: Bergin et al., Environ. Sci. Technol. Lett. 2017, DOI 10.1021/acs.estlett.7b00197 (citation confirmed through search; full text not read).
- **Confidence:** High.
- **What a smart tracker would change:** Tilting steep could shed the loose dust (92% of the grime), but it would not remove the sticky pollution particles, so the tracker cuts the loss but does not replace cleaning.

## 2. A dust storm in western Rajasthan uprooted about 700 solar panels near Bhadla

- **Date:** Night of Monday 1 June 2026 (National Herald report is dated 2 June 2026; PTI report updated 4 June 2026).
- **Place:** Phalodi district (Bhadla Solar Park area, plus villages Baap, Kanasar, Nikh, Badi Did), with wider damage in Jaisalmer and Barmer.
- **What happened:**
  - A dust storm with rain and high winds hit western Rajasthan.
  - Officials said nearly 700 solar panels were uprooted and around 800 electricity poles were damaged. PTI calls the panel figure "believed" and the pole figure "reportedly", so these are not final counts.
  - Inside Bhadla Solar Park (about 2.25 GW), the Phalodi Collector said only a 300 MW plant run by ACME Solar Holdings reported damage, and "no major large-scale destruction" across the park.
  - PM-KUSUM farm solar projects on private land were also hit.
  - The storm also killed a folk artist in Jaisalmer and two children in Barmer.
- **Panel type:** Mix of a utility plant (ACME, 300 MW) and small farm plants (PM-KUSUM). Mounting type is not stated in either source. It is not confirmed whether the 700 uprooted panels were inside or outside the park.
- **Cause:** High wind during a dust storm (structural damage, not soiling).
- **Sources:**
  1. National Herald (NH Digital), "Dust storm kills folk artist, 2 children in Rajasthan; solar parks, power network hit", 2 June 2026. https://www.nationalheraldindia.com/amp/story/national/dust-storm-kills-folk-artist-2-children-in-rajasthan-solar-parks-power-network-hit (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  2. PropNewsTime, citing PTI, "Cyclonic storm causes damage at Bhadla Solar Park, restoration work underway", last updated 4 June 2026. https://propnewstime.com/latestnewsstories/MzEyNjE%3D/cyclonic-storm-causes-damage-at-bhadla-solar-park-restoration-work-underway (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  3. Related: Mercom India (Rakesh Ranjan), "Extreme weather exposes hidden risks in rooftop solar module design", 4 June 2026, says modules in Rajasthan were "bent, torn, or detached" and blames thin aluminium frames. Paywalled preview only; no counts. https://mercomindia.com/extreme-weather-exposes-hidden-risks-in-rooftop-solar-module-design-in-depth-analysis (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
- **Confidence:** High that the event happened and solar assets were damaged (two outlets, one is PTI). Medium on the 700 panel figure (officials' early estimate).
- **What a smart tracker would change:** Dust detection does nothing against wind; a tracker only helps if it moves to a flat "wind stow" position in time, and a single-post dual-axis tracker catches more wind than a low fixed rack, so it could fare worse without that feature.

## 3. Two storms in 25 days damaged Bhadla Solar Park in 2019

- **Date:** 17 April 2019 and 11 May 2019.
- **Place:** Bhadla Solar Park, Jodhpur district, Rajasthan (then over 1,500 MW installed).
- **What happened:**
  - The April 17 storm brought winds reported as high as 130 km/h at the site and "reportedly damaged a large number of solar panels". No panel count was published.
  - Developers named: ACME, SB Energy, Azure, Adani, ReNew. Rooftop panels at Udaipur railway station were also hit.
  - The May 11 storm had lower winds but caused more damage, possibly because the first storm had weakened the structures. Developers named: NTPC, ReNew, Azure, Tata Power, Hero Future Energies.
  - The outlet raised the question of poor workmanship versus winds above the rated level. No official damage total was found.
- **Panel type:** Utility scale. Mounting type not stated.
- **Cause:** High wind during pre-monsoon storms.
- **Sources:**
  1. I Am Renew, "Bhadla Solar Park hit after Massive Storm in Rajasthan", 22 April 2019. https://www.iamrenew.com/green-energy/bhadla-solar-project-hit-after-massive-storm-in-rajasthan/ (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  2. I Am Renew, "Fresh hit to Bhadla from May 11 storm. Worrying Situation for Top Developers", 13 May 2019 (updated 14 May). https://www.iamrenew.com/green-energy/fresh-hit-to-bhadla-from-may-11-storm-worrying-situation-for-top-developers/ (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  3. Saur Energy carries the same May 11 headline (Saur News Bureau, 14 May 2019); body not read. https://www.saurenergy.com/solar-energy-news/fresh-hit-bhadla-may-11-storm-worrying-situation-top-developers (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
- **Confidence:** Medium. Only Indian solar trade outlets covered it; no mainstream outlet or official count found. The 130 km/h figure is reported, not independently confirmed.
- **What a smart tracker would change:** Same as event 2: dust detection would not help, and only a working wind stow mode would reduce this kind of damage.

## 4. Bhadla's dust is bad enough that plants pay for nightly robot cleaning

- **Date:** Ongoing; robot deals announced December 2016, March 2018, mid-2018; robot trial February to March 2019.
- **Place:** Bhadla Solar Park, Jodhpur district, Rajasthan.
- **What happened:**
  - Ecoppia (a robot cleaning company) claimed in 2016 that Bhadla's dust storms "can reduce energy generation by as much as 40% in a matter of minutes". This is a vendor claim, not a measured, published figure.
  - ENGIE had 168 MWp cleaned nightly by robots, with a claimed saving of over 1.5 billion litres of water. Ostro Energy fitted 84 MW (claimed 0.5 billion litres saved). SB Energy ordered about 2,000 robots for five sites.
  - Japanese firm Miraikikai trialled robots on the 70 MW Phase-2 plant in 2019 and said, from its own research, soiling can cut generation by more than 10% in a month in the worst case.
  - Before robots, many plants used manual wet cleaning, which costs scarce desert water and labour.
- **Panel type:** Utility scale, fixed rows (robots run along fixed rows). Exact tilt not stated.
- **Cause:** Desert dust settling on panels; frequent dust storms; water scarcity for washing.
- **Sources:**
  1. pv magazine India, "Ecoppia deploys PV cleaning systems for 84 MW Ostro Energy solar plants in Bhadla", 20 March 2018. https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/2018/03/20/ecoppia-deploys-three-pv-cleaning-systems-for-84-mw-ostro-energy-solar-plants-in-bhadla/ (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  2. PR Newswire (Ecoppia press release), "ENGIE Group leverages Ecoppia...", 7 December 2016. https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/engie-group-leverages-ecoppia-to-deliver-lower-lcoe-higher-output-in-successful-indian-solar-park-bid-605166556.html (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  3. pv magazine (Miraikikai press release), "Miraikikai to commence dry-cleaning robot operation at Bhadla Solar Park, Rajasthan", 2 April 2019. https://pv-magazine.com/press-releases/miraikikai-to-commence-dry-cleaning-robot-operation-at-bhadla-solar-park-rajasthan (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
- **Confidence:** High that soiling is a real, costly problem at Bhadla (reported by pv magazine India, March 2018, and Renewable Watch, 29 June 2019: https://renewablewatch.in/2019/06/29/robots-at-work/). Low for the numbers (40% in minutes, more than 10% a month), because they come only from vendors selling cleaning robots.
- **What a smart tracker would change:** Tilting steep after a dust storm could shed loose sand quickly, but fine dust that sticks overnight with dew would still need cleaning, so a tracker reduces cleaning trips rather than ending them.

## 5. A 5 MW Rajasthan plant made less than half the money it was supposed to

- **Date:** Plant built 2010 to 2011; findings reported 12 September 2017.
- **Place:** Rajasthan (exact town not named in the article).
- **What happened:**
  - The plant was projected to make 7.5 million units a year (about USD 1.8 million income) but earned about USD 860,000 a year, less than half.
  - Researchers from NISE, IIT Bombay, and BITS Pilani tested it. Average module degradation was 2.75% over four years, and more than 20% of surveyed modules degraded unusually fast.
  - An NISE survey string showed a 9% output drop; about 3 to 3.5 percentage points were put down to soiling and installation mistakes, the rest to PID (potential induced degradation: power loss when high voltage and heat push charge leakage through the module).
  - Heat was named the primary cause of the PID.
- **Panel type:** Utility scale, crystalline silicon. The article discusses tilt angle, which suggests fixed tilt, but mounting is not stated outright.
- **Cause:** Mixed: cheap modules, poor workmanship, heat, and soiling. Dust was only part of the loss.
- **Sources:**
  1. pv magazine India (Ian Clover), "PID, soiling and module degradation in hot climates", 12 September 2017. http://www.pv-magazine-india.com/2017/09/12/pid-soiling-and-module-degradation-in-hot-climates/ (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
- **Confidence:** Medium (one reputable trade outlet citing named researchers).
- **What a smart tracker would change:** It would recover part of the soiling share (a few percent) and add sun-tracking gain, but it would not fix bad modules, PID, or poor installation, which caused most of the loss.

## Best pick for the story

**Event 1: the IIT Gandhinagar and Duke University dust study (2017).** It has two independent reputable sources and a peer-reviewed paper. It is about dust on panels that do not move, and it has one simple number (a 50% jump after cleaning).

Story text (for a 12 year old):

> Scientists from Duke University and IIT Gandhinagar left solar panels in Gujarat, right next to Rajasthan, without cleaning for a few weeks, as Down To Earth reported in June 2017. When they finally washed the dust off, the panels made about 50 percent more power right away. They worked out that dust and dirt can quietly steal 17 to 25 percent of solar power in northern India, even when panels are cleaned once a month.

Honest limit to keep in mind: most of that grime (92%) was dust that tilting can shake off, but some of it was sticky pollution that only washing removes.

Rajasthan backup: if the story must be set in Rajasthan, use Event 2 (the June 2026 storm that uprooted about 700 panels near Bhadla, reported by National Herald and PTI). It is well sourced, but it is about wind, not dust, and a dust-detecting tracker does not solve wind damage.
