# Pakistan's solar boom, the metering problem, and what comes next

All sources accessed on 11 October 2026.

Confidence key:
- **High:** two independent reputable sources agree.
- **Medium:** one reputable source.
- **Low:** blog, vendor, unsourced article, or search snippet only.

Important caveat on import numbers: almost every "Pakistan imported X GW" figure comes from Chinese customs export data, compiled by Ember (a UK energy think tank). Reuters, Renewables First and The Independent all cite that one dataset. So when several outlets agree, they agree because they read the same table, not because they counted separately. Renewables First (an Islamabad think tank) also uses Pakistan's fiscal year (July to June), which is why its numbers do not line up with calendar-year numbers.

## 1.2 What share of electricity solar reached

These three numbers measure different things, so do not mix them.

- **Ember, June 2026:** distributed solar (rooftop, off-grid, net-metered) went from 10% of Pakistan's electricity generation in FY23 to 28% in FY25. Counting grid losses and theft, it was 32% of supply. Distributed solar output grew from 15 TWh to 51 TWh. Electricity demand rose 21% in two years while grid generation fell 3%. https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/the-solarisation-of-pakistans-energy-economy/ . Also reported by PV Tech (Will Norman, 29 June 2026). https://www.pv-tech.org/pakistan-added-27gw-of-distributed-solar-in-2023-25-as-consumers-move-off-grid/ . **High** (Ember plus PV Tech, though PV Tech is reporting Ember's study).
- **Renewables First, May 2026:** distributed solar made 51 TWh in FY25, "roughly 46% of grid-supplied electricity" in the same period. Utility-scale generation fell 2% to 135 TWh, the fourth yearly fall in a row, from a peak of 154 TWh in FY22. pv magazine, 19 May 2026. https://www.pv-magazine.com/?p=69206 . **Medium.**
- **Reuters / Ember, June 2025:** solar supplied an average 25.3% of Pakistan's utility electricity in January to April 2025. Arab News (Reuters), 17 June 2025. https://www.arabnews.pk/pakistan/pakistans-solar-surge-lifts-it-into-rarefied-25-club-2604763 . **Medium.**
- **Earlier warning sign:** in August 2024, national grid demand fell more than 17% compared with August 2023. PV Tech, 29 June 2026 (link above). **Medium.**

"Net metering" means the meter runs backward when your panels push spare power into the grid, so your exported units cancel your imported units.

- About 466,000 (466,506) existing net-metering consumers by early 2026, versus 37.6 million grid-only consumers. The 466,506 figure also appears in Dawn coverage of the minister's statement (found by search).
- **Confidence:** High for about 283,000 at end 2024 (Dawn twice, from the ECC). High for about 466,000 by early 2026 (The News plus Dawn).

**Net-metered capacity**
- 4.9 GW by end of March 2025, 5.3 GW by end of April 2025, passed 5 GW in May 2025 (Renewables First data). pv magazine (Patrick Jowett), 2 June 2025. https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/06/02/pakistans-net-metering-capacity-hits-5-3-gw/ . About 95% of net-metered connections are under 25 kW. **Medium.**

**Plain summary:** net metering roughly went from 4 GW (end 2024) to 6 to 7 GW (late 2025 / early 2026), and from about 283,000 to about 466,000 users.

## 1.4 Policy changes to net metering

1. **13 March 2025, ECC decision.** The ECC cut the buyback rate (the price the utility pays for each spare unit you export) for new users to Rs10 per unit, about a two-thirds cut, and capped new systems at the sanctioned load plus a 10% cushion (before, systems could be 50% bigger than sanctioned load). Existing users would move over as their seven-year contracts expired. The Power Division claimed net metering had already pushed Rs159 billion of costs onto non-solar customers by December 2024. Dawn, 14 March 2025. https://www.dawn.com/news/1897740/solar-users-face-higher-costs-as-policy-revised
2. **26 March 2025, cabinet backs off.** The federal cabinet, chaired by Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, did not approve the change and sent it back to the Power Division for consultation. Profit put the old rate at Rs27 and the proposed rate at Rs8 to 9. Profit (Pakistan Today), 26 March 2025. https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/03/26/cabinet-members-express-dissatisfaction-with-impact-of-revised-net-metering-buyback-rate . **High** that the March 2025 cut was proposed and then shelved (Dawn plus Profit).
3. **October 2025.** A new proposal to cut the buyback rate from Rs22 to Rs11.30. The PM ordered the Power Division to review it. Seen in Profit search results only, not read in full. **Low.**
4. **29 January 2026, credits withheld then restored.** Some net-metering users with systems bigger than their licence got no credit at all for exported units in one billing cycle, due to a directive from the Power Purchase and Management Company. Power Minister Awais Leghari ordered credits restored and limited the cut to units above the licensed size. The News, 29 January 2026. https://www.thenews.pk/print/1395706-minister-steps-in-as-power-ministry-restores-net-metering-credits-nationwide . **Medium.**
5. **9 February 2026, net metering ends.** The regulator NEPRA notified the Prosumer Regulations 2026. Net metering is replaced by net billing (imports and exports are priced separately, not swapped unit for unit). Imports cost Rs37 to 55 per unit by slab. New users get about Rs10 to 11 per exported unit instead of Rs26. New contracts last five years instead of seven. Systems may not exceed sanctioned load. Distribution companies must stop accepting new solar on a transformer once it reaches 80% of rated capacity. Dawn, 10 February 2026. https://www.dawn.com/news/1972203 . Also The News, 11 February 2026 (link in 1.3). **High.** Note: Tribune and Nukta said the Rs11 rate was not yet formally notified (search results only).
6. **11 February 2026, PM pushes back again.** PM Shehbaz Sharif told the Power Division to file an appeal with NEPRA to "protect the contract of existing solar consumers". The News, 11 February 2026 (link in 1.3). **Medium.** Search results (Business Recorder) say NEPRA published a draft amendment on 17 February 2026 to keep existing users on old terms until their contracts expire. I did not read it. **Low.** I found no confirmed final ruling after that.

## 1.5 "The grid owes households" and the "utility death spiral"

**Who owes whom (both sides argue it)**
- **Households' side:** Dawn wrote that distribution companies "have not made quarterly payments for net credits in the past", and that existing users' export credits now last one month instead of three. Dawn, 10 February 2026. https://www.dawn.com/news/1972203 . The January 2026 credit freeze above is a real case of exported units not being credited. **Medium.**
- **Utilities' side:** the Power Division said net metering pushed Rs159 billion of costs onto grid-only customers by December 2024, and could reach Rs4.24 trillion by 2034 without changes. Dawn, 14 March 2025 (link above). **Medium** (this is a government claim, not an audit).

**Utility death spiral (plain words: fewer people buy grid power, so each remaining buyer pays more for the same fixed costs, so more people leave)**
- Muhammad Mustafa Amjad of Renewables First described exactly this "utility death spiral" and said grid demand fell 4 to 5% in the past year. Yale Environment 360, interview by Beth Gardiner, 9 July 2025. https://e360.yale.edu/features/pakistan-solar-boom
- A climate ministry official told Reuters that rooftop solar would, for the first time, exceed grid demand in daytime hours in some industrial regions in 2026, with Lahore most likely, then Faisalabad and Sialkot, giving "negative grid-linked demand". Reuters said the boom had hurt "debt-laden utilities" through a long fall in grid demand. Reuters via Aaj News, 24 November 2025. https://english.aaj.tv/news/amp/330446304
- Renewables First: grid sales fell 9.4% in FY23, 2.6% in FY24 and 1% in FY25. Whitepaper, November 2025 (link in 1.1).
- **Confidence:** High that the death spiral is a real, widely discussed risk (Yale e360 plus Reuters plus Renewables First). Whether it is actually happening is debated: Renewables First's May 2026 report said grid sales rose 1.7% in FY25 (pv magazine link in 1.2), which conflicts with the whitepaper's "fell 1% in FY25". The two may use different definitions.

## 1.6 Why metering and measuring rooftop solar is hard in Pakistan

- **Most solar is not on any meter.** The Power Division estimates about 7,000 MW of metered on-grid solar, more than 13,000 MW off-grid, and says unmetered hybrid systems push the capacity outside metering beyond 19,000 MW. The new rules only touch metered users. Dawn, 10 February 2026. https://www.dawn.com/news/1972203
- **Imports far exceed registrations.** In 2024, 16 GW of panels came in but only 1.2 GW joined the net-metering scheme. Ember, 26 August 2025 (link in 1.1).
- **Nobody knows where 17 to 27 GW went.** Of 50,592 MW imported by September 2025, Renewables First estimates 780 MW at utility scale, 6,100 MW net-metered, 16,500 to 26,400 MW behind the meter or off-grid, and 17,300 to 27,200 MW "stockpiled, undeployed". The ranges are huge because there is no register. Whitepaper, November 2025 (link in 1.1).
- **Estimates of installed solar disagree.** For June 2025: Renewables First 38 GW, Transition Zero 27.5 GW, PRIED 33 GW. Ember says official statistics "have largely missed" the change and existing accounting relies on fuel-sales data. Ember, 25 June 2026 (link in 1.1).
- **Behind-the-meter solar escapes national statistics.** The Independent (via AOL), 14 April 2025, quoting Ember that growth is happening "outside formal energy planning frameworks". https://www.aol.com/pakistan-quietly-became-world-biggest-111210056.html
- **Even registered users get it wrong.** The January 2026 credit freeze happened because many users had installed more panels than their licence allowed (The News, link in 1.4).
- **Confidence:** High (Dawn, Ember, Renewables First and The Independent all say the true installed amount is unknown).

## 1.7 Three plain-words sentences for a 12 year old

1. Ember, a UK energy research group, says Pakistan bought about 17 gigawatts of solar panels from other countries in 2024, twice as much as the year before.
2. Ember also found that only 1.2 gigawatts of those panels were signed up to the government's meter scheme, so most of them are working where nobody is counting.
3. In February 2026, Dawn reported that the power regulator cut what new solar homes get paid for spare electricity from about Rs26 a unit to about Rs10 or 11.

# Topic 2: Making solar cheaper and more efficient, 2024 to 2026

## 2.1 Perovskite-silicon tandem cells (a thin perovskite layer stacked on a silicon cell, so the two layers catch different colours of light)

- **Current lab record: 35.5%.** LONGi, two-terminal crystalline silicon-perovskite tandem cell, presented 14 July 2026, certified by the European Solar Test Installation (ESTI, part of the European Commission's Joint Research Centre). Cell area not disclosed. No mass-production plan. pv magazine (Vincent Shaw), 15 July 2026. https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/07/15/longi-sets-new-world-record-with-35-5-efficient-perovskite-silicon-tandem-cell/ . Also pv Europe. https://www.pveurope.eu/solar-modules/longi-lifts-silicon-perovskite-tandem-cell-efficiency-355-percent . **High.**
- **Previous record: 34.85%.** LONGi, certified by the US National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL), announced 21 April 2025. LONGi gives the theoretical limit for this kind of tandem as 43%, versus 33.7% for a single-junction cell (the Shockley-Queisser limit). LONGi. https://www.longi.com/us/news/longi-world-record-crystalline-silicon-perovskite/ . Confirmed by pv magazine (above). **High.**
- **Large-area cell: 33%** on 260.9 cm2, NREL-certified, June 2025 (SNEC). LONGi. https://www.longi.com/en/news/new-world-record-in-snec-2025/ (seen in search; **Medium**).
- A 35.2% LONGi figure in the Solar Cell Efficiency Tables (version 68, May 2026) was seen only in a search summary. **Low.**
- **Oxford PV module record: 26.9%**, 60-cell residential module, about 1.6 m2, certified by Fraunhofer CalLab, 19 June 2024. Best silicon modules of a similar size are about 25%. Oxford PV. https://www.oxfordpv.com/news/oxford-pv-debuts-residential-solar-module-record-setting-269-efficiency . Also Electrek, 19 June 2024. https://electrek.co/2024/06/19/oxford-sets-a-new-world-record-for-solar-panel-efficiency/ . **High.**
- **Oxford PV first commercial shipment:** 72-cell tandem panels at 24.5% efficiency to a US utility customer, September 2024. eeNews Europe. https://www.eenewseurope.com/en/oxford-pv-ships-first-tandem-perovskite-solar-panels . **Medium.**

## 2.2 Bifacial panels (panels that also catch light reflected off the ground onto their back side)

- On single-axis trackers, bifacial modules add 4% to 15% more energy depending on module type and ground reflectivity, with a global average of 9%. Ayala Pelaez, Deline, Greenberg, Stein, Kostuk, "Model and Validation of Single-Axis Tracking with Bifacial PV", IEEE Journal of Photovoltaics 9(3), 2019 (NREL and Sandia authors). https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1514849
- Same paper: field systems showed 9.4% higher performance (Oregon) and 10 to 14.9% monthly rear gain (Albuquerque).
- Industry examples in trade press: Lightsource BP 8 to 12%, Array Technologies 9 to 10% (search results only, **Low**).
- **Confidence:** Medium-high for "about 5 to 10% typical, 9% average on trackers". The best numbers come from one NREL-led research group.

## 2.3 Dust: anti-soiling coatings and dry robotic cleaning

- **Size of the problem:** even with optimized cleaning, soiling (dust and dirt on panels) cuts world solar output by at least 3 to 4% and costs at least 3 to 5 billion euros a year. The authors projected 4 to 7% and 4 to 7 billion euros by 2023 (a projection, not a measurement). Ilse et al., "Techno-Economic Assessment of Soiling Losses and Mitigation Strategies for Solar Power Generation", Joule 3(10), 2019. https://elib.dlr.de/129424 . Reported by pv magazine (Emiliano Bellini), 24 October 2019, which adds that halving soiling might add only about 2 euros per m2 in net cost and that coatings only stretch the time between cleanings. https://www.pv-magazine.com/?p=22998 . **High.**
- **Anti-soiling coating, measured:** a thin-film coating field test (Madison Area Technical College) gave 1,097 kWh/kW per year versus 1,058 for uncoated, about a 3 to 3.5% gain, for about 2.6 US dollars per panel (about 1.4% of panel cost). pv magazine USA. https://pv-magazine-usa.com/?p=11935 (from search summary; **Medium-low**).
- **Robotic dry cleaning, reputable study:** Pillai, Figgis et al. (QEERI at Hamad Bin Khalifa University, with TotalEnergies), Applied Energy vol. 424, article 128460, December 2026. Daily robotic cleaning kept panels above 99% clean. Longer gaps between cleanings dropped below 95%. Robot vibration was much smaller than wind vibration. No cost figures. https://elmi.hbku.edu.qa/en/publications/real-world-performance-and-module-degradation-impacts-of-robotic-/ . **Medium.**
- **Robotic dry cleaning, cost (vendor numbers only):** Ecoppia's CEO told National Geographic that a 300 MW plant might spend over 5 million US dollars a year on cleaning and lose at least 3.6 million dollars of energy to dust, that his robots cost about 11 million dollars for that size with roughly 18-month payback, and save 420 million litres of water over 10 years. National Geographic (Christina Nunez), 6 November 2014. https://www.nationalgeographic.com/environment/article/how-to-clean-all-those-solar-panels-get-robots-to-do-it . **Low** (old, and the numbers are the seller's own).
- **Waterless future idea:** MIT (Panat and Varanasi) used static electricity to push dust off panels, recovering up to 95% of lost power in the lab. Science Advances, 11 March 2022, doi 10.1126/sciadv.abm0078. MIT Climate Portal. https://climate.mit.edu/posts/how-clean-solar-panels-without-water . **Medium** (lab scale only).

## 2.4 How much more energy trackers give, and what they cost

- **Single-axis (tilts east to west through the day):** 15 to 25% more energy than fixed tilt. IEEE Journal of Photovoltaics, 2019 (link in 2.2). NEXTracker's Dan Shugar told POWER magazine fixed to single-axis gains "about 20%". POWER (Bentham Paulos), 1 March 2019. https://www.powermag.com/cost-effective-solutions-for-boosting-solar-farm-production/ . **High.**
- **Dual-axis (also tilts up and down):** 30.4 to 34.6% more than fixed tilt at three sites in Turkey. Gönül, Yazar, Duman, Güler, Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews vol. 168, 112770, October 2022, doi 10.1016/j.rser.2022.112770. https://research.itu.edu.tr/en/publications/a-comparative-techno-economic-assessment-of-manually-adjustable-t/ . POWER says dual-axis adds about 10% over single-axis (link above). **High** for "roughly 30 to 35% over fixed, about 10 points over single-axis".
- **Honest range:** gains depend on latitude, cloudiness and spacing. Reviews quote anything from 20 to 50% for dual-axis. One Mumbai study found dual-axis gave no clear gain over single-axis (search results only, **Low**). In cloudy weather much light is diffuse (comes from the whole sky), and pointing at the sun helps less.
- **Why dual-axis is rare at utility scale:**
  - Berkeley Lab's Joachim Seel told POWER that the extra generation "often does not outweigh the incremental capital, and [operations and maintenance] costs". NEXTracker said dual-axis needs "a massive amount of steel and concrete" because the whole panel area sits on one pivot and catches wind. POWER, 1 March 2019 (link above). **Medium.**
  - In the Turkey study, dual-axis had the longest payback of all options, 16.7 to 24 years, versus 10.3 to 13.3 years for fixed. The best value was simply re-tilting a fixed panel by hand once a month: 3.6 to 5% more energy and payback about 8 months shorter. RSER 2022 (link above). **Medium.**
  - Berkeley Lab data (2020 edition): 88% of US utility-scale PV capacity added in 2019 used single-axis tracking. OSTI. https://osti.gov/biblio/1706670 (from search summary; **Medium-low**; the latest edition could not be opened).
  - Vendor cost quote (Gonvarri via TaiyangNews): dual-axis structures about 0.25 to 0.33 euros per watt, versus 0.07 to 0.09 euros per watt for a single-axis tracker. **Low.**
- **Household trackers:**
  - HomeServe (citing Solar Reviews and Forbes): roughly 500 US dollars per panel for single-axis and 1,000 dollars per panel for dual-axis hardware. https://www.homeserve.com/en-us/blog/home-improvement/solar-tracker-cost . **Low.**
  - Rooftops are hard: Solar Power World said a tall pole-mounted tracker "couldn't support the weight or wind loads" on a roof. Kelly Pickerel, 15 January 2018. https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2018/01/solar-trackers-find-new-home-roof/ . **Medium.**
  - Low-cost builds in research: a dual-axis tracker with its own power electronics for about 720 US dollars. Vargas do Nascimiento et al., Sustainable Energy Technologies and Assessments vol. 43, 2022 (published October 2022 per the listing). https://upcommons.upc.edu/handle/2117/372901 . **Medium.** A Ghana build reported 132 US dollars (2015, minor journal, **Low**). Off-the-shelf dual-axis controller kits with a wind sensor sell for about 110 to 180 US dollars, electronics only (retail listings, **Low**).

## 2.5 Is a 3-axis or 4-axis solar tracker a real thing that would help?

Mostly no, and the reasoning in the brief is correct. The sun's position in the sky is fully described by two angles: NREL's Solar Position Algorithm calculates exactly two, the solar zenith angle (how far from straight up) and the azimuth angle (compass direction) (Reda and Andreas, Solar Energy 76(5), 2004; NREL. https://midcdmz.nrel.gov/spa ). A two-axis tracker can therefore keep the panel "perpendicular to the solar beam" at all times, which is the best possible aim (Penn State EME 812, section 3.4, "Types of tracking systems". https://courses.ems.psu.edu/eme812/book/export/html/584 ). A third rotation could only spin the panel around the line it already points along, which does not change the angle of the sunlight, so it adds nothing to energy capture. This last step is geometry, not a quote from a source. Extra motions do exist for other jobs: tilting steeply to stow in high wind or to shed dust and water, or levelling a tracker on sloped ground. Those are protection or cleaning moves, not better aiming. I found no reputable source selling or testing a 3-axis or 4-axis flat-panel tracker that beats two-axis on energy. **Confidence: High** on two angles being enough (NREL plus Penn State). **Medium** on "no real 3-axis product exists" (absence of evidence, not proof).

## 2.6 Four plain-words sentences for the "what comes next" chapter

1. A 2022 study in the journal Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews found a two-axis tracker made 30 to 35% more power than a fixed panel, but it cost so much that it took up to 24 years to pay back, so the next step is a two-axis tracker cheap enough for a home roof.
2. A 2019 study in the journal Joule found that dust steals at least 3 to 4% of the world's solar power even with cleaning, so panels that wipe, rinse and drain themselves can win that power back without paying workers to wash them.
3. Dawn reported in February 2026 that Pakistan's power ministry has only about 7,000 megawatts of solar on its meters, while more than 19,000 megawatts sit outside any meter, so cheap meters that count every panel would help the grid plan.
4. In July 2026, pv magazine reported that LONGi made a lab solar cell that turns 35.5% of sunlight into electricity by stacking a new material called perovskite on top of silicon, while normal panels today turn about a quarter.

## Gaps and conflicts to know before using these numbers

- **No reputable full-year 2025 import total.** Only The Friday Times (16.9 GW, unsourced) gave one.
- **2024 imports:** 16, 16.6 and 17 GW all trace back to Ember's reading of Chinese customs data. Rounding and data updates explain the spread.
- **Net-metering capacity:** 2,813 MW (Economic Survey, March 2025, not read) versus 4.9 GW (Renewables First, March 2025) is unexplained.
- **Grid sales FY25:** Renewables First whitepaper says down 1%; Renewables First May 2026 review says up 1.7%.
- **Buyback rate:** Dawn gives Rs10 to 11 for new users under the 2026 rules; other outlets said the exact rate was not yet formally notified.
- **Final status of the February 2026 draft amendment protecting existing users:** not confirmed.
- **Reuters and Bloomberg pages could not be opened directly.** Reuters stories were read through syndicated copies (Arab News, Aaj News). The Bloomberg / BNEF piece found (13 GW in the first half) is from August 2024, not 2025.
- **Robotic cleaning cost:** no independent cost figure found. Only vendor numbers.
