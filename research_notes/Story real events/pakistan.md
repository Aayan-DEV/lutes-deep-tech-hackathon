# Real events in Pakistan: fixed solar panels hurt by dust and weather

All sources accessed on 11 October 2026.

Confidence key:
- **High:** two independent reputable sources agree.
- **Medium:** one reputable source.
- **Low:** blog, forum, or vendor press release only.

Note on panel type: most news reports do not say how the panels were mounted. Where the source does not say, this file says "not stated" and does not guess.

---

## Warning first: the "Quaid-e-Azam Solar Park only makes 18 MW" story is shaky

Many people repeat that the 100 MW park in Bahawalpur "only produced 18 MW". After reading the original sources, this claim does not hold up as proof of dust damage.

- **Where it came from:**
  - Dawn blog by Musa Khan Durrani, 2 October 2015: the park "generates on average only 18MW every hour for the whole day". https://www.dawn.com/news/1209933
  - Dawn, Nudrrat Khawaja, 6 December 2015: journalist Anwer Hussain Sumra said the park produces "only 18 megawatt of electricity instead of the 100 megawatt" promised. Energy expert Arshad Abbasi blamed heat above 45 degrees Celsius. Dust is not mentioned. https://www.dawn.com/news/1224548
  - Profit (Pakistan Today), K K Shahid opinion, 9 October 2017: calls the 18 MW figure "speculated" and blames heat. https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2017/10/09/pakistan-looking-into-the-sun
- **Why it is misleading:**
  - "18 MW on average over the whole day" counts the night. Averaged over 24 hours, a 100 MW solar plant in Pakistan is expected to give roughly 17 to 19 MW. This is normal (the "capacity factor", meaning average output divided by peak rating, is about 17 to 19% for solar).
  - Check: the park's own yearly target of 153 GWh divided by 8,760 hours in a year is about 17.5 MW average. The 150 GWh a year reported by Reuters in 2016 is about 17.1 MW average.
  - Dawn, 5 November 2015 (Faisal Ali Ghumman): the CEO Najam Ahmed Shah said the plant gave "169 gigawatt hour against the annual target of 153GW hour", so it was above target in its first months. https://www.dawn.com/news/1217587
  - The operator QA Solar also calls the 18 MW claim a misunderstanding of MW (power) versus MWh (energy), and says it produces 400 to 500 MWh a day depending on weather. https://www.qasolar.com/annual-reports/misunderstood-facts/
- **What I could not confirm:**
  - No Senate committee statement or NEPRA report about the park underperforming because of dust was found.
  - Wikipedia says dust cut panel efficiency "by 40%" and cites Dawn of 8 September 2015 and a Dawn e-paper page of 30 September 2015. I read the 8 September article and the 40% figure is not in it. The e-paper page could not be read. **Treat the 40% figure as unconfirmed.**
- **Bottom line:** Do not use "18 MW instead of 100 MW" in the story. Dust at the park is real (see event 1), but this number is not evidence of it.

## 1. Dust and sand were the biggest problem at Pakistan's flagship solar park, so 100 workers washed panels every week

- **Date:** Reuters report published 20 September 2016 (Dawn). Earlier cleaning details from Dawn, 8 September 2015. Field dust study run in summer 2016 or earlier, published June 2017.
- **Place:** Quaid-e-Azam Solar Park, Cholistan desert, about 20 km from Bahawalpur, Punjab.
- **What happened:**
  - Operations manager Muhammad Hassan Askari told Reuters that "dust and sand are the greatest technical challenges" at the plant.
  - A permanent team of about 100 labourers washed the panels every week. There is no battery storage, and the original 100 MW plant made about 150 GWh a year.
  - In September 2015, Dawn reported that 30 people took 10 to 15 days to clean the 400,000 panels, using an estimated 1 litre of water per panel. Askari said unusual rains that year had "cleaned automatically" the panels.
  - Renewable Energy World (June 2016) wrote that the plant "suffers excess soiling due to sand storms and unsettled dust in the ground".
  - A field study at the park (Abbas, Harijan, Hameed, Bhayo, Mehran University, 2017) compared a dusty module with a clean one over three summer months. The dusty polycrystalline module gave on average 19% less power. Dust density on the glass was 0.786 mg/cm2 in June, 0.681 in July and 0.601 in August.
- **Panel type:** Utility scale, ground mounted on screw piles. Crystalline silicon modules (JA Solar, 15.6% efficiency per Renewable Energy World). No source I read states "fixed tilt" in words, and no tracker is mentioned anywhere, so fixed racking is very likely but **not confirmed by a source**.
- **Cause:** Desert dust and sand storms settling on the glass. Heat is a second factor: the CEO said in 2015 that panels "produced less power in high temperatures".
- **Sources:**
  1. Dawn (Reuters), "Solar scale-up in Pakistan hits roadblock after payments cut", 20 September 2016. https://www.dawn.com/news/amp/1284780 (accessed 11 Oct 2026). Same Reuters text also on Gulf News: https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/pakistan/solar-scale-up-in-pakistan-hits-roadblock-after-payments-slashed-1.1898882
  2. Dawn (Zofeen T. Ebrahim, via thethirdpole.net), "World's largest solar park to light up Pakistan's future", 8 September 2015. https://www.dawn.com/news/1205484 (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  3. Abbas et al., "Effect of Dust on the Performance of Photovoltaic System (A Case Study of Quaid-E-Azam Solar Park Bahawalpur, Pakistan)", Noble International Journal of Scientific Research, vol. 1(6), pp. 73 to 79, June 2017. https://ideas.repec.org/a/nap/nijsrr/2017p73-79.html (abstract read, accessed 11 Oct 2026)
- **Confidence:** High that dust is the main problem and heavy cleaning is needed (Reuters plus Dawn 2015 plus Renewable Energy World). Medium on the 19% figure (one small study, two modules, minor journal).
- **What a smart tracker would change:** Tilting steep at night or when dust is detected could shed loose sand and cut how often the 100 workers and the water are needed, but it would not remove stuck-on dust, and facing the sun all day adds heat, so it would not fix the heat loss.

## 2. In Bahawalpur, an uncleaned rooftop panel lost a quarter of its power in six weeks

- **Date:** Experiment ran 1 June to 15 July 2022. Paper published 30 November 2023. pv magazine report 30 January 2024.
- **Place:** House rooftop in Bahawalpur, run side by side with a rooftop in Islamabad.
- **What happened:**
  - Two identical 40 W polycrystalline panels at each site. One was wiped regularly, one was left dirty.
  - After 42 days in Bahawalpur, dust on the dirty panel reached 10.254 g/m2 (about 0.244 g/m2 per day), and its power was 25.42% lower than the clean panel.
  - In Islamabad, dust reached 6.388 g/m2 and power fell 15.08%. Bahawalpur lost about 1.5 times more.
  - The dirty panels also ran hotter than the clean ones (the paper gives no number), which adds to the loss.
- **Panel type:** Rooftop, fixed tilt, south facing. The paper says 34 degrees in one place and 34.5 degrees in another.
- **Cause:** Desert dust with almost no rain to wash it off, plus frequent wind and dust storms.
- **Sources:**
  1. Rashid et al., "Effect of dust accumulation on the performance of photovoltaic modules for different climate regions", Heliyon 9(12), e23069, 30 November 2023, DOI 10.1016/j.heliyon.2023.e23069. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10731227/ (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  2. pv magazine (Lior Kahana), "New research analyzes effect of dust on PV module performance", 30 January 2024. https://www.pv-magazine.com/2024/01/30/new-research-analyzes-effect-of-dust-on-pv-module-performance/ (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
- **Confidence:** Medium to high. Peer reviewed paper plus independent trade press coverage, but both rest on one small experiment.
- **What a smart tracker would change:** A steep tilt would shed much of this loose desert dust and claw back a large part of the 25% loss, but a tracker cannot shed dust that sticks, so occasional wiping would still be needed.

## 3. Lahore has one of the highest dust loss rates measured in South Asia, and steep panels lost about half as much

- **Date:** 100 day experiment reported in 2019. 120 day experiment from October to January reported in 2020.
- **Place:** Open rooftop in Lahore (LUMS research group, Nauman Zafar Butt).
- **What happened:**
  - Panels tilted at 30 degrees lost about **0.8% of their output per day** to dirt between October and January. The authors call this one of the highest soiling rates reported for cities in South Asia and the Gulf.
  - Over 100 days without cleaning, a flat (horizontal) panel lost **26.2%** of its energy, while a vertical panel lost only **13.5%**.
  - The best cleaning schedule worked out to about once a week at 30 degrees tilt, but only once every three weeks for a vertical panel.
  - Lab analysis of the dust found a lot of carbon (from air pollution) and quartz (sand).
  - Note: 0.8% per day should not be multiplied out over months. Losses level off as wind and rain remove some dirt, which is why 100 days gave 26%, not 80%.
- **Panel type:** Rooftop test panels at fixed angles (horizontal, 30 degrees, vertical). Monofacial and bifacial.
- **Cause:** Dust plus sticky carbon from urban air pollution and smog season.
- **Sources:**
  1. Ullah, Amin, Haider, Saleem, Butt, "Investigation of soiling effects, dust chemistry and optimum cleaning schedule for PV modules in Lahore, Pakistan", Renewable Energy vol. 150, pp. 456 to 468, 2020, DOI 10.1016/j.renene.2019.12.090. https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/renene/v150y2020icp456-468.html (abstract read, accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  2. Ullah, Imran, Maqsood, Butt, "Investigation of optimal tilt angles and effects of soiling on PV energy production in Pakistan", Renewable Energy vol. 139, pp. 830 to 843, 2019, DOI 10.1016/j.renene.2019.02.114. https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/renene/v139y2019icp830-843.html (abstract read, accessed 11 Oct 2026)
- **Confidence:** High for the numbers (two peer reviewed papers in a top journal), though both come from the same research group. This is a measured study, not a news event.
- **What a smart tracker would change:** This is the strongest evidence for the tracker idea, because steep panels lost about half as much as flat ones; but the sticky carbon part of Lahore dust would still need washing, and a tracker that faces the sun at a low angle in winter spends more time near flat, where dust settles fastest.

## 4. A hailstorm in Islamabad shattered solar carports and rooftop panels

- **Date:** Wednesday 16 April 2025.
- **Place:** Islamabad (worst in sectors E-11, G-11, F-10) and parts of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
- **What happened:**
  - Dawn: the storm lasted about 35 minutes in Islamabad and caused "widespread damage to vehicles, windscreens, and solar panels". No hail size or panel count given.
  - Profit (Pakistan Today): hail shattered panels and bent steel frames on solar carports used as EV charging stations. Several were left unusable. Early estimates put losses at "tens of millions of rupees" for public and private operators. Profit says the storm was on "Monday", which conflicts with Dawn and other outlets (Wednesday).
  - No official total of solar losses was published in the sources I read.
- **Panel type:** Solar carports (fixed canopies) and rooftop panels. Exact tilt not stated.
- **Cause:** Large hail.
- **Sources:**
  1. Dawn, "Vehicles damaged as hailstorms batter Islamabad, parts of KP", 16 April 2025. https://www.dawn.com/news/1904643/vehicles-damaged-as-hailstorms-batter-islamabad-parts-of-kp (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  2. Profit (Pakistan Today), "Islamabad reels from devastating hailstorm; green infrastructure, vehicles suffer heavy losses", 16 April 2025. https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/04/16/islamabad-reels-from-devastating-hailstorm-green-infrastructure-vehicles-suffer-heavy-losses/ (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
- **Confidence:** High that solar panels were damaged (Dawn plus Profit). Medium on the "tens of millions of rupees" figure (Profit only, early estimate).
- **What a smart tracker would change:** Dust detection does nothing here; a tracker could only help if it got a hail warning in time and turned the glass steep so stones glance off, and it does not make the glass any stronger.

## 5. A windstorm across Punjab ripped rooftop solar panels loose

- **Date:** Saturday 24 May 2025.
- **Place:** Across Punjab, including Lahore, Multan, Hafizabad, Gujranwala, Attock, Sialkot and Jhelum.
- **What happened:**
  - Dawn: at least 19 people were killed and over 90 injured across the country. "About two dozen incidents of the fall of solar panels and billboards" were reported, plus damage to solar panels in Lahore.
  - Geo News: strong winds "blew away solar panels at several sites" in Multan, Hafizabad, Gujranwala, Attock and Sialkot.
  - Daily Pakistan: the Director General of PDMA Punjab (not named) said 80% of the storm damage in the province was due to uprooted rooftop solar panels, and asked people to mount panels securely. Only this outlet reports the 80% figure.
- **Panel type:** Rooftop, mostly home installations. Tilt not stated.
- **Cause:** High wind during a dust storm and rain (structural failure, not soiling).
- **Sources:**
  1. Dawn, "Hailstorm, rains ravage Punjab, KP, Islamabad", 25 May 2025. https://www.dawn.com/news/amp/1913151 (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  2. Geo News, "Punjab, parts of country brace for more rain after storms kill 14, injure 51", 25 May 2025. https://www.geo.tv/latest/606104-punjab-parts-of-country-brace-for-more-rain-after-storms-kill-14-injure-51 (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  3. Daily Pakistan, "Solar panels blamed for 80% damage in recent Lahore dust storm", 26 May 2025. https://en.dailypakistan.com.pk/26-May-2025/solar-panels-blamed-for-80-damage-in-recent-lahore-dust-storm (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
- **Confidence:** High that rooftop panels were blown loose (Dawn plus Geo). Medium on the "80% of damage" claim (one outlet).
- **What a smart tracker would change:** Dust detection does not help against wind; a tracker only helps if it moves flat into a "wind stow" position in time, and a tall single post tracker catches more wind than a low fixed rack, so without stow it could do worse.

## Best pick for the story

**Event 1: dust and sand at the Quaid-e-Azam Solar Park in Bahawalpur.** It is in Cholistan, it is on regular fixed panels, and it was named by the plant's own manager to Reuters.

Story version (3 sentences for a 12 year old):

> Pakistan's biggest solar park sits in the Cholistan desert near Bahawalpur, and its own manager said dust and sand were its biggest problem, as Reuters reported in Dawn in 2016. The panels never move, so dust just sits on them, and about 100 workers had to wash them every single week. A study at the same park found that a dusty panel made about 19% less power than a clean one.

Why this pick:
- Real place, named person, two outlets (Reuters via Dawn and Gulf News, plus Dawn 2015 and Renewable Energy World).
- Simple: dust on still panels, people wash them by hand.
- Directly matches what the tracker does: tilt steep to shed loose dust, so fewer washes.

Backup if a stronger number is needed: Event 2 (Bahawalpur rooftop, 25.42% loss in six weeks, Heliyon 2023). For the "why steep tilt works" proof: Event 3 (Lahore, flat panel lost 26.2% but vertical lost 13.5% over 100 days).

Do not use: "the park only makes 18 MW instead of 100 MW". See the warning at the top.
