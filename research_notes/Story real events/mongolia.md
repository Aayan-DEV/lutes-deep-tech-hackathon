# Mongolia: real weather events that hit solar power

Researched 11 October 2026. All sources were accessed on 11 October 2026.

## Read this first: what I could and could not find

- I found **no news report, government report, or ADB report** that says a named Mongolian solar plant (Sainshand, Zamyn-Uud, Darkhan, Sumber, Monnaran, Bulgan) lost output or was damaged by snow, dust, hail, or wind. I searched in English, Mongolian, and Chinese. If such a report exists, it is not indexed where I could reach it.
- I found **no report** that herder home solar panels (the "100,000 Solar Ger" programme) failed during a dzud or a sandstorm. It is likely, but no source says it, so it is **not confirmed**.
- Reuters.com blocks my search tool, so I could not read or quote any Reuters story. The sources below are Montsame (Mongolia's state news agency), NPR, Xinhua, the IFRC, WHO, Al Jazeera, and peer-reviewed journals.
- What *is* solid: the March 2021 Gobi sandstorm (well reported), a peer-reviewed study that measured how much that same storm cut solar output across the Gobi and northern China, a 10-year panel test at Sainshand, and a dust-and-snow study on solar collectors in Ulaanbaatar.

## Event 1: The March 2021 Gobi sandstorm killed 10 people and knocked down gers and power lines

- **Date:** 14 to 16 March 2021 (storm hit on 14 March; search for missing people ended 16 March).
- **Place:** 51 soums in 12 aimags, including Dundgobi (worst hit), Dornogobi, Umnugobi, Gobisumber, Sukhbaatar, Dornod and others.
- **What happened (numbers):**
  - Winds of 34 to 40 m/s for 10 to 15 hours in Dundgobi (Montsame via AKIpress).
  - 10 dead (9 adults, 1 child). 590 people reported missing; 580 found.
  - Roofs ripped off 69 structures, windows broken on 30 vehicles, 587 fences and **92 gers destroyed**.
  - Xinhua (citing Mongolia's emergency agency): "many yurts and power lines were taken down", especially in Dundgobi.
  - NPR (May 2021): about 1.6 million livestock killed by that spring's sandstorms in southern Mongolia; 3,600 animals buried by sand in Airag county.
- **Type of panel:** Not reported. Many herder gers carry small fixed home panels on a pole, but **no source says how many panels were lost**. Do not claim a number.
- **Cause:** A Mongolian cyclone with a cold front over a very dry Gobi (almost no snow the winter before, per NPR's interviewees).
- **Sources:**
  1. AKIpress, relaying Montsame, "580 previously missing in Mongolia sandstorm safe and sound", 17 March 2021: https://akipress.com/news:655695:580_previously_missing_in_Mongolia_sandstorm_safe_and_sound (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  2. NPR (Khaliun Bayartsogt and Emily Feng), "Buried Alive In Mongolia's Worst Sandstorms In A Decade", 30 May 2021: https://www.npr.org/sections/goatsandsoda/2021/05/30/1000530563/buried-alive-in-mongolias-worst-sandstorms-in-a-decade (read via the GBH mirror https://www.wgbh.org/news/international-news/2021-05-30/buried-alive-in-mongolias-worst-sandstorms-in-a-decade, accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  3. China Daily, relaying Xinhua, "At least 6 killed in dust, snow storms in Mongolia", 15 March 2021: https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202103/15/WS604efc14a31024ad0baaf3e1_2.html (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
- **Confidence:** **High** for the storm, deaths and damage (Montsame, NPR, Xinhua, IFRC all agree). **Not confirmed** for any solar panel damage in Mongolia.
- **What a smart tracker would and would not change:** A tracker that stows flat or edge-on in high wind could protect its own panel, but in 34 to 40 m/s winds that flattened gers it would not save a herder's home, and no source tells us how many fixed panels were actually lost.

## Event 2: The same March 2021 dust cloud cut solar output by up to 70% across the Gobi and northern China

- **Date:** 15 to 20 March 2021 (the dust event studied).
- **Place:** The study's "Gobi Desert", northwest China and North China Plain regions. These hold over 50% of China's solar capacity. **The study is about plants in China, not plants inside Mongolia.** The dust started in the Gobi.
- **What happened (numbers):**
  - Solar output potential fell by **up to 70%** in the worst-hit areas.
  - Across 34 major solar plants in the affected regions, the capacity factor (how much of its maximum a plant actually makes) dropped **21%** on average.
  - About **16.5 GWh** of lost electricity, worth about **1.36 million US dollars**.
  - **84 to 89%** of the loss came from dust in the air blocking sunlight ("dimming"). Only **about 16%** came from dust sitting on the panels ("soiling").
- **Type of panel:** Utility scale. The model tested three setups: flat fixed, fixed at the best tilt, and one-axis trackers. All three lost a similar share (up to 70%). Fixed-tilt panels showed the biggest percentage drop.
- **Cause:** Dust storm (dimming plus soiling).
- **Source:**
  1. Yin, Yao, Luo, Gao, Lu and Yi, "Substantial reduction of solar photovoltaic potential in China by an extreme dust event", *Communications Earth & Environment* (Nature Portfolio) 7, 44, published 14 January 2026. DOI 10.1038/s43247-025-03123-1: https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-025-03123-1 and https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/substantial-reduction-of-solar-photovoltaic-potential-in-china-by (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
- **Confidence:** **Medium.** One peer-reviewed study. It is a model checked against satellite data, not plant meter readings. The authors themselves say the loss "is difficult to be prevented by cleaning the PV panels" because dimming dominates.
- **What a smart tracker would and would not change:** Tilting steep could shake off some of the dust that lands on the glass (the roughly 16% soiling part), but no tracker can clear dust that is still in the air, and the study's own one-axis trackers lost about the same share as fixed panels.

## Event 3: The 2023 to 2024 dzud buried more than 1,000 herder gers under the heaviest snow in 49 years

- **Date:** Winter 2023 to 2024 (heavy snow from November; peak around February and March 2024).
- **Place:** Up to 90% of Mongolia's territory.
- **What happened (numbers):**
  - Heaviest snowfall in 49 years; temperatures to minus 30°C or lower (WHO).
  - More than 5.9 million livestock dead by 28 March 2024 (WHO). Al Jazeera reported 4.7 million on 19 March 2024.
  - Heavy snow buried **more than 1,000 herder households' gers** (Al Jazeera).
- **Type of panel:** Herder home systems (small fixed panels on a pole or ger roof). The World Bank says its portable solar programme brought power to about 70% of nomadic herders.
- **What is NOT confirmed:** No source I found says these panels were buried or stopped working. It is a fair guess that a buried ger meant a buried panel, but it is a guess.
- **Cause:** Snow and extreme cold (dzud).
- **Sources:**
  1. WHO Mongolia, "Dzud emergency in Mongolia moves to risk of floods and environmental contamination", 4 April 2024: https://www.who.int/mongolia/news/detail/04-04-2024-dzud-emergency-in-mongolia-moves-to-risk-of-floods-and-environmental-contamination (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  2. Al Jazeera, "Harsh Mongolian winter leaves 4.7m animals dead; Red Cross issues appeal", 19 March 2024: https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/3/19/harsh-mongolian-winter-leaves-4-7m-animals-dead-red-cross-issues-appeal (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  3. World Bank, "Mongolia: Portable Solar Power for Nomadic Herders", 8 April 2013: https://www.worldbank.org/en/results/2013/04/08/portable-solar-power-for-nomadic-herders (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
- **Confidence:** **High** for the dzud itself. **Low / unconfirmed** for any effect on solar panels.
- **What a smart tracker would and would not change:** Tilting near vertical would shed falling snow and catch the low winter sun better than a flat-ish fixed panel, but it would not help if the whole ger and pole are buried in drifts, and a motorised tracker is far more than a herder's 60 W home kit costs.

## Event 4: A 10-year panel test in Sainshand showed fixed panels in the Gobi lose about 1% of power a year

- **Date:** 2002 to 2012 (test station installed October 2002).
- **Place:** Sainshand, Dornogobi (Gobi Desert).
- **What happened (numbers):**
  - Two crystalline silicon modules, fixed mount.
  - Performance ratio (how much of the sunlight's potential the panel actually turns into power) 0.85 and 0.80.
  - Yearly output 1,880 and 1,789 kWh per kW; reference yield 2,223 hours.
  - Power fell **1.28% and 0.86% per year** (an earlier 6-year write-up said over 1.5% per year). The drop came from falling short-circuit current.
  - Cold gave a **bonus**: panels made more power in the very cold air.
  - **Not confirmed:** how much of the loss came from dust or snow. The abstracts I could read do not split it out.
- **Type of panel:** Fixed test modules (research station).
- **Cause:** Long-term degradation in desert conditions. Dust and snow likely play a part, but the papers I could read do not give a number.
- **Sources:**
  1. Bayandelger, Ueda, Batbayar, Adiyabat, Enebish and Otani, "Long-term performance and degradation rate analyses of photovoltaic modules exposed in the Gobi Desert, Mongolia", *Japanese Journal of Applied Physics* 57, 08RG07, August 2018. DOI 10.7567/JJAP.57.08RG07: https://tus.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/long-term-performance-and-degradation-rate-analyses-of-photovolta/ (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  2. Adiyabat, Ganbaatar, Otani and Enkhmaa, "Long Term Performance Analysis of Photovoltaic Modules in the Sainshand of Dornogobi Province", National University of Mongolia Physics journal. DOI 10.22353/physics.v17i362.715: https://journal.num.edu.mn/physics/article/view/715 (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
- **Confidence:** **Medium.** Peer-reviewed and measured, but both papers come from the same research team.
- **What a smart tracker would and would not change:** A tracker would collect more sunlight each day, but it would not slow the slow ageing of the cells, which is what these numbers mostly measure.

## Event 5: In Ulaanbaatar, winter dust dirtied solar collectors and snow cleaned them

- **Date:** October 2015 to May 2016 (exposure test).
- **Place:** Ulaanbaatar.
- **What happened (numbers):**
  - Dust built up on the glass tubes of a solar water-heating collector through the cold season.
  - **Snow washed dust off the glass even below 0°C**, and snow cleaned better than rain.
  - A follow-up simulation found that cleaning **once**, between mid January and early February, gives **about 12% more energy** over the heating season than never cleaning.
  - **Not confirmed:** the exact transmittance loss. A review table cites up to 50% after 20 weeks at a 60° tilt, but I could not read the original paper to check that number.
- **Type of panel:** Solar thermal collector (evacuated glass tubes), **not PV**. Fixed tilt.
- **Cause:** Dust and city air pollution (soiling).
- **Sources:**
  1. Erdenedavaa, Akisawa, Adiyabat and Otgonjanchiv, "Observation and modeling of dust deposition on glass tube of evacuated solar thermal collectors in Mongolia", *Renewable Energy* 130, 613 to 621, 2019. DOI 10.1016/j.renene.2018.06.077: https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/renene/v130y2019icp613-621.html (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
  2. Same group, "Model Analysis of Solar Thermal System with the Effect of Dust Deposition on the Collectors", *Energies* 11(7), 1795, July 2018: https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jeners/v11y2018i7p1795-d156940.html (accessed 11 Oct 2026)
- **Confidence:** **Medium.** Peer-reviewed and measured, one research group, and about solar heat collectors rather than solar panels.
- **What a smart tracker would and would not change:** It supports the idea that snow can be a free cleaner, so a tracker that tilts steep to let snow slide off may also take dust with it, but this was measured on glass tubes, not PV panels, so it is a hint, not proof.

## Best pick for the story

**Event 1 plus Event 2: the March 2021 Gobi sandstorm.** It is the only event that is real, Mongolian, well sourced, simple, and has a peer-reviewed number for solar output.

> In March 2021, the worst sandstorm in ten years blew out of the Gobi Desert, killed 10 people in Mongolia and knocked down 92 gers and many power lines, as Mongolia's news agency Montsame and NPR reported. The same dust cloud rolled into China, and scientists writing in the journal Communications Earth & Environment in January 2026 found it cut what solar panels could make by up to 70% in the worst-hit areas. Most of that loss came from dust in the air hiding the sun, but some came from dust settling on the panels themselves.

**Honesty notes for the pitch:**
- Do not say Mongolian solar plants or herder panels were damaged in this storm. No source says so.
- Do not say a tracker would have prevented the 70% loss. The study says 84 to 89% of it was dust in the air, which nothing on the ground can fix. A tracker with dust detection can only help with the dust that lands on the panel (about 16% of the loss).
- If you want to quote Reuters, find and read the Reuters article yourself first; I could not access it.
